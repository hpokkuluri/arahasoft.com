1Walk me through how you would design the enterprise structure for a multi-country Fusion Financials implementation.Enterprise StructuresIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a senior design question. They want a method and building blocks.

Answer:

"I start from how the business is organized legally, managerially and for tax, not from the org chart. The building blocks are legal entities, one per registered company; primary ledgers, one per unique combination of the four Cs, where countries that share all four can share a ledger with a balancing value per entity; business units where transactions happen, with service-provider relationships if a shared services center processes for many BUs; reference data sets to share setups like payment terms across BUs; and secondary or reporting currency ledgers for other GAAPs or group currency. My approach is workshops with finance, tax and legal to confirm entities, currencies and statutory needs by country, then design the chart for group consolidation first, with local needs handled by secondary ledgers or mapping. And I prototype in a sandbox with real transactions and a close before locking it in, because this is the hardest thing to change later."

Tip: Add a real example: how many ledgers, and why you chose secondary ledgers or reporting currencies.

Likely follow-up

What's the most expensive enterprise structure mistake to fix later?

Too few balancing segment values or ledgers mapped wrongly to legal entities, because changing them after go-live means re-implementing ledgers.

2How do you design a chart of accounts for a global company, and what mistakes have you seen?Chart of AccountsIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: COA design shapes reporting for years.

Answer:

"A good chart answers reporting questions with segments, not with thousands of natural accounts. Typical segments are company for balancing, cost center, natural account, intercompany, product or line of business, and a future segment, each with a clear owner. Segments should be independent, one meaning each, with no codes that pack two things together. I leave room in number ranges, use hierarchies for roll-ups instead of summary values, and set up cross-validation rules early. The mistakes I see are copying the legacy chart one-for-one with all its workarounds, using cost centers to track projects or customers, having too many segments, and leaving out the intercompany segment, which makes eliminations painful."

Tip: Give a before-and-after, like cutting 4,000 natural accounts to 900 by moving detail into segments and hierarchies.

Likely follow-up

How many segments would you recommend?

Usually six to eight: company, cost center, account, sub-account or product, intercompany and a couple of future segments, depending on reporting needs.

3Explain Subledger Accounting (SLA) and give an example of a rule you customized.Subledger AccountingIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: SLA is a favorite senior question, and the example proves you've done it.

Answer:

"SLA turns subledger events, like an AP invoice being validated or a receipt being applied, into accounting entries before they go to GL. It separates what happened from how we account for it, so rules can change without touching transactions. The pieces are event classes and types, journal line rules, account derivation rules, description rules and supporting references, all grouped in an accounting method assigned to the ledger. My example: the business wanted AP liability on a different natural account for intercompany suppliers. I copied the standard method, built a mapping set from supplier type to natural account, created an ADR that overrides only the natural account segment, attached it to the liability line, activated it, and tested invoice, payment and cancellation."

Tip: Always say you copied the seeded method and tested the full life cycle: create, adjust, cancel, pay.

Likely follow-up

How do you make sure SLA changes are safe?

Copy the seeded method, change only what's needed, test with draft accounting across transaction types, and migrate through change control.

4How do you run and improve the month-end close in Oracle Financials?Period CloseIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: every finance leader wants a faster, cleaner close.

Answer:

"I run the close as a sequenced checklist with owners, cut-off times and reconciliations. Procurement and AP first: receipts, accruals, invoices accounted, AP closed. Then AR, then Assets with depreciation, then Cash Management reconciliations. GL last: post journals, revalue and translate, intercompany and allocations, reconcile subledgers to GL, close and consolidate. To improve it, I make status visible with the Close Monitor and task lists, move work earlier with daily accounting and continuous reconciliation, and automate accruals. On one program we went from 8 working days to 5 by automating accruals and bank reconciliation and moving intercompany settlement to weekly."

Tip: Lead with the number, days to close before and after, then explain what you changed.

Likely follow-up

What's the single biggest lever to speed up the close?

Removing manual reconciliations by automating subledger to GL ties and bank reconciliation.

5A supplier is paid twice. How would you investigate and prevent it from happening again?Accounts PayableIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: duplicate payments are a classic control failure. They want root cause and prevention, not just recovery.

Answer:

"I'd start by finding both invoices and comparing them: invoice number, amount, date and supplier site. Duplicates usually differ by one character, a leading zero or a different site. Then I check how each one came in, because the most common pattern is one entered by hand and one from imaging or an integration. I'd also check whether duplicate checking was bypassed. To recover, I contact the supplier for a refund or a credit memo and net it on the next payment, making sure the 1099 amounts stay right. To prevent it, I tighten duplicate checks across supplier, number, amount and date, normalize invoice numbers on import, restrict who can override, and run a duplicate-payment report before each payment run."

  • Investigate: compare both invoices, check the source, check overrides.
  • Recover: refund or credit memo, fix 1099.
  • Prevent: stronger duplicate checks, normalized numbers, override control, pre-run report.

Tip: Interviewers listen for root cause and the control you'd add, not just "we got the money back."

Likely follow-up

What control would you add first?

Tighter duplicate invoice checking and a pre-payment duplicate report reviewed before each payment run.

6How do you design the cash application process to reduce unapplied cash?Accounts ReceivableIn-depth

Why they ask: a design question with a clear business outcome. They want a method and a result.

Answer:

"The biggest lever is remittance data, so I start there: lockbox files from the bank, bank statements in BAI2 or CAMT.053, and remittance emails, which can be captured with Intelligent Document Recognition. Then I set up AutoMatch and lockbox matching to look for invoice number, PO number, amount and customer reference, with tolerances for small differences. Application exception rules decide what happens to under- and over-payments within tolerance, like write off below $10 or put on account. Anything left goes to a work queue with a named owner and an ageing target. Where the business agrees, AutoCash rules apply receipts with no remittance to the oldest invoice first. I measure auto-application rate, unapplied and on-account balances, and days to apply. On one rollout we moved auto-applied receipts from about 45% to 80% by adding customer-reference matching and cleaning customer data."

Tip: Close with the bank reconciliation in Cash Management. It shows the process ends with the cash proven, not just applied.

Likely follow-up

What's a realistic auto-application target?

Often 80 to 90% for customers who send remittance; the rest needs better remittance data or AI matching.

7Explain how asset books, categories and depreciation work in Oracle Assets, including tax books.Fixed AssetsIn-depth

Why they ask: a broad question to see how you explain the whole module.

Answer:

"The corporate book is the main book, linked to a ledger, and holds depreciation for financial reporting. Tax books hold copies of the same assets with different methods and lives, like MACRS in the US, kept in step with Mass Copy each period. Categories default the accounts, method, life and prorate convention for each book. Depreciation runs per book per period, and Create Accounting sends it to GL through SLA. Assets come in by manual entry, from AP through Mass Additions, or from Projects as CIP that's later capitalized. The things I watch are category design that works for both books and tax, mass additions reviewed weekly, and retirements, transfers and impairments flowing correctly to GL."

Tip: If you've done IFRS and local GAAP, mention a second corporate book on a secondary ledger. It sets you apart.

Likely follow-up

Why keep tax books separate?

Tax depreciation rules, like MACRS or bonus, differ from book depreciation, so the tax team needs its own calculations.

8Tell me about a time you had to push back on a business request during an implementation.LeadershipIn-depth

"During design, the controller wanted the legacy 200-line approval matrix rebuilt exactly in Fusion. I owned the AP and approvals design and the go-live date. Tip: Show data, respect for the controller's concern, and a way to revisit. You didn't just win the argument.

Likely follow-up

How did the controller react?

Once I showed the reporting impact and maintenance cost, we agreed a phased approach, and the controller became a supporter.

9What SOX controls do you build into an Oracle Financials implementation?SOX & ControlsIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: public companies need controls designed in, not added later.

Answer:

"I think in four groups. Access controls: role design with segregation of duties, so nobody can create a supplier and pay it, checked with Advanced Access Controls or a third-party tool, plus quarterly access reviews and logged, time-limited support access. Configuration controls: change control for setups with approval and test evidence, migration with configuration packages, and audit trail on key setups like suppliers, bank accounts and payment terms. Process controls: invoice approvals and holds, three-way match, payment approvals, journal approval rules, reconciliations with sign-off, and supplier bank change verification. And evidence: reports the auditors can rely on, validated for completeness and accuracy."

Tip: Say you involve internal audit early in design. Controls bolted on before go-live always cause delays.

Likely follow-up

How do you test SoD in custom roles?

Run Advanced Access Controls or a role conflict matrix before go-live and after every role change.

10How do you approach data conversion for a Financials go-live?Integrations & DataIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: conversion is one of the biggest go-live risks.

Answer:

"First scope: open AP invoices, open AR transactions, assets and GL balances, and how much history, usually summary balances for one or two years. Master data goes first: suppliers, customers and banks, then transactions. I use FBDI through UCM and the import processes, or REST for small sets, with a COA mapping table owned by the business. Quality comes from at least three mock conversions, each reconciled with record counts, control totals and sample checks, and signed off by finance. Data is cleansed in the source before extraction. And there's a cutover plan with timing, owners and a fallback."

Tip: Say finance signs off the reconciliations. Auditors expect finance to own the numbers.

Likely follow-up

How much history would you convert?

Usually open items plus summary balances for one or two years; detailed history stays in an archive for audits.

12How do you estimate effort for a Finance implementation?Workstream LeadershipIn-depth

Why they ask: leads are often asked to size work.

Answer:

"I break the scope into modules times legal entities or BUs times countries, plus RICEW items, reports, interfaces, conversions, extensions and workflows, each rated simple, medium or complex. I use reference effort from past projects for each item type. I add effort for CRPs, test cycles, mock conversions, training, cutover and hypercare. I add 10% to 20% contingency depending on unknowns like data quality and client availability. Then I validate with the team and document the assumptions, like 'client provides test data' or 'maximum two mocks'."

Tip: Say you estimate bottom-up and sanity-check top-down against similar projects.

Likely follow-up

How do you account for unknowns in your estimate?

Add contingency based on risk, typically 15 to 20%, and revisit after design.

13How do you run fit-gap sessions effectively?Workstream LeadershipBasic

Why they ask: fit-gap decides how much custom work a project takes on.

Answer:

"I prepare process scenarios per module, like 'pay a foreign supplier by wire', and demo the standard Oracle flow first. For each step we agree whether it's a fit, a fit with configuration, or a gap. Gaps are classified as process change, report, extension or integration, with the business value of closing them. I push back on gaps that are only legacy habits. Decisions are captured with owners and dates, and big gaps go to the steering committee. The outcome is a prioritized gap list with solutions and estimates, signed off before build."

Tip: Say you demo standard first. It anchors the conversation on Oracle's process.

Likely follow-up

How do you avoid fit-gap sessions turning into wish lists?

Demo standard first, ask what business problem a request solves, and log gaps with priority.

14How would you design a shared services model for AP across 20 business units?DesignIn-depth

Why they ask: shared services is a common Fusion design.

Answer:

"I'd set up one or a few shared-service BUs as service providers for Payables invoicing and payments, serving the 20 client BUs. Reference data sets share payment terms and suppliers, and supplier sites are assigned to the client BUs. A central invoice inbox with document recognition routes invoices by BU. Approval rules route to approvers in the client BU, like cost center managers. A payment hub uses central payment process profiles and disbursement accounts per legal entity. Shared-services staff get access to all the BUs they serve. And the KPIs are cost per invoice, touchless rate and on-time payment."

Tip: Name the service provider relationship. It's the Fusion feature that makes this work.

Likely follow-up

What's the risk in a shared services AP model?

Approvals and coding knowledge sit in the business, so routing and reference data need to be right for every client BU.

15How do you design the payment process for a global company with many banks?DesignIn-depth

Why they ask: global payments are complex and high-risk.

Answer:

"For connectivity, host-to-host SFTP, or a bank hub or SWIFT, using ISO 20022 pain.001 wherever banks support it, to cut custom formats. Payment methods by country: ACH, wire, SEPA, check and local clearing. Payment process profiles per bank and format, and disbursement accounts per legal entity and currency. Payment approval for large amounts and positive pay for checks. Acknowledgment files, pain.002, update payment status. And bank statements, CAMT.053, feed reconciliation. I also agree treasury controls on who can release payments, and test with each bank."

Tip: Mention testing with each bank. Every bank has its own surprises.

Likely follow-up

Why standardize on ISO 20022?

One format for many banks reduces custom templates and testing, and supports richer remittance data.

16How would you design consolidation for a multi-entity group: GL vs FCCS?DesignIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: choosing the right consolidation tool matters.

Answer:

"Fusion GL can handle simple consolidation with translation, ledger sets, elimination rules and reporting currencies, which works for a few entities with simple ownership. FCCS is for complex groups: partial ownership, minority interest, multi-GAAP, intercompany matching, consolidation journals, statutory reporting and close task management. GL balances are loaded to FCCS each close through Data Integration, and mapping the GL chart to the FCCS account structure is key. The decision depends on ownership complexity, number of entities, reporting needs and the close timeline."

Tip: Give the decision drivers. It shows you'd choose, not just list.

Likely follow-up

At what point does FCCS make sense?

With many entities, partial ownership, multiple currencies and a need for consolidated cash flow.

17How do you handle multi-GAAP requirements, for example US GAAP and local statutory?DesignIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: multi-GAAP is common in global companies.

Answer:

"There are a few options. A subledger-level secondary ledger with a different accounting method, where SLA creates two sets of entries from the same transaction. An adjustment-only secondary ledger, holding only manual GAAP adjustments, with reports combining primary plus adjustments. Separate corporate and tax books in Assets for depreciation differences. And a different chart of accounts in a secondary ledger with a COA mapping. I choose by how many transactions differ: few differences, adjustment-only; systematic differences, like revenue or leases, a full subledger-level secondary ledger."

Tip: The "how many transactions differ" rule is the answer. Lead with it if pressed.

Likely follow-up

Which option is lightest?

An adjustment-only secondary ledger or reporting adjustments, if differences are few.

18How would you approach an EBS R12 to Fusion Financials migration?DesignIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: many clients are making this move.

Answer:

"I'd reimplement, not lift and shift: rethink enterprise structures, the chart of accounts and processes, and drop EBS customizations. Map EBS objects to Fusion: operating units to BUs, responsibilities to roles, customizations to extensions or standard features. For data, open transactions plus summary balances, with a decision on where history lives. Integrations are rebuilt on OIC and REST, and database-level ones retired. Decide on coexistence, for example Fusion Financials with EBS SCM integrated for a period. Manage the change: new roles and UI, and reporting moving to OTBI, BIP and Smart View. And use Oracle's EBS-to-Cloud tools where they fit."

Tip: Say "reimplement, not lift and shift" first. It sets the right expectations.

Likely follow-up

What's the biggest mistake in EBS to Fusion projects?

Copying EBS customizations and structures instead of redesigning for Fusion standard processes.

19How do you integrate a third-party tax engine like Vertex or Avalara?TaxIn-depth

Why they ask: most US Fusion customers use a partner engine.

Answer:

"Fusion Tax has a tax partner integration. I set up the tax regime for partner services, the provider configuration, and tax partner subscriptions for the business units or legal entities. Item tax codes and ship-from and ship-to locations must be accurate, because the partner uses geocodes to find jurisdictions. AR invoices, AP invoices and POs then call the partner to calculate tax, and the results are stored on the tax lines. Filing happens in the partner tool, and we reconcile tax accounts in GL. In testing I focus on exempt customers with certificates, use tax on AP, and jurisdiction edge cases like addresses near city limits."

Tip: Mention address quality and geocodes. They cause most partner tax errors.

Likely follow-up

What do you test in a Vertex integration?

Tax calculation for each nexus, exemptions and the tax returned on invoices and credit memos.

20How would you design billing for a subscription or services business in Oracle?Accounts ReceivableIn-depth

Why they ask: they want to see you pick the right tool for the billing model, not force everything into AR.

Answer:

"I'd choose by how complex the billing is. For recurring billing with renewals, upgrades and usage charges, Subscription Management is the right tool. For services billed against projects, time and materials or milestones, Project Billing. If an outside billing system already does the rating, AR with AutoInvoice is enough. Whatever bills, Revenue Management handles ASC 606 allocation and over-time recognition. The design points I'd settle early are invoice frequency, proration for mid-cycle changes, upgrades and downgrades, and usage rating. On top sit collections and credit management, and an integration with CRM or CPQ so contracts flow in cleanly."

Tip: Say "I'd choose based on the billing model" up front. It answers the real question.

Likely follow-up

What decides between Subscription Management and AR?

The complexity of renewals, upgrades and usage billing; simple fixed recurring invoices can stay in AR.

21What are the common causes of AR-to-GL reconciliation differences and how do you prevent them?Accounts ReceivableIn-depth

Why they ask: reconciliation is a control. They want causes and prevention, not just "I run a report".

Answer:

"The common causes are: transactions not yet accounted or not transferred and posted; manual journals posted straight to the receivable account in GL; transactions accounted in a different period than the aging date; misunderstanding the unapplied and on-account cash accounts; and revaluation entries that the aging doesn't show. To prevent them, I set the receivable accounts as third-party control accounts so nobody can post a manual journal to them, schedule Create Accounting daily, and make the Receivables to Ledger Reconciliation report a sign-off step on the close checklist. With those three in place, differences become rare and easy to explain."

Tip: The control-account setting is the key prevention step. Name it.

Likely follow-up

How do you prevent manual journals to the receivable account?

Make it a third-party control account so only subledger entries can post to it.

22A CFO wants to shorten the close from 10 days to 5. What would you do?Period CloseIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a business-outcome question. They want a diagnosis first, then levers.

Answer:

"I'd start by mapping the current close: every task, owner, dependency and duration, to find the critical path. Then I'd pull the levers that shorten that path: continuous accounting with daily Create Accounting, daily bank reconciliation and weekly intercompany settlement; automated accruals, recurring journals and allocations; earlier AP and AR cut-offs with estimates for the last days; automatic reconciliation of balance sheet accounts; a standard close checklist in a task tool so everyone sees status; and fixing data problems upstream so close days aren't spent on corrections. Then I'd measure days to close and post-close adjustments every month and keep improving."

Tip: Say "find the critical path first". Cutting tasks that aren't on it won't shorten the close.

Likely follow-up

What's a realistic first-year improvement?

Often two to three days from automation and parallel tasks, then more with continuous improvement.

23What is a third-party control account in SLA?Period CloseIn-depth

Why they ask: a key control that keeps subledger and GL in sync.

Answer:

"It's a natural account flagged so that only subledger transactions with a supplier or customer can post to it, like the AP liability or AR receivable account. That stops manual GL journals hitting those accounts, makes third-party balance reports available from SLA, and keeps the subledger-to-GL reconciliation clean. It's set on the natural account value, with Third Party Control Account set to Supplier or Customer."

Tip: Tie it to reconciliation. That's why controllers want it.

Likely follow-up

What error do users see if they try to post manually?

GL rejects the journal because the account requires a third party from a subledger.

24How do you design a finance test strategy for a large implementation?TestingIn-depth

Why they ask: testing strategy is a lead responsibility.

Answer:

"Unit and configuration testing by consultants. SIT with end-to-end flows across modules and integrations: P2P, O2C, record-to-report and acquire-to-retire. Data validation of conversions with counts, values and samples. UAT by business users with real scenarios and day-in-the-life tests. A parallel run or reconciliation for high-risk areas, like closing one month in both systems. Performance testing for high volumes, like AutoInvoice and payment runs. Regression automation for quarterly updates. And entry and exit criteria, a defect triage rhythm, and sign-off by the controller."

Tip: Mention a parallel close. It's the strongest proof for a controller.

Likely follow-up

What test phase do projects most often rush?

Parallel runs or month-end close simulations, which catch the real issues.

25How do you plan a Finance cutover?CutoverIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: cutover is where months of work succeed or fail.

Answer:

"I build a minute-by-minute cutover plan for the go-live weekend, with owner, start time, duration, dependencies and a verification step for each task. Legacy is frozen: last invoice entry, last payment run, last receipts. Conversion runs master data, then open AP and AR, then assets, then GL balances, reconciled and signed off at each step. Then periods are opened, integrations switched on, and security checked. A go/no-go meeting uses agreed criteria. There's a fallback plan if a critical step fails. And hypercare covers the first payment run and the first close. I rehearse it in at least two timed mock cutovers."

Tip: Mention timed rehearsals. Without them, the weekend runs long.

Likely follow-up

What's your go/no-go criteria?

Data reconciled, key tests passed, cutover steps verified and business sign-off.

26What do you focus on during hypercare after a Finance go-live?CutoverBasic

Why they ask: hypercare protects the business after go-live.

Answer:

"The first payment run and first bank file, because they're the highest risk. The first month-end close: subledger accounting errors and reconciliations. Integrations, with failures monitored daily. User support with floor walkers, a triage war room and a daily issue review. KPIs like open tickets, invoices on hold, unapplied cash and accounting errors. And a clean handover to the support team with knowledge transfer and runbooks."

Tip: Lead with the first payment run. Paying suppliers wrong is the most visible failure.

Likely follow-up

How long does hypercare usually last?

Through at least the first month-end close, often two or three closes.

33How are AI and agents being used in Oracle Fusion Financials?AIIn-depth

Why they ask: a current knowledge question for finance consultants.

Answer:

"Intelligent Document Recognition reads invoices.
