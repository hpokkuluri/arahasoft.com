1How do you lead the SCM workstream on a Fusion implementation?Workstream LeadershipBasicTop question

Why they ask: they want to see how you run SCM end to end.

Answer:

"I own the scope and plan for Procurement, Inventory, Order Management, Costing, Product Hub, and Manufacturing and Planning where in scope. I'm the design authority for end-to-end flows, P2P, O2C, plan-to-produce, returns and intercompany, consistent across modules and with Finance. I lead CRPs, the decisions log and gap resolution with operations leaders. I assign modules, review configuration and designs, and coach. I coordinate with Finance on costing and accruals, with integrations like 3PL, MES and e-commerce, and with data for items, suppliers and on-hand. And I drive testing, cutover and hypercare readiness for warehouses and plants."

Tip: Stress consistency with Finance. SCM designs often fail at that seam.

Likely follow-up

How do you keep SCM and Finance from designing in silos?

Joint design sessions on every touchpoint — receipt accruals, costing, intercompany, AR invoicing from shipments — with both leads signing off. I keep a shared list of integration decisions, and run end-to-end tests that cross both workstreams, like procure-to-pay through to GL.

2How would you design inventory organizations for a company with plants, DCs and third-party warehouses?DesignIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: org design affects costing, planning and close.

Answer:

"One inventory org per physical site with distinct costing, planning or legal ownership: each plant and each DC. 3PL warehouses get their own orgs, often with simpler setups, integrated through shipment and receipt messages. Subinventories separate stock types within a site: raw, WIP staging, finished goods, quarantine and returns. Cost organizations group orgs that share costs, like all US DCs on one average cost. The item master org sits at the top, with shipping networks and sourcing rules between orgs. And if orgs belong to different legal entities, SFO flows handle the finance. I avoid too many orgs, because each adds setup, planning and close overhead."

Tip: End with "avoid too many orgs". It shows restraint.

Likely follow-up

When would a 3PL warehouse not need its own inventory org?

If you don't need to see its stock separately — for example a 3PL holding a tiny amount of low-value stock. But usually you do need it, for visibility of stock by location, valuation and replenishment, so I'd normally create an inventory org or at least a subinventory for each 3PL.

3Standard vs average costing — how do you help a client choose?DesignIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a key finance-SCM decision.

Answer:

"Standard cost gives stable, predictable valuation, and variances show purchasing and manufacturing performance. But it needs yearly cost updates and variance analysis, and it's common in manufacturing. Perpetual average cost reflects actual purchase prices with less maintenance and no variance analysis, but cost can swing with prices and inefficiencies are harder to spot; it's common in distribution. I help the client decide based on industry, the margin analysis they need, finance team capacity, how volatile purchase prices are, and group reporting standards. If they're unsure, I prototype both with real data."

Tip: Offer to prototype both. It turns a debate into evidence.

Likely follow-up

What's the hidden cost of standard costing?

The annual cost-setting and variance analysis. Standard costs need good BOM and routing data and a finance team that reviews and explains variances every month. If the client doesn't have that discipline, average costing may give them more accurate numbers with less effort.

4How would you design centralized procurement for a company with 10 operating companies?DesignIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: central procurement is a common Fusion design.

Answer:

"One procurement BU, or a few regional ones, serving the requisitioning BUs through service provider relationships. Global agreements enabled for all requisitioning BUs, with local agreements where needed. Category-based buyer assignment for category managers. Approval rules in the requisitioning BUs for budget owners, and in the procurement BU for buyer and contract approvals. A shared supplier master with site assignments to each BU. Sold-to legal entity and bill-to BU per operating company, so AP and liabilities sit in the right entity. And SFO if a central entity buys and resells internally."

Tip: Mention sold-to legal entity. It keeps liabilities in the right company.

Likely follow-up

What's the risk of centralizing procurement?

Local needs can be missed — like a plant that needs a local supplier for emergency parts. I'd keep local buyers for site-specific categories under the central model, with central agreements and policies, so you get leverage on spend without slowing down the plants.

5How do you design supply chain financial orchestration flows?DesignIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: SFO design needs both supply chain and finance thinking.

Answer:

"I map the physical flows, who ships and receives, and the financial flows, who buys and sells legally, with tax and finance. For each flow I define the trade relationships between entities, transfer pricing rules like cost-plus or a price list, documentation, meaning real intercompany AR and AP invoices or journals only, and the accounting. Then I set up the financial orchestration flows, procurement or shipment, with assignment rules. I consider VAT and customs, and profit-in-inventory elimination. And I test end to end, including intercompany invoices and costing in each entity."

Tip: Say "with tax and finance". Getting them in early prevents redesigns.

Likely follow-up

Who must sign off SFO flows?

Tax and finance, because transfer prices and legal ownership drive tax liabilities in each country. A wrong transfer price can mean penalties in two jurisdictions. I'd get tax to approve the pricing rules and test the intercompany invoices before go-live.

6How would you handle drop-ship vs warehouse fulfillment decisions in the O2C design?DesignIn-depth

Why they ask: fulfillment choices affect cost, service and accounting.

Answer:

"Item and supplier attributes decide eligibility, for example bulky items always drop ship. Sourcing rules and orchestration process assignment route lines to the drop-ship or warehouse process. I consider customer promise and lead times, margins and freight, visibility through supplier ASNs, and the financial flows, with logical receipt and shipment accounting. I check AP and AR timing, supplier invoice versus customer invoice. And returns: does the customer send them to the supplier or to us?"

Tip: Mention returns. It's the question nobody asks until go-live.

Likely follow-up

How do you track drop ship margin?

Revenue from AR and cost from the supplier invoice, matched through the order and PO link.

7How do you set up item data governance in Product Hub?Product HubIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: governance is what keeps the item master clean.

Answer:

"I define item classes with required attributes and numbering. New Item Requests have definition steps for each function, engineering, procurement, planning, costing and quality, and an approval step. Validation rules and templates keep things consistent. Change orders handle changes after release. Each class has a data steward, and we track cycle time and data completeness. If there's a PLM system, like Oracle PLM or Agile, it's the source for engineering items. The result is fewer duplicates and complete attributes before anyone transacts."

Tip: Mention data stewards per class. Governance needs named people.

Likely follow-up

What's the most important governance control?

Required attributes and approval steps in New Item Requests, so an item can't go active without complete data — UOM, category, costing and planning attributes. Bad item data is the root cause of many downstream issues, from wrong costs to MRP errors.

8How would you clean and migrate a legacy item master with many duplicates?Product HubIn-depth

Why they ask: a common migration challenge.

Answer:

"First I profile the data: duplicates by description and manufacturer part number, UOM inconsistencies, inactive items. Then agree rules with the business: which items migrate, for example activity in the last 24 months, open transactions or on-hand, and how to pick the golden record. Then standardize descriptions and UOMs and map to the new item classes and categories. Then build a cross-reference from legacy to new items for open orders, BOMs and history. Load through item import in stages, reconcile counts and key attributes, and get business sign-off on each mock."

Tip: Give the 24-month activity rule. It usually cuts the item count a lot.

Likely follow-up

How do you decide which duplicate survives?

The one with the most open transactions or cleanest data, with cross-references for the others.

9How do you fix invoice matching problems on blanket POs with many receipts?ProcurementIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a real problem in high-volume procurement. They want a fix with its impacts.

Answer:

"The symptom is AP staff having to pick specific receipts, often old ones, and invoices going on hold. One fix is changing the invoice match option on the agreement or PO schedules from Receipt to Purchase Order. It's still 3-way matching, because the total received quantity is still checked, but AP matches at the PO level instead of picking receipts. I'd update the document style and agreement defaults so new releases default correctly, and consider a mass update for open POs. Then check the impacts: price variance by receipt, accrual clearing and reports. Receivers need to receive promptly so totals are right. And I'd pilot with a few high-volume suppliers first."

Tip: Explain that it's still 3-way matching. That answers the auditor's first worry.

Likely follow-up

What's the tradeoff in switching from receipt to PO matching?

It's simpler for AP because they match to the PO line instead of picking among many receipts. But you lose the control that the invoice matches what was actually received, so the receipt-close tolerance and the quantity-received tolerance become the controls. I'd set tolerances like 5% and review exceptions.

Which procurement KPI do executives watch most?

Spend under management and savings against baseline.

11How would you run an inventory accuracy improvement program?InventoryIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: accuracy affects planning, customer service and audit.

Answer:

"I measure record accuracy by count and value variance by location. I set up ABC cycle counting with tolerances and root-cause codes. I fix process causes: receiving delays, unrecorded issues, wrong locators and UOM mistakes. I move to scanning and mobile transactions instead of paper. I use locator control and material status to stop misplacement. And I review adjustments weekly with warehouse leads. A typical target is 98% or better for A items."

Tip: Give the target. It shows you've run one.

Likely follow-up

What accuracy target do you aim for?

Typically 95 to 98% record accuracy, depending on the industry — pharma and high-value electronics aim higher. I'd measure it by cycle count hit rate, fix the root causes like unrecorded moves and wrong UOMs, and count A items more often until the number holds.

12How would you design lot and serial control for a pharmaceutical company?InventoryIn-depth

Why they ask: pharma has strict traceability and regulatory requirements.

Answer:

"Lot control on all APIs, raw materials and finished goods. Serial control at saleable units where DSCSA or EU FMD serialization applies, often in a dedicated serialization system integrated with Oracle. Lot attributes include expiry, retest date, manufacturer lot, grade and origin. Material status moves from quarantine to approved or released to rejected, and QA release controls availability. Picking is FEFO. Genealogy supports recalls from raw material lot to customer shipment. And GxP: a validated system, audit trails, and electronic signatures under 21 CFR Part 11 for key transactions."

Tip: Mention material status and QA release. It's the core of pharma inventory control.

Likely follow-up

What's DSCSA?

The US Drug Supply Chain Security Act, which requires serialized traceability for prescription drugs.

13How do you plan the inventory cutover for a warehouse go-live?InventoryIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a bad cutover means a warehouse that can't ship.

Answer:

"I freeze legacy transactions, stopping receiving and shipping or logging them by hand. Then a physical count or a validated cycle-count snapshot by item, lot, serial and locator. On-hand is loaded with costs from finance and reconciled for quantity and value to legacy and GL. Open POs, sales orders and transfers are converted after on-hand. Manual transactions from the freeze are caught up after go-live. And shipping and receiving restart with support on the floor. I plan it around low-volume days and tell customers and suppliers in advance."

Tip: Mention telling customers and suppliers. Cutover affects them too.

Likely follow-up

How do you handle transactions during the freeze?

Log every transaction manually during the freeze — receipts, issues, shipments — then enter them in Fusion right after go-live, in the same order. Keep the freeze as short as possible, ideally a weekend, and keep emergency shipments to a pre-agreed list.

14How would you design the O2C process for a company selling through e-commerce and EDI?Order ManagementIn-depth

Why they ask: a multi-channel design question.

Answer:

"Orders come in from e-commerce through REST order import or OIC, and EDI 850s through a B2B provider into OM. Pricing either runs in Fusion or honors the channel price. Orchestration covers standard ship, drop ship and back-to-back. GOP promises dates, and ATP is shown to customers. Shipping integrates with carriers, sends 856 ASNs to EDI customers and tracking to e-commerce. Invoicing sends 810s for EDI, and card payments settle for e-commerce. Returns work in both channels. And order import failures are monitored."

Tip: Name the EDI documents. It shows you've done real EDI work.

Likely follow-up

How do you handle EDI acknowledgments?

Send 855 acknowledgments and 856 ASNs back through the B2B provider after booking and shipping.

16How would you approach implementing Fusion Supply Planning?PlanningIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a delivery approach question.

Answer:

"Data readiness comes first: item planning attributes like lead times, order modifiers and safety stock, accurate BOMs and routings, and sourcing rules. Then collections setup and frequency. Then design plan types, supply plan, replenishment and MPS, and horizons. Then define the exceptions planners will act on. Then pilot one product line and compare the plan's recommendations with what planners would have done. Training focuses on trust: planners need to see why the plan recommends something. And I measure service level, inventory and expedites."

Tip: Mention the side-by-side comparison in the pilot. It's how trust is built.

Likely follow-up

What should run in parallel before go-live?

Run the new Fusion plan alongside the legacy plan for a few cycles and compare the recommendations — planned orders, quantities and dates. Big differences usually reveal data problems like lead times or safety stock, which are much cheaper to fix before planners rely on the new plan.

17How do you set safety stock?PlanningIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: safety stock is a direct trade-off between service and cash.

Answer:

"Safety stock can be a fixed quantity, days of supply, or statistical. The statistical method uses demand variability, lead-time variability and a target service level; for example, 95% service uses a z-value of about 1.65. Fusion can calculate safety stock from forecast error. I set higher service levels for A items and critical customers and lower for C items, and review them every quarter, because static safety stock goes stale."

Tip: Mention the z-value. It shows you understand the math.

Likely follow-up

What's the formula idea for statistical safety stock?

Service factor times the standard deviation of demand over the lead time. For example, a 95% service level has a factor of about 1.65; if the demand standard deviation over lead time is 100 units, safety stock is about 165 units. Higher service levels need much more stock.

18Discrete vs process manufacturing — what is different in Oracle?ManufacturingIn-depth

Why they ask: industries need different models.

Answer:

"Discrete manufacturing assembles items from components, like electronics or machinery, using BOMs, work definitions, work orders with operations, and serial tracking. Process manufacturing uses recipes and formulas with yields, by-products and co-products, batch sizes, and potency or grade, like chemicals, food and pharma. Fusion supports both, with process work orders that have multiple outputs. Costing differs too: process manufacturing handles yields and allocates cost across co-products."

Tip: Mention co-product costing. It's the hard part of process manufacturing.

Likely follow-up

What's different in process costing?

It handles formulas, yields and co-products, with costs allocated across outputs.

19How would you integrate quality inspection into receiving for a regulated company?QualityIn-depth

Why they ask: a regulated-industry design question.

Answer:

"Regulated items use inspection receipt routing. Inspection plans by item and supplier set sampling, often AQL, and specifications. Received material goes to a quarantine subinventory or status until QA releases it. Failures create nonconformances and supplier corrective action requests. Release uses electronic signatures. And results feed supplier performance scores."

Tip: Mention quarantine until QA release. It's the core control.

Likely follow-up

What's AQL?

Acceptable Quality Level, a sampling standard that sets how many units to inspect and how many defects are allowed.

21How would you design an MES integration for work order execution?IntegrationIn-depth

Why they ask: MES integration is critical for plants.

Answer:

"From Fusion to MES go released work orders, work definitions, and item and lot master data, triggered by business events or a schedule. From MES to Fusion come operation completions, material consumption with lots, assembly completions, scrap and resource time, through the work order transaction REST APIs. I decide the system of record for each step so nothing is entered twice. Error handling includes retries, an error queue and reconciliation reports for WIP and on-hand. And timing is near real time for inventory accuracy, with batching only where it's acceptable."

Tip: Say "system of record for each step". It prevents double entry.

Likely follow-up

What's the biggest MES integration risk?

Timing mismatches, like completions reported before materials are issued.

Integration partners like 3PLs and EDI join using their test environments. I check the accounting at each step: receipt, issue, COGS and intercompany invoices. Warehouse and procurement users run day-in-the-life tests. And I run volume tests for pick release and order import."

The finance accounting from each flow.

24How do you balance operations (speed) and finance (control) requirements in SCM design?StakeholdersBasic

Why they ask: this tension is in every SCM project.

Answer:

"I get both in the room for the key designs: receiving, inventory adjustments, costing and returns. I use risk-based controls, with approvals only where value or risk is high, like adjustments above a set amount. I automate controls with tolerances and material status instead of manual approvals. I make the accounting impact visible in CRPs. And I agree KPIs both sides care about, like inventory accuracy and cost per order."

Tip: "Automate controls instead of approvals" is the key idea.

Likely follow-up

Give an example of a risk-based control.

Approval only for adjustments above a value threshold.

25How do you reconcile inventory valuation to the General Ledger?CostingIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a key month-end control.

Answer:

"I run the perpetual inventory valuation report as of period end by cost org and valuation unit, and compare it to the GL inventory account balances, by org through the cost center or org segment. Differences usually come from uncosted transactions, cost accounting not created or transferred, manual GL journals to inventory accounts, or wrong account mapping in cost accounting rules. I block manual journals to inventory accounts, and I only close the cost period after reconciling."

Tip: Say you block manual journals to inventory accounts. It's the prevention step.

Likely follow-up

What's a common cause of valuation differences?

Manual GL journals posted to inventory accounts. The subledger says $5 million, GL says $5.1 million, and the difference is a journal posted directly. Others are uncosted transactions and cost adjustments that weren't transferred. I'd lock inventory accounts against manual journals.

26What are the steps of the cost period close in Fusion Cost Management?CostingIn-depth

Why they ask: a practical close sequence question.

Answer:

"I make sure inventory, receiving and WIP transactions are complete and transferred to costing. I run the cost processor and fix errors. I run Create Receipt Accounting and Create Cost Accounting and transfer to GL. I process landed cost adjustments and any standard cost updates. I close work orders and calculate variances. I review unprocessed transactions and reconcile valuation to GL. Then I close the cost period for the cost org and book, and open the next."

Tip: Keep the order clear. Each step depends on the one before.

Likely follow-up

What blocks the cost period close?

Uncosted transactions or cost processor errors.

27How would you design a returns (RMA) process with inspection and refurbishment?ReturnsIn-depth

Why they ask: returns design touches OM, inventory, manufacturing and AR.

Answer:

"Return orders reference the original order, and return reasons drive the flow. Returns are received into a returns subinventory with inspection routing. Disposition is restock if good, refurbish through a repair work order, scrap, or return to vendor. Credit is created in AR once received and inspected, or immediately for trusted customers. Returned items are valued at original cost or a refurbishment cost. And analytics on return reasons help fix root causes."

Tip: Mention credit timing for trusted customers. It shows business awareness.

Likely follow-up

How do you value refurbished items?

At refurbish cost or a separate item with its own cost.

28When would you recommend a WMS over standard Fusion Inventory?LogisticsIn-depth

Why they ask: a judgment question about tools.

Answer:

"When a DC has high volume, many lines a day, and complex picking like waves, zones and cartonization. When operations are LPN-based, with cross-docking and directed putaway. When they need labor management and automation like conveyors or robots. And when mobile RF is used everywhere. Fusion Warehouse Management now covers many of these natively, and for very large or automated DCs, a specialist WMS like Manhattan or Blue Yonder. The decision comes down to volume, complexity, automation and integration cost."

Tip: End with the decision criteria. It shows judgment rather than preference.

Likely follow-up

What's the cost of WMS?

More setup and training, so only worth it at high volume and complexity.

29What changes when moving SCM from EBS to Fusion?EBSIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: EBS clients want to know what's different.

Answer:

"OM workflows become order orchestration, which is process-driven with change management. Org-level costing becomes cost organizations and cost books. Item categories and templates become Product Hub item classes, NIRs and change orders. ASCP becomes Supply Chain Planning Cloud, with collections from Fusion. Advanced Pricing modifiers become pricing strategies, segments and algorithms. Intercompany transaction flows become SFO. And customizations become extensions with Visual Builder and OIC, while reports move to OTBI and BIP. So it's a redesign, not a technical upgrade."

Tip: Close with "a redesign, not an upgrade". It sets client expectations.

Likely follow-up

What's the biggest mindset shift for EBS SCM users?

Order orchestration replacing workflows. In EBS, order flows were set up through workflows and custom code; in Fusion, the orchestration process defines the steps, with change management and status monitoring built in. Users need to learn to read the orchestration plan to find where an order is stuck.

30How do you plan SCM data conversion?Data MigrationBasic

Answer:

"The sequence is locations and org setup, suppliers and customers, items with categories and cross-references, BOMs and work definitions, costs, on-hand with lots and serials, open POs and agreements, open sales orders, open work orders, and open transfers. The data is cleansed in the source, and the business owns the mappings. I run at least three mocks, timing and reconciling each one, with automated validation of counts, values and key attributes. Tip: Give the sequence. Loading things out of order breaks everything downstream.

What's loaded last in SCM conversion?

On-hand balances and open transactions, right before go-live.

31What makes an SCM cutover harder than a Finance cutover?CutoverIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: to see if you understand physical operations.

Answer:

"Physical operations don't stop: trucks arrive and orders ship, so you need a freeze window or a manual catch-up. On-hand accuracy depends on a count at cutover. Documents are in motion: in-transit shipments, partly received POs, orders picked but not shipped. Partner integrations, EDI, 3PL and carriers, must switch on the same day. And labels and documents must work on day one. I manage it with rehearsals, partner communication, extra staff and a day-one war room."

Tip: Mention labels. A warehouse that can't print labels can't ship.

Likely follow-up

How do you shorten the freeze window?

Pre-load all static data — items, suppliers, customers, sourcing rules — weeks before, and count only high-value or high-volume locations during the freeze, using cycle count results for the rest. That can cut a five-day freeze to two.

32What do you monitor in the first two weeks after an SCM go-live?CutoverBasic

Why they ask: hypercare monitoring protects operations.

Answer:

"Orders: import errors, lines stuck in orchestration, backorders and on-time shipping. Receipts: ASN and receipt errors, and the quarantine backlog. Inventory: negative balances, unprocessed transactions and cycle count variances. Costing: uncosted transactions. Integrations: 3PL and EDI failures. Procurement: requisitions failing auto-PO and approval delays. And users: ticket volumes and training gaps. I run a daily stand-up with operations and IT and track KPIs against the baseline."

Tip: Mention uncosted transactions. They surface at the first month-end if missed.

Likely follow-up

What's the most common week-one SCM issue?

Orders stuck in orchestration because of missing setup.

33Tell me about a difficult trade-off you made in an SCM design.BehavioralBasicTop question

Why they ask: design is about trade-offs.

Answer (example):

"Operations wanted every warehouse adjustment approved, which would have slowed cycle counting. I had to balance control and throughput. I analyzed the adjustment history and found 90% were under $200. We set small, in-tolerance adjustments to post automatically, required approval for high-value or A items, and added a weekly review report. Cycle counting kept pace, finance kept control over 95% of adjustment value, and audit accepted the design."

Tip: Use data to settle the trade-off. It's the convincing part.

Likely follow-up

What did you decide?

Approval only above a value threshold — for example inventory adjustments over $5,000. That kept daily operations fast for small adjustments while giving finance control over large ones, which is where the risk really was.

Tip: End with the lesson. It shows you'll plan for it next time.

A file-based interim interface until the 3PL's API was ready.

36How would you design supplier onboarding with risk and compliance checks?ProcurementIn-depth
