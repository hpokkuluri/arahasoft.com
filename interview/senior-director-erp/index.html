Senior Director, ERP Interview Questions

The questions interviewers ask for a Senior Director of ERP role, with why they ask, a full answer you can say in the room, and a tip. Tap a question to see the answer.

55 questions 31 basic 24 in-depth
1How is the Senior Director role different from a Director in ERP?RoleBasic

Why they ask: to see if you're ready for enterprise scope.

Answer:

"The portfolio is wider: ERP plus adjacent platforms like EPM, HCM, procurement networks, integration and analytics, often enterprise-wide. The organization is larger, several directors and managers, 50 to 150 people including partners, with budgets in the tens of millions. I own strategy and transformation with multi-year roadmaps. I partner with the CFO, COO and CHRO and get exposure to the board or audit committee. I design the operating model, like product and platform teams. And I own the talent strategy and the leadership bench."

Tip: Mention operating model design. It's what changes most at this level.

2How would you develop an enterprise applications strategy for the next 3–5 years?StrategyIn-depth

Why they ask: strategy is the core of the role.

Answer:

"I start with business strategy: growth, M&A, the operating model like GBS, margin goals and risk. Then the current state: platforms, cost, technical debt, capability gaps and satisfaction. I agree principles: cloud-first, platform consolidation, standard processes, data as an asset, AI-enabled and secure by design. Then a target architecture and capability map, a roadmap with investment waves and value milestones, the operating model and talent plan, and funding, governance and KPIs."

Tip: Mention principles. They make hundreds of later decisions easier.

3How do you align the ERP roadmap with business strategy?StrategyBasic

Why they ask: relevance to the business.

Answer:

"I translate business objectives into capabilities, for example 'expand into five countries' becomes localizations and template rollouts. I plan jointly with business leaders, annually and quarterly. I prioritize by value with clear outcomes. I keep a portfolio view across programs. And I track and report benefits."

Tip: Use the five-countries example. It shows the translation clearly.

4Would you organize enterprise applications by product/platform teams or by project? Why?Operating ModelIn-depth

Why they ask: a key operating model decision.

Answer:

"A product or platform model uses long-lived teams aligned to business capabilities, like R2R, P2P, O2C, Plan and Hire-to-Retire, with product owners. That gives continuous improvement, deep knowledge and accountability for outcomes. A project model uses temporary teams, which suits big transformations. My usual answer is a product model to run and evolve, with a program overlay for major transformations, and shared platform teams for integration, data and security. Funding moves from projects to capacity-based product funding."

Tip: Give the hybrid answer. It's realistic and shows nuance.

5How do you design a Global Business Services–aligned enterprise applications team?Operating ModelIn-depth

Why they ask: GBS alignment is common.

Answer:

"Process ownership sits in GBS on the business side, platform ownership sits in IT, with joint governance. There's a shared roadmap for automation, including AI agents and workflow. A service catalog with SLAs. Teams co-located or aligned with GBS hubs. And KPIs tied to GBS outcomes, like cost per transaction and quality."

Tip: Separate process and platform ownership clearly. It avoids turf battles.

6How do you manage a $30M enterprise applications budget?FinanceIn-depth

Why they ask: financial leadership at scale.

Answer:

"I split the portfolio into run, licenses, support and infrastructure; change, projects; and innovation. I benchmark costs per user and per transaction against peers. I optimize through license rationalization, vendor consolidation, automation and location strategy. Investments go through business cases, stage gates and benefit tracking. And I run monthly financial reviews, manage variances, and report transparently to the CIO and CFO."

Tip: Mention benchmarking. It answers "are we spending the right amount?".

7How do you negotiate a large Oracle renewal or expansion?FinanceIn-depth

Why they ask: commercial skill.

Answer:

"I know our usage and needs, current and on the roadmap, so we avoid shelfware. I use timing, like Oracle's fiscal year-end, competition and multi-year commitments as leverage. I bundle needed products like EPM, FDI and OIC with price protections and caps on increases. I negotiate contract terms: flexibility to swap or reduce, support terms and SLAs. And I work with procurement and legal, with executive sponsorship."

Tip: Mention caps on price increases. Renewals often hide large uplifts.

8How do you build a strategic partner ecosystem for enterprise applications?VendorsBasic

Why they ask: partner strategy.

Answer:

"I tier partners: a strategic SI for transformation, an AMS partner for run, and niche specialists. Roles are clear to avoid overlap and finger-pointing. Performance is managed with KPIs and quarterly business reviews. I expect innovation, like accelerators and AI. Knowledge ownership stays internal. And I keep competitive tension without constant churn."

Tip: Mention avoiding finger-pointing. Overlapping partner roles cause it.

9How would you lead a global ERP transformation across 30 countries?TransformationIn-depth

Why they ask: large-scale transformation leadership.

Answer:

"Executive sponsorship from the CFO or COO and a clear case for change. A global template owned by process owners, with a design authority. A phased rollout, pilot then waves, run as a rollout factory. Data and integration programs started early. Change management at scale with country leads, super users and communications. Program governance: steering committees, a PMO, risk management and benefit tracking. And strong program leadership, retaining key people."

Tip: Mention a rollout factory. It's how 30 countries become repeatable.

10How do you manage organizational change and resistance in a large ERP transformation?TransformationBasic

Why they ask: change leadership.

Answer:

"Leadership alignment first, with leaders visibly championing the change. Stakeholder mapping and engagement plans. A change network with champions in each business area. Communication that covers why, what, when, and how it affects me. Role-based training at the right time. Resistance is addressed by listening, involving people and adjusting where reasonable, while holding firm on principles. And readiness and adoption are measured."

Tip: Say "hold firm on principles". Flexibility without limits erodes the template.

11How do you report to the audit committee or board on ERP risk and programs?ExecutiveIn-depth

Why they ask: board-level communication.

Answer:

"Concisely: program status, top risks, control status, cybersecurity posture and major incidents. In business language, focused on implications for financial reporting and operations. With mitigations and decisions needed. And prepared with the CFO, CIO and internal audit, so there are no surprises."

Tip: Say "prepared with internal audit". Boards trust aligned messages.

12How do you build credibility with the C-suite?ExecutiveBasic

Why they ask: executive presence.

Answer:

"I deliver on commitments. I understand the business deeply: the P&L and operations. I bring insight: data, benchmarks and opportunities. I'm candid about risks. I communicate concisely. And I build relationships beyond formal meetings."

Tip: Put "deliver on commitments" first. Everything else depends on it.

13How do you build a leadership bench in a large organization?PeopleBasic

Why they ask: succession at scale.

Answer:

"I identify high-potentials and set succession plans for key roles. I develop them through stretch assignments, rotations, coaching and leadership programs. I delegate real decisions. I give feedback and sponsorship. I build a diverse pipeline. And I measure the internal promotion rate and successor readiness."

Tip: Mention delegating real decisions. Leaders grow by deciding.

14How do you shape culture in an enterprise applications organization?PeopleBasic

Why they ask: culture leadership.

Answer:

"I make values concrete: focus on the business customer, ownership, quality, learning and collaboration. Leaders model the behaviors. Rituals reinforce them: demos, retrospectives and recognition. Hiring and promotion reflect the values. And I address behaviors that conflict with them, even from high performers."

Tip: Mention addressing high performers. Culture is defined by what you tolerate.

15How would you restructure an organization that is siloed by technology rather than business process?PeopleIn-depth

Why they ask: organization redesign.

Answer:

"I'd design teams around end-to-end business capabilities, for example P2P covering procurement, AP and the integrations. Shared services provide platforms: integration, data and security. The transition explains the reasoning, involves leaders, phases the changes, and supports people through new roles. And I measure outcomes like cycle times and satisfaction."

Tip: Mention supporting people through role changes. Restructures fail on people, not charts.

16How do you approach cybersecurity for ERP and enterprise applications?RiskIn-depth

Why they ask: security accountability.

Answer:

"I partner with the CISO on the shared responsibility model for SaaS. Identity: SSO, MFA, privileged access management and automated joiner-mover-leaver. Data protection: encryption, masking and DLP. Integration security: OAuth, secret management and least privilege. Monitoring with logs sent to the SIEM and anomaly detection. Third-party risk for partners with access. And incident response playbooks that include ERP."

Tip: Mention the shared responsibility model. SaaS doesn't make security Oracle's problem alone.

17How do you prepare for a major ERP outage or ransomware scenario?RiskIn-depth

Why they ask: resilience planning.

Answer:

"A business impact analysis of critical processes and maximum tolerable downtime. Oracle's SaaS resilience covers the app, so I plan for our integrations, extensions and data exports. Manual workarounds for payments, payroll and shipping. Backups of critical data and configuration outside the platform. Tabletop exercises with business and security. And communication plans."

Tip: Mention tabletop exercises. Plans that aren't practiced fail.

18How would you lead an enterprise AI program for back-office processes?AIIn-depth

Why they ask: an executive-level question.

Answer:

"I'd set the vision: augment teams, automate routine work and improve insight. Build a portfolio of use cases across finance, procurement, supply chain and HR, scored by value and risk. Use embedded Oracle AI first, and an enterprise AI platform for cross-system use. Govern with a responsible AI policy, risk tiers, human oversight and audit trails. Fix data foundations. Plan the workforce impact and reskilling. And track value."

Tip: Mention workforce planning. Leaders want to see you've thought about people.

19How do you make enterprise data a strategic asset across ERP, HCM and SCM?DataBasic

Why they ask: data strategy.

Answer:

"A data strategy owned by the business. A unified data platform, Fusion Data Intelligence plus an enterprise lake, with governed models. Master data management across domains. Data quality and lineage. Self-service analytics with governance. And AI readiness: clean, accessible and secure data."

Tip: Tie it to AI readiness. It's why data is getting executive attention.

20How do you prepare enterprise applications to support frequent acquisitions?M&AIn-depth

Why they ask: M&A readiness.

Answer:

"An M&A playbook with a due diligence checklist, day-one requirements and integration options. Template-based onboarding for entities, data and integrations. A dedicated integration team or partner. Reusable assets and standard timelines, like 90 or 180 days. Synergy tracking with finance. And lessons learned after each deal."

Tip: Mention standard timelines. It gives deal teams something to plan around.

21How do you measure the success of a multi-year transformation?TransformationBasic

Why they ask: results at scale.

Answer:

"Business outcomes: cost reduction, working capital, close speed and service levels. Platform outcomes: legacy retired, stability and technical debt. Adoption and satisfaction. Program health: budget, schedule and scope. And benefits realization against the business case, validated by finance."

Tip: Say "validated by finance". It makes the benefits credible.

22Tell me about a time you had to make a tough call that was unpopular.BehavioralBasic

Why they ask: leadership courage.

Answer (example):

"I stopped a regional rebuild of a custom billing system in favor of the global template. The reasons were cost, risk and strategic alignment. I explained the reasoning, listened to the region's concerns, offered support and alternatives, and made sure the executives were aligned. Over time it saved significant cost and standardized billing, and the relationship recovered because I stayed engaged with the region's needs."

Tip: Show how you kept the relationship. Tough calls are easier when trust survives.

23How do you handle a peer executive who undermines your program?BehavioralBasic

Why they ask: political skill.

Answer:

"I meet them privately to understand their concerns and incentives. I look for common goals and involve them in decisions. I use data and transparency to clear up misconceptions. If needed, I escalate constructively through the sponsor, with facts. And I stay professional and focused on outcomes."

Tip: Start private. Public confrontation makes it worse.

24How do you decide which capabilities should be standardized globally and which can differ locally?StrategyBasic

Why they ask: template governance at scale.

Answer:

"I standardize where consistency drives value: core financial processes, controls, master data, the chart of accounts, reporting and integration patterns, for scale, compliance and comparability. I allow local differences for statutory and tax requirements, language, local banking and genuine market differences. Governance decides the gray areas with business cases. And I revisit as regulations and the business change."

Tip: Say why you standardize: scale, compliance, comparability.

25How would you evaluate whether to consolidate onto a single ERP vendor across the enterprise?StrategyBasic

Why they ask: a portfolio decision.

Answer:

"I look at the current landscape's costs, capabilities and integration complexity. Business needs by division, since some may need specialized systems. The benefits of consolidating: lower cost, simpler data, unified processes and negotiating power. The risks: vendor lock-in, migration cost and disruption. And a phased approach with a business case."

Tip: Acknowledge divisions that may need specialized systems. It shows balance.

26How do you communicate a major program delay to the CFO and CEO?ExecutiveBasic

Why they ask: delivering bad news at the top.

Answer:

"Early, before they hear rumors. With facts: what happened, the root causes, and the impact on the business and costs. With options, trade-offs and my recommendation. With a recovery plan, milestones and leading indicators. Taking ownership, with no blame-shifting. And with regular follow-up updates."

Tip: Say "before rumors". Executives forgive delays more than surprises.

27How do you get executive sponsorship for a transformation?ExecutiveBasic

Why they ask: sponsorship is a critical success factor.

Answer:

"I build a business case tied to their priorities: growth, margin and risk. I involve them early in shaping scope. I define the sponsor role clearly: decisions, communications and removing blockers. I give regular, concise updates and ask for specific help. And I share wins publicly."

Tip: Mention asking for specific help. Sponsors engage when they have a job to do.

28How do you fund enterprise applications in a product model?Operating ModelIn-depth

Why they ask: funding models shape behavior.

Answer:

"Capacity-based funding for persistent product teams covering run and evolve. Investment cases for larger initiatives on top. Quarterly business reviews to rebalance capacity across products by value. Transparent cost allocation to business units, through showback or chargeback. And outcome metrics per product."

Tip: Mention quarterly rebalancing. It keeps capacity flowing to value.

29What role should business process owners play?Operating ModelBasic

Why they ask: a common failure point.

Answer:

"They're accountable for process design, policy, KPIs and adoption. They have decision rights on process changes and priorities. They partner with product owners in IT. They take part in testing and change management. Without strong process owners, ERP becomes an IT project, which is one of the most common reasons for failure."

Tip: End with the failure point. It shows why it matters.

30How do you handle a strategic SI that is underperforming on a major program?VendorsIn-depth

Why they ask: vendor escalation.

Answer:

"I escalate with data at the executive level, to the partner's leadership. I require a remediation plan: team changes, added experts, and a revised plan with commitments. I use contract levers: holdbacks, penalties and milestone payments. I prepare a contingency: other partners or in-house capability. And if remediation fails, I decide quickly, because sunk cost shouldn't drive the decision."

Tip: Say "sunk cost shouldn't drive decisions". It shows discipline.

31How would you reduce total cost of ownership of the application portfolio?FinanceIn-depth

Why they ask: cost leadership.

Answer:

"Application rationalization using a model like TIME: tolerate, invest, migrate or eliminate. License optimization and vendor consolidation. Moving legacy on-premise apps to cloud. Automation of support and testing. Location strategy for support. And demand management to avoid low-value work."

Tip: Name the TIME model. It shows a structured approach.

32How do you manage a portfolio of 20 concurrent projects?DeliveryBasic

Why they ask: portfolio management.

Answer:

"Portfolio governance with intake, prioritization and stage gates. Capacity planning across shared resources. Dependency management and an integrated release calendar. A portfolio dashboard of status, risks and value. The willingness to stop low-value or failing projects. And PMO standards without bureaucracy."

Tip: Mention stopping projects. Good portfolios kill things.

33How do you ensure quality across many teams and partners?DeliveryBasic

Why they ask: quality at scale.

Answer:

"Standards for design, testing, security and documentation. Quality gates in delivery: design reviews, test exit criteria and the change board. A shared automated testing platform. Metrics like defects reaching production and change failure rate. And retrospectives with learning shared across teams."

Tip: Mention a shared testing platform. It raises quality everywhere at once.

34How do you handle a leader on your team who delivers results but has poor people practices?PeopleBasic

Why they ask: values-based leadership.

Answer:

"I give feedback with examples and the team impact, like attrition and engagement. I'm clear that how results are achieved matters. I offer coaching and 360 feedback. If nothing changes, there are consequences, reassignment or exit. Because how I handle it signals to the whole organization what's valued."

Tip: Say the last line. It shows you understand leadership signaling.

35How do you ensure diversity and inclusion in your organization?PeopleBasic

Why they ask: inclusive leadership.

Answer:

"Diverse hiring slates and structured interviews. An inclusive culture where everyone is heard in meetings, with flexible work. Fair processes for promotion and pay, with audits for gaps. Mentoring and sponsorship programs. And measuring representation and engagement by group."

Tip: Mention auditing pay and promotion. It shows commitment beyond words.

36How do you manage regulatory change affecting enterprise systems (e.g. e-invoicing, privacy)?RiskBasic

Why they ask: compliance foresight.

Answer:

"A regulatory watch with tax, legal and compliance. Impact assessments on systems and processes. Placing each change on the roadmap with its deadline. Using vendor localizations where available. Testing and compliance evidence. And a business leader who owns each regulation."

Tip: Mention a business owner per regulation. IT can't own compliance alone.

37How do you address workforce concerns about AI replacing jobs in finance and operations?AIIn-depth

Why they ask: a people leadership question.

Answer:

"I'd be open about the goal: augment teams and move capacity to higher-value work. I'd involve the teams in designing the use cases, because they know the work best. I'd offer reskilling in analysis, exception handling and supervising AI. I'd measure and share the results honestly. And I'd work with HR on workforce planning."

Tip: Say "involve the teams in design". It builds trust and better solutions.

38What risks do you see with AI in enterprise applications and how do you mitigate them?AIIn-depth

Why they ask: a balanced risk question.

Answer:

"Accuracy and made-up answers: human review, grounding in enterprise data and testing. Security and privacy: data access controls, no leakage of sensitive data, and vendor assessments. Compliance and auditability: logging and explainability. Bias in HR use cases: testing and oversight. Over-reliance: process controls that still check the work. And cost: usage monitoring."

Tip: Pair each risk with its mitigation. It shows structured thinking.

39How do you approach data privacy across ERP and HCM globally?DataBasic

Why they ask: privacy is a growing risk.

Answer:

"Privacy by design in processes and integrations. A data inventory of what personal data we hold, where and why. Retention and deletion policies implemented in the systems. Data residency requirements. Access controls and monitoring. And DPIAs for new processes, working with the privacy office."

Tip: Mention implementing retention in the systems. Policies on paper don't delete data.

40How do you sustain benefits after a transformation program ends?TransformationBasic

Why they ask: long-term value.

Answer:

"Process ownership and KPIs embedded in how the business is managed. Continuous improvement teams. Governance to prevent customization creep. Training for new hires. And benefit tracking for one to two years after the program."

Tip: Mention customization creep. It quietly undoes transformations.

41What is the most strategic decision you've made in your career?BehavioralBasic

Why they ask: strategic thinking.

Answer (structure, use your own):

"Pick one with enterprise impact, like moving to a product operating model, consolidating ERPs or choosing a cloud platform. Explain the context, the options, your reasoning, how you aligned stakeholders, how it was executed, the results, and what you'd do differently."

Tip: Include what you'd do differently. It shows reflection.

42How do you stay strategic while operational fires demand attention?BehavioralBasic

Why they ask: time management at senior level.

Answer:

"I build strong leaders below me who own operations. I set escalation rules so only true P1s come to me. I protect strategy time on my calendar. I use metrics and alerts to know when to engage. And I treat incidents as signals for strategic fixes."

Tip: Mention treating incidents as signals. It connects fires to strategy.

43Describe how you led through a major crisis.BehavioralBasic

Why they ask: crisis leadership.

Answer (structure):

"Describe the situation, for example a failed go-live affecting shipments or payroll. Then the actions: a command structure, prioritization, a communication rhythm, business workarounds and support for the team. Then the outcome and the lasting changes. Throughout, show calm, decisiveness and care for people."

Tip: Show care for the team. Crisis leadership is judged on it.

44Where is enterprise applications heading and how should we prepare?StrategyBasic

Why they ask: vision.

Answer:

"AI agents taking over routine transactions while people manage exceptions and judgment. Composable platforms: SaaS cores with low-code extensions and an integration fabric. Real-time data and continuous close. Product operating models. And rising security and regulatory complexity. To prepare: clean data, standard processes, new skills, governance for AI, and flexible funding."

Tip: Tie each trend to a preparation step. Vision needs action.

45How do you balance the interests of IT (CIO) and business (CFO/COO) when you report to one of them?ExecutiveBasic

Why they ask: enterprise perspective.

Answer:

"I act as an enterprise leader, not a partisan. I build shared goals and joint governance. I'm transparent with both, with no surprises. I escalate genuine conflicts to the right forum. And I build trust through delivery."

Tip: Say "enterprise leader, not partisan". It's the key mindset.

46How do you build the case to invest in technical debt reduction to executives?FinanceIn-depth

Why they ask: debt is hard to fund.

Answer:

"I translate debt into business risk and cost: outages, slow delivery, audit issues and the inability to adopt new features like AI. I quantify it: incident costs, support costs and delayed value. I present a plan with milestones and measurable outcomes. And I bundle it with value initiatives where possible."

Tip: Mention the inability to adopt AI. It makes debt a strategic issue.

47Why this Senior Director role, and why now?BehavioralBasic

Why they ask: motivation and fit.

Answer (tailor it):

"My experience matches their scale and challenges. I've delivered results at director level: transformations, teams and value. I'm ready for an enterprise portfolio and executive partnership. And I'm motivated by their strategy, transformation stage and culture, which I'd show through research on their business."

Tip: Do real research. It's obvious when candidates haven't.

48How would you set up a Center of Excellence for Oracle across the enterprise?Operating ModelBasic

Why they ask: enterprise CoE design.

Answer:

"A charter covering standards, architecture, release management, knowledge, vendor management and innovation. A structure with platform owners, architects, process experts, release and test automation, and data and integration. Services offered to business units and project teams. Metrics: adoption of standards, stability, delivery speed and cost. And a funding model."

Tip: Mention services to business units. A CoE should serve, not just govern.

49How would you approach a phased global rollout schedule to minimize business risk?DeliveryIn-depth

Why they ask: rollout planning.

Answer:

"A pilot in a representative but manageable region. Avoiding fiscal year-end, peak seasons and audits. Waves sized by capacity and complexity. Stabilization periods between waves. Lessons learned after each wave. And business readiness criteria for each country."

Tip: Mention stabilization between waves. Skipping it compounds problems.

50How do you evaluate whether a program is still worth continuing after major overruns?FinanceIn-depth

Why they ask: hard decisions.

Answer:

"I ignore sunk costs and compare remaining cost to remaining benefits. I re-validate the benefits with business owners. I lay out the options: continue as is, re-scope, pause or stop. I assess the risk of each, operational, financial and reputational. And I decide with the sponsor and steering committee and communicate the reasoning clearly."

Tip: Lead with "ignore sunk costs". It's the core principle.

51How would you scale AI agents across ERP, HCM and SCM portfolios?AI Agent StudioIn-depth

Why they ask: a scaling and operating model question.

Answer:

"I'd set up an enterprise agent operating model: intake, risk tiers, standards, evaluation, monitoring and ownership. Platform choices: AI Agent Studio for Fusion-centered agents, an enterprise AI platform for cross-domain ones, connected through A2A and MCP. A value portfolio validated by finance. A workforce plan to reskill teams in finance, procurement and HR operations. And regular reporting to the board and audit on AI risk and value."

Tip: Mention finance validating the value. It keeps the numbers honest.

52How do you think about the workforce impact of agents in finance and procurement operations?AI Agent StudioIn-depth

Why they ask: leaders need a clear view on people impact.

Answer:

"Agents take over lookups, routine drafting and triage, so people focus on exceptions, judgment, and supplier and customer relationships. I'd plan how freed capacity is redeployed with HR, not just treat it as cuts. Reskilling covers exception handling, data analysis and supervising agents. And open communication keeps trust."

Tip: Say "redeploy, not just cut". It's the message that keeps teams engaged.

53Why does Redwood matter strategically, beyond new screens?RedwoodBasic

Why they ask: to see if you think strategically about the platform.

Answer:

"Redwood is the foundation for Oracle's AI features, and new capabilities ship on Redwood pages first. Classic pages are being retired, so it's mandatory, and delaying adds risk and cost. It's also a chance to simplify customizations and improve user productivity. So I'd plan it as part of the platform roadmap, not as IT housekeeping."

Tip: Lead with "AI ships on Redwood first". It changes how executives see it.

54How would you engage Oracle and partners on AI Agent Studio?VendorsIn-depth

Why they ask: AI vendor strategy.

Answer:

"With Oracle: roadmap briefings, early adopter programs, and support for delivered agents. With partners: validated marketplace agents and implementation help for custom agents. In contracts: clarity on premium LLM costs and how data is used. And we keep internal ownership of agent design and governance."

Tip: Mention clarity on data use. It's a board-level concern.

55What would a 2-year "AI-enabled ERP" roadmap look like?StrategyIn-depth

Why they ask: AI roadmap at senior level.

Answer:

"Year one: Redwood adoption, data cleanup, delivered agents, three to five pilots, and governance live. Year two: scale the proven agents, automate workflows across AP, close, procurement and order management, add advanced analytics with Fusion Data Intelligence, and measure productivity. Throughout: quarterly release adoption, value tracking and risk reviews."

Tip: Put governance in year one. It has to come before scale.
