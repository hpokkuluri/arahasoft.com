Skip-Level Meeting with a Senior Director Interview Questions

How to handle a skip-level meeting with a senior director: team health, ideas, risks and your career. Each question has why it comes up, a sample answer and a tip. The sample answers use an example Senior ERP Analyst; swap in your own projects, wins and goals. Tap a question to see the answer.

50 questions 31 basic 19 in-depth
1Tell me about yourself and what you do.IntroBasic

Why they ask: they may not see your work day to day.

Sample answer:

"I'm Senior ERP Analyst on the ERP applications team. I'm accountable for the finance applications and the integrations that feed them. Right now my biggest focus is the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project. A recent result I'm proud of is cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule, which directly supports a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance."

Tip: 45–60 seconds, outcome first.

2How is the team doing?Team HealthBasic

Why they ask: skip-levels exist to hear what doesn't come through the normal chain.

Sample answer:

"The team is strong and committed. We've delivered a lot this year while keeping operations stable. The main strain is capacity — a tight go-live date and one open role on the team. What would help most is backup coverage for our critical areas and clearer prioritization when new requests arrive. I've started on both."

Tip: Describe patterns and fixes, not individuals.

3What's working well, and what isn't?Team HealthIn-depth

Why they ask: looking for honest signal.

Sample answer:

"Working well: our weekly prioritization with the business — it's cut down surprise escalations a lot. Not working as well: urgent support work and project work compete for the same people, so projects get interrupted. Separating the two more clearly, with routine work going to our support partner, would help us deliver faster."

Tip: If something involves your manager, raise it with your manager first.

4How is it working with your manager?ManagerIn-depth

Why they ask: a normal check — and sometimes a probe.

Sample answer:

"Good. My manager gives me real ownership and supports me when I need decisions or escalations. One thing I'd like more of is exposure to senior stakeholders, and I've mentioned that to them as well."

Tip: Positive and brief. Never share what they told you privately.

5Do you understand where the organization is heading?StrategyIn-depth

Why they ask: checking alignment.

Sample answer:

"My understanding is that our priorities are a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. My work on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project supports that by making our systems reliable and ready for growth. One thing I'd value your view on: how do you see our function evolving over the next year or two?"

6If you were in my seat, what would you change?IdeasIn-depth

Why they ask: testing leadership thinking.

Sample answer:

"I'd create a standard playbook for big changes — new business units, upgrades, major releases — covering design, testing, cutover and support. Each one would go faster and with less risk, and it frees senior people to work on improvements. I'd be happy to draft it."

7What risks do you see that I might not hear about?RisksIn-depth

Why they ask: early warning.

Sample answer:

"Two. First, key-person risk — a few critical areas depend on one or two people; I'm addressing it with documentation and cross-training. Second, a tight go-live date and one open role on the team, which could affect our dates if not managed. I have a plan for both and will flag early if anything changes."

Tip: Facts and plans, not gossip.

8What are your career goals?CareerIn-depth

Why they ask: retention and development.

Sample answer:

"My goal is leading a larger team within the next year. My scope has already grown well beyond my original role, and I'd like a clear path to formalize the next step. What would you want to see from someone ready for that level here?"

Tip: Ask for criteria — it turns ambition into a plan they can support.

9Are you happy here?CareerBasic

Why they ask: retention check.

Sample answer:

"Yes — I like the work and the team, and I want to grow here. The one thing I'd like is clarity on my next step, because I've been working toward it for a while."

Tip: Positive, honest, no ultimatum.

10What would help you do your best work?SupportBasic

Why they ask: genuinely looking for blockers they can remove.

Sample answer:

"Two things. Faster decisions from the business on scope and priorities — delays there push our dates. And visibility: a chance to present results directly to leadership, so the team's work is recognized."

11Who on the team deserves recognition?RecognitionBasic

Why they ask: looking for hidden stars — and how you lift others.

Sample answer:

"Several people stepped up on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project. In particular, one colleague took on difficult testing and cutover work over weekends and it made the difference for go-live. I'd love for that to be recognized."

Tip: Use real names and specifics.

12Do people feel comfortable raising concerns?CultureIn-depth

Why they ask: testing psychological safety.

Sample answer:

"Mostly, yes. Where it's harder is when priorities change late — people hesitate to push back. A short regular retrospective would help people raise issues earlier."

13What should I ask in a skip-level?Your questionsBasic
  • "What are the organization's top priorities this year?"
  • "What do you wish our team did more of?"
  • "What skills will matter most here in the next two years?"
  • "What would you want to see from someone ready for the next level?"
14How should I prepare for a skip-level?EtiquetteBasic
  • Three key updates with numbers, one risk with a plan, one idea.
  • Tell your manager it's happening — no surprises.
  • Be positive and solution-focused; never criticize colleagues.
  • Follow up with a short thank-you and anything you promised.
15How is morale on the team?Team HealthBasic

Why they ask: skip-levels are an early warning system for leaders.

Sample answer:

"Morale is generally good — people are proud of cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. The pressure point is workload during the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project. Recognition from leadership when we hit the next milestone would go a long way."

Tip: Be honest and constructive, never personal.

16Is anyone at risk of leaving?Team HealthIn-depth

Why they ask: retention of key people.

Sample answer:

"I don't want to speak for individuals, but in general people want clear growth paths and fair workload. Investing in development and backup coverage would reduce that risk."

Tip: Protect confidences; talk about themes.

17How is your manager doing?ManagerIn-depth

Why they ask: a direct check on leadership — handle it carefully.

Sample answer:

"my manager has been very supportive — especially in shielding the team during escalations and backing our priorities. One thing that would help us even more is earlier visibility of leadership priorities, which I think depends on information flowing down to both of us."

Tip: Lead with genuine positives. Frame suggestions as system improvements, not criticism.

18What could your manager do better?ManagerIn-depth

Why they ask: they may be gathering feedback — or testing your loyalty.

Sample answer:

"I'd frame it as what would help the team: clearer prioritization across competing requests. That's something my manager and I are already working on together."

Tip: Never undermine your manager. Show partnership.

19What's working well?WorkingBasic

Why they ask: to reinforce good practices.

Sample answer:

"Our collaboration with the business on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project — regular reviews and quick decisions have kept us on track. Also, our incident response has improved; recurring issues are down."

Tip: Specific examples with outcomes.

20What's not working?Not WorkingBasic

Why they ask: they want problems surfaced early.

Sample answer:

"Request intake across teams. Urgent requests arrive through many channels and disrupt planned work. A single intake and a monthly priority review with business leaders would make delivery more predictable."

Tip: One systemic issue with a solution.

21If you were in my role, what would you change?IdeasIn-depth

Why they ask: strategic thinking.

Sample answer:

"I'd invest in automation of routine operations and cross-training, so the team spends more time on a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance and less on manual work."

Tip: Business-level ideas.

22Do you understand our strategy?StrategyBasic

Why they ask: alignment check.

Sample answer:

"My understanding is we're focused on a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. Our work on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project supports that directly. Is there anything changing that I should prepare the team for?"

Tip: Restate it, connect your work, ask a question.

23How do you see your growth here?CareerBasic

Why they ask: talent planning at the next level up.

Sample answer:

"I want to grow toward leading a larger team within the next year. my manager and I have a development plan. From your perspective, what experiences would make someone ready at that level?"

Tip: Show alignment with your manager and seek advice.

24How can I mention promotion in a skip-level?CareerIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) delicate.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I'm working toward the next level with my manager. What would you want to see from someone ready for that role?"

Tip: Ask for criteria, don't lobby. Tell your manager afterward.

25What are you working on that I should know about?VisibilityBasic

Why they ask: visibility into impact.

Sample answer:

"the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project, which supports a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. A recent milestone was cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. The next phase lands next quarter and will reduce manual effort for the business."

Tip: Business outcomes first.

26What risks keep you up at night?RisksIn-depth

Why they ask: risk awareness.

Sample answer:

"Single points of knowledge on critical systems, and the timing of a tight go-live date and one open role on the team. We're addressing both with cross-training and phased plans."

Tip: Risks with mitigation.

27What are our business customers saying?CustomersBasic

Why they ask: voice of the customer.

Sample answer:

"They appreciate responsiveness and the results on cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. Their main request is more predictability on when changes will land."

Tip: Balanced.

28What can I do to help your team?SupportBasic

Why they ask: senior leaders can remove bigger obstacles.

Sample answer:

"Help align business leaders on priorities at your level, so conflicting urgent requests get resolved earlier. And recognition for the team after cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule would mean a lot."

Tip: Ask for things only they can do.

29How would you describe our culture?CultureIn-depth

Why they ask: cultural insight.

Sample answer:

"Collaborative and committed — people go above and beyond during critical moments. The opportunity is better planning so heroics aren't needed as often."

Tip: Honest and constructive.

30Where do you see waste in how we work?ProcessBasic

Why they ask: improvement ideas.

Sample answer:

"Duplicate status reporting and manual data pulls. A shared dashboard could save several hours per person per week."

Tip: Quantify.

31How is collaboration with other teams?Cross-teamBasic

Why they ask: organizational friction.

Sample answer:

"Good with most teams. With one partner team, priorities sometimes conflict. Clear escalation rules and a shared roadmap would help."

Tip: Systemic, not personal.

32What should I bring to a skip-level?PreparationBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) preparation shows respect.

Sample answer (checklist):

  • Three wins with business impact.
  • One improvement idea with a solution.
  • One question about strategy.
  • Your career goal (briefly).
  • Tell your manager it's happening and share key points afterward.

Tip: Never use it to complain about your manager.

33Are you planning to leave?LoyaltyIn-depth

Why they ask: retention risk, sometimes asked directly.

Sample answer:

"No — I'm committed to the work and the team. Like anyone, I want to keep growing, and I'm working with my manager on a path toward leading a larger team within the next year."

Tip: Reassure, then link to growth.

34What feedback do you have for leadership?FeedbackBasic

Why they ask: upward feedback.

Sample answer:

"Town halls are helpful. More context on why priorities change would help teams adapt faster and feel included."

Tip: Respectful and specific.

35Who on your team deserves recognition?RecognitionBasic

Why they ask: leaders want to know stars.

Sample answer:

"A team member led the testing that made cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule possible. A note from you would be very motivating."

Tip: Name names generously.

36How is the team handling recent changes?ChangeBasic

Why they ask: change readiness.

Sample answer:

"People understand the direction but want clarity on roles and timelines. Regular updates from leadership would reduce uncertainty."

Tip: Constructive.

37Will what I say get back to my manager?ConfidentialityIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) understanding norms.

Sample answer (approach):

Assume themes may be shared. Speak in a way you'd be comfortable with your manager hearing.

Tip: No surprises for your manager.

38What should I do after the skip-level?AfterBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) close the loop.

Sample answer (approach):

Send a short thank-you note with any follow-ups, and brief your manager on topics discussed.

Tip: Transparency with your manager.

39What trends do you see affecting our area?IndustryIn-depth

Why they ask: broader perspective.

Sample answer:

"AI-assisted operations, cloud platform changes and tighter security expectations. We should build skills now so we're ready to support a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance."

Tip: Connect trends to action.

40What are you most proud of?WinsBasic

Why they ask: impact and values.

Sample answer:

"cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule — especially how the team pulled together and how we kept the business informed throughout."

Tip: Credit the team.

41What skills are you developing?GrowthBasic

Why they ask: development mindset.

Sample answer:

"Executive communication and strategic planning, plus skills related to a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. I'm taking on a cross-team initiative to practise."

Tip: Show action.

42What should I ask a senior director?QuestionsBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) good questions build rapport.

Sample answer (questions):

  • "What are your top priorities for the next year?"
  • "What would make our team even more valuable to you?"
  • "What do you see as our biggest risk?"
  • "What advice would you give someone growing toward leading a larger team within the next year?"

Tip: Strategic and curious.

43What if I disagree with a decision from above?ConflictsIn-depth

Why they ask: maturity.

Sample answer:

"I'd share concerns with data through the right channel, and once a decision is made, I'd support it fully with my team."

Tip: Disagree and commit.

44How formal should a skip-level be?ToneBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) calibration.

Sample answer (approach):

Professional but conversational: prepared, concise, and open. Treat it as a relationship-building conversation.

Tip: Prepared, not scripted.

45Does your team have what it needs?ResourcesBasic

Why they ask: resource planning.

Sample answer:

"Mostly. Our gap is backup coverage on critical systems and capacity during peaks like the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project."

Tip: Specific gaps.

46What motivates you?EngagementBasic

Why they ask: retention and fit.

Sample answer:

"Solving meaningful business problems, seeing the impact of our work, and growing people on the team."

Tip: Genuine.

47How do you keep the team connected?RemoteBasic

Why they ask: team cohesion.

Sample answer:

"Regular stand-ups, a weekly win-sharing slot and occasional in-person sessions. Recognition keeps energy up."

Tip: Concrete practices.

48What's something leadership doesn't want to hear?Hard TruthIn-depth

Why they ask: candor test.

Sample answer:

"That we're relying on heroics during peak periods. It works, but it isn't sustainable — investing in automation and coverage would reduce risk."

Tip: Honest, solution-oriented.

49Anything else on your mind?ClosingBasic

Why they ask: a final open door.

Sample answer:

"Just thank you for the time. I'm excited about the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project and happy to help with anything that supports a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance."

Tip: End positively.

50How do I show I acted on their advice?Follow-throughBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) follow-through builds credibility.

Sample answer (approach):

At the next interaction, reference their advice and what you did: "You suggested X; I've done Y, and here's the result."

Tip: Closes the loop.
