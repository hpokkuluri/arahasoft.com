1:1 with a VP Interview Questions

How to handle a 1:1 with a VP: business impact, strategy, risks and visibility. Each question has why it comes up, a sample answer and a tip. The sample answers use an example Senior ERP Analyst; swap in your own projects, wins and goals. Tap a question to see the answer.

HP
Questions and answers by Hari Pokkuluri

Senior Oracle ERP Solution Architect with 22+ years in Oracle Fusion Cloud and E-Business Suite: Financials, SCM, HCM and Procurement. Oracle-certified in R11/R12 Financials and OCI AI Foundations. Arahasoft.

1So, how are things going?OpeningBasicTop question

Why they ask: an open door. What you choose to say first shows what you think matters.

Sample answer:

"Things are busy but in good shape. As Senior ERP Analyst, my focus is the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project. Day-to-day operations are stable, and the biggest recent result is cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. The one thing I'm watching closely is a tight go-live date and one open role on the team — I'll walk you through how I'm handling it."

Tip: Lead with business outcomes in the first 30 seconds. Never open with a complaint.

Likely follow-up

How do I avoid a vague "fine"?

Give one specific win and one thing you're working on.

2Remind me what you own today.OpeningBasicTop question

Why they ask: VPs see many people; they want your scope in one breath.

Sample answer:

"I'm Senior ERP Analyst on the ERP applications team. I own the finance applications and the integrations that feed them. In practice that means I'm the person accountable when the business needs something from these systems — whether it's an outage, a change or a new initiative like the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project."

Tip: Describe accountability, not tasks.

Likely follow-up

How do I describe scope crisply?

In one sentence with a number, like systems, users or spend.

3With everything on your plate, how is the workload?WorkloadBasic

Why they ask: to check burnout and whether delivery is at risk. "I'm overwhelmed" signals you can't handle scope; "everything's fine" sounds unaware.

Sample answer:

"It's a full plate, and it's manageable because I've put structure around it: a fixed weekly triage of incoming work, clear priority rules agreed with the business, and a release calendar so changes don't collide. The real pressure point is timing — a tight go-live date and one open role on the team. I've already planned for it, and if priorities shift I'll come to you with options rather than surprises."

Tip: Show control plus one honest risk with a plan.

Likely follow-up

How honest should I be about workload?

Honest, framed as how to scale, not that you're overwhelmed.

4Is the team stretched too thin?WorkloadIn-depth

Why they ask: retention and delivery risk.

Sample answer:

"We're stretched, but not breaking. The team has absorbed a lot — the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project on top of normal support. What would make it sustainable is backup coverage for our most critical areas and moving routine work to our support partner, so the core team can focus on the projects that matter most for a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. I'm working on both."

Tip: Name the fix, not just the strain.

Likely follow-up

What if the team is stretched?

Give evidence and options, like prioritizing or adding help.

5What business difference has your work made this year?ImpactBasicTop question

Why they ask: to understand your value in a leader's language — money, risk, speed, customers.

Sample answer:

"Three things. First, cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. Second, operations stayed stable through every major change this year, which matters most during close and peak periods. Third, the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project is setting us up for a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. In short: fewer disruptions for the business, faster delivery of what they ask for, and lower risk."

Tip: Bring one or two real numbers (tickets, SLA %, days saved, dollars).

Likely follow-up

What makes impact credible?

A number tied to business outcomes.

6Where are we on your biggest initiative?StatusBasicTop question

Why they ask: quick health check; it may be reported upward.

Sample answer:

"Overall it's on track. The design and build work is done, testing is underway, and we're holding the go-live date. The main risk is a tight go-live date and one open role on the team; we've mitigated it with extra testing cycles and a clear cutover plan. The only thing I'd ask from you is help keeping business decisions on schedule — if decisions slip, the date slips."

Tip: Status, risk with mitigation, one clear ask. Under 45 seconds.

Likely follow-up

What if it's behind?

Say so, with the plan.

7Is our support vendor / managed services partner giving us value?VendorIn-depth

Why they ask: vendors are a big cost line; the VP wants a candid owner's view.

Sample answer:

"Better than six months ago. We now review open items with them weekly, track response and resolution against SLA, and escalate anything aging. Resolution times have improved. Where I'm still pushing is first-time fix quality and getting them to own more of the routine work end to end, so our internal team spends its time on projects. If that doesn't improve by next quarter, I'd recommend we revisit the scope in the renewal."

Tip: Owners talk about value and accountability, not just ticket counts.

Likely follow-up

How do I assess a vendor fairly?

Use SLAs and outcomes, not opinions.

8What keeps you up at night?RisksIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: to see if you think like a leader — ahead of problems.

Sample answer:

"Two things. First, a tight go-live date and one open role on the team — my plan is to protect the critical dates with extra coverage and early testing. Second, key-person risk: a few areas depend on one or two people. I'm addressing it with documentation and cross-training over the next quarter. Neither needs action from you today, but I want you to know they're on my radar."

Tip: A risk with a plan shows maturity; a risk without one sounds like worry.

Likely follow-up

How do I answer without alarming?

Name the risk and the mitigation.

9How is the team's morale?TeamIn-depth

Why they ask: especially after someone else raised concerns — the VP is checking whether it's widespread.

Sample answer:

"The team is committed and proud of what they've delivered. People are busy, and what keeps morale up is seeing a clear path — clear priorities, recognition and growth. Recognizing the team's effort on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project would go a long way."

Tip: Speak for the work, not for other people's private feelings.

Likely follow-up

What if morale is low?

Share it with causes and ideas.

10How is it working with your manager?ManagerIn-depth

Why they ask: the VP may be checking on your manager, especially after a recent difficult conversation.

Sample answer:

"It works well. My manager gives me ownership, trusts me with the business directly, and backs me when I escalate. We're aligned on priorities."

Tip: Keep it short and positive. Never repeat what your manager told you privately, and don't comment on their career plans — that's their conversation to have.

Likely follow-up

How do I answer carefully?

Positive and specific; never criticize my manager to a VP.

11How do you feel about your role? Are you happy here?CareerIn-depth

Why they ask: retention check. This is your best opening to raise your career goal calmly.

Sample answer:

"I like the work and I want to grow here, so I'll be open with you. My goal is leading a larger team within the next year. Over the last couple of years my scope has grown well beyond the role I was hired into — today I'm accountable for the finance applications and the integrations that feed them, plus the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project. I'm effectively operating at the next level, and I'd like to make it official. What would you need to see from me, and what timeline is realistic?"

Tip: Ask for criteria and a timeline, not a yes or no. Calm and factual — no frustration, no comparisons with others.

Likely follow-up

What if I'm not happy?

Focus on what would make it better.

12Are you thinking about leaving?CareerIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a direct retention check — especially if someone else recently signaled frustration.

Sample answer:

"No, I'm not looking. I want to build my career here — that's exactly why I'm raising my growth path with you. I'd like a clear plan so that we both know what the next step looks like."

Tip: Say it once, calmly, then stop. No hints, no ultimatums. Threats only work if you are ready to leave tomorrow.

Likely follow-up

How do I answer if I'm looking?

Focus on what keeps you engaged.

13What would the next level look like for you?CareerIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: to see whether you understand the bigger job, not just a bigger title.

Sample answer:

"Owning the outcome for the whole function, not just the work in front of me: setting priorities with business leaders, managing the vendor relationship as a strategic partner, building a repeatable approach for big changes like the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project, and developing people so the team doesn't depend on me. I already do parts of this; the next level is doing all of it, formally, with the authority to match."

Likely follow-up

How do I talk about the next level?

Name the scope you want and ask what it takes.

14Where should we be investing next year?StrategyIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: testing whether you think beyond your queue.

Sample answer:

"Three places. First, a repeatable playbook for big changes — onboarding new business units or upgrades — so each one is faster and cheaper than the last. Second, automation and AI for routine support, so skilled people spend time on improvements. Third, reporting the business can serve itself, which removes a whole category of requests. Each supports a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance."

Likely follow-up

What makes investment ideas credible?

A business case with payoff.

15If you were in my seat, what would you change?IdeasIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a chance to show leadership thinking — and a test of judgment.

Sample answer:

"I'd be clearer about where routine work goes versus project work. Today the same people do both, so urgent tickets keep interrupting strategic projects. Splitting the two — routine work to our support partner with clear SLAs, a small core team protected for projects — would speed up delivery without adding headcount. I'd be glad to put a short proposal together."

Tip: One idea, its payoff, and an offer to own it.

Likely follow-up

How do I answer?

One focused improvement with impact.

16I'm not sure I agree with that.PushbackIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: VPs test conviction and composure.

Sample answer:

"That's fair — help me understand your concern. Is it cost, timing or risk? The data I'm relying on is our ticket trends and the project timeline. If the concern is cost, one option is to pilot it for a quarter and measure the result before committing. I'm happy to go with your call either way."

Tip: Curious, calm, data-based — and willing to be overruled.

Likely follow-up

What do I do when the VP disagrees?

Stay calm, explain my reasoning briefly and listen.

17Anything else you want to tell me?ClosingBasic

Why they ask: a last open door — use it.

Sample answer:

"Two quick things. Thank you for the time — it's valuable to hear your priorities directly. And to recap the career point: I'd appreciate your support for the next level, and I'll follow up with a short note on the criteria we discussed."

Likely follow-up

What's worth adding?

One idea or ask.

18What should I ask the VP?Your questionsBasicTop question

Why they ask: a VP remembers people who ask about priorities and growth with clear intent.

Answer:

Pick two:

"What are your top priorities for our area over the next year?"

"What gap do you see between where I am and the next level?"

"If I meet those criteria, would you support me in the next promotion cycle?"

"As we grow, how do you see this function being structured?"

Tip: Ask the promotion question only after you have shown the value you bring.

Likely follow-up

How many questions?

Two.

19How do I prepare for a VP skip-level?PrepBasicTop question

Why they ask: VPs have little time, so a tight, outcome-focused update earns trust.

Answer:

Write your three biggest updates with one number each. Add one risk with your plan and one idea with its payoff. Put your career ask into two sentences — the criteria and the timeline you are aiming for. Tell your manager the meeting is happening so nothing surprises them. Afterwards, send a short thank-you note recapping the key points and anything the VP asked for.

Tip: Numbers, one risk, one idea, one clear ask.

Likely follow-up

What's essential to prepare?

Three updates with numbers and a career ask.

20What should I avoid saying in a VP skip-level?AvoidBasicTop question

Why they ask: one wrong phrase can undo the impression you came to make.

Answer:

Avoid "my manager and I both feel overlooked" — it sounds like a group complaint. Avoid "if I don't get promoted I'll have to look elsewhere" — that's an ultimatum. Instead of "I'm overwhelmed," say "we need to structure this to scale." Never repeat anything your manager told you in confidence. And skip long technical detail — keep it at the level of business outcomes.

Tip: Frame every concern as a plan.

Likely follow-up

What's the biggest mistake?

Complaining or giving ultimatums.

Tip: Link your work to their top priorities explicitly.

Likely follow-up

How do I link work to strategy?

Likely follow-up

Why they ask: VP time is for decisions, not updates.

Tip: Bring one clear decision with a recommendation.

Likely follow-up

How do I ask for a decision?
