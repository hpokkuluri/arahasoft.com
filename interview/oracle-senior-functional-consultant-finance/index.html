Oracle Senior Functional Consultant – Finance Interview Questions

1Walk me through the phases of an Oracle Cloud implementation and your role in each.ImplementationBasic

Why they ask: senior consultants need to know the life cycle and their part in it.

Answer:

"Most projects follow Oracle Unified Method or a client's agile hybrid. Discovery and planning gathers requirements, current processes and scope. Design, or CRP1, configures a baseline, demos it to the business and runs fit-gap. Build, or CRP2, finishes configuration and builds extensions, reports, integrations and conversions. Testing covers SIT, UAT and parallel runs or reconciliations. Deploy covers mock conversions, cutover, go-live and hypercare. As a senior consultant I own the module design, for example AP and Payments: I write the functional design, configure, write test scripts, lead the CRP demos, train key users, and support cutover and hypercare."

Tip: Name what you own in each phase. That's the real question.

2What is a Conference Room Pilot (CRP) and how do you run a good one?ImplementationBasic

Why they ask: CRPs are where design is won or lost.

Answer:

"A CRP is a scripted demo of the configured system using the client's own data and processes, so the business can validate the design. I agree scenarios in advance, like 'PO-matched invoice with a price variance'. I use realistic data: their suppliers, accounts and amounts. I walk end to end, including accounting and reports, not just screens. I capture gaps and decisions live in a log with owners. And I follow up with updated designs and a sign-off. CRP1 validates the baseline; CRP2 validates the near-final solution with integrations."

Tip: Mention using the client's real data. It's what makes the business engage.

3How do you handle a requirement that Oracle cannot meet out of the box?ImplementationBasic

Why they ask: customization decisions affect cost and upgrades for years.

Answer:

"First I challenge the requirement: is it a real business need or a legacy habit? Could a process change solve it? Then I look for configuration: descriptive flexfields, SLA rules, approval rules, BIP reports or OTBI. If that's not enough, I look for a light extension: a Visual Builder page, an OIC integration, a BIP report, or a REST-based process. I record it in the RICEW inventory with its cost and support impact. And the business signs off on the trade-off."

Tip: Give an example where you avoided an extension by changing the process. Interviewers love that.

4How would you change the liability account on AP invoices based on supplier type using SLA?Subledger AccountingIn-depth

Why they ask: a hands-on SLA question. They want the exact steps.

Answer:

"In Manage Accounting Methods I copy the seeded Standard Accrual method; I never edit seeded. I create a mapping set with supplier type as input and natural account value as output. Then I create an account derivation rule for the liability that starts from the default account and overrides the natural account segment with the mapping set result. I copy the seeded journal entry rule set for the Invoices event class and attach the new rule to the Liability journal line. I activate the rule set and assign the new method to the ledger. Then I test invoice, payment, cancellation and prepayment application."

Tip: Keep the steps in order. It's a test of whether you've actually clicked through it.

5What are mapping sets, sources and supporting references in SLA?Subledger AccountingIn-depth

Why they ask: these three are the building blocks of SLA rules.

Answer:

"Sources are the transaction data SLA can use in rules, like supplier type, item category or project; custom sources can be built from attributes and descriptive flexfields. Mapping sets are lookup tables that turn an input value into a segment value or a full account, for example item category to natural account. Supporting references are extra attributes stored on journal lines, like supplier or invoice number, so you can report balances by them and reconcile. I find supporting references most useful for intercompany and tax account reconciliations."

Tip: Give one use for each. It keeps the answer practical.

6How do you configure journal approval in Fusion GL?General LedgerIn-depth

Why they ask: journal approval is a key financial control.

Answer:

"I enable journal approval on the ledger and on the journal sources that need it, like Manual and Spreadsheet. The rules are set in BPM through Manage Journal Approval Rules: by journal amount, for example over $50,000 goes to the controller; by ledger or category; and by supervisor hierarchy or approval groups. I test the edge cases: amounts exactly at the threshold, journals across ledgers, and self-approval prevention. Subledger journals usually don't need approval, because they're controlled upstream."

Tip: Mention testing at the threshold amount. It's the case that usually breaks.

7What is AutoPost and when would you use it?General LedgerIn-depth

Why they ask: AutoPost keeps GL current without manual effort.

Answer:

"AutoPost automatically posts journals that match criteria sets, by ledger, source, category, period and priority. I use it for subledger journals from Payables, Receivables and Assets, and for trusted integrations, so finance doesn't post them by hand. It runs on a schedule. I leave out sources that need review, like manual journals. Combined with Create Accounting's transfer and post options, GL stays current every day."

Tip: Say which sources you'd exclude. That's the control part of the answer.

8How do segment value security rules work?General LedgerIn-depth

Why they ask: security design affects users and performance.

Answer:

"Segment value security limits which values of a segment a user can see or use, for example a regional accountant can only use cost centers in their region. I enable security on the value set, create data security policies with the value conditions, and grant them through roles in the Security Console. It applies to entry, inquiry and reporting, and works alongside data access sets, which control ledger and balancing segment access. I'm careful with it, because too many rules slow things down and confuse users."

Tip: Mention the performance cost. It shows you've seen it go wrong.

9What is a data access set?General LedgerBasic

Why they ask: a basic GL security question.

Answer:

"A data access set controls which ledgers or ledger sets, and which primary balancing segment values, a user can access in GL, and whether it's read-only or read and write. It can give full ledger access or only specific values, like companies 101 to 110. It's assigned to users with their GL roles through Manage Data Access for Users. With a ledger set, one data access set can cover many ledgers that share a chart and calendar."

Tip: Give the example of a company range. It makes the answer concrete.

10How does Fusion Intercompany work?IntercompanyIn-depth

Why they ask: intercompany is complex, and Fusion has two parts that people confuse.

Answer:

"There are two parts. Intercompany balancing is automatic: when a journal crosses balancing values, GL or SLA creates due-to and due-from lines based on the intercompany balancing rules. Intercompany transactions use the Intercompany module: one entity creates a transaction, like a management fee, the partner approves it, and it creates journals in both ledgers and, if needed, AR and AP invoices to settle it. The setup includes intercompany organizations, transaction types, receivables and payables assignments, and the intercompany reconciliation report."

Tip: Separate the two parts clearly. It's what the question is testing.

11How do you reconcile intercompany balances at month end?IntercompanyIn-depth

Why they ask: intercompany differences delay consolidation.

Answer:

"I run the Intercompany Reconciliation report, which compares due-to and due-from between each pair of entities. Then I investigate the differences: timing, where one side booked in the next period; FX rate differences; missing transactions; and manual journals with the wrong intercompany segment. Having an intercompany segment in the chart makes it possible to report balances by trading partner. I agree a cut-off date and a settlement process, usually netting. Eliminations happen in consolidation, in FCCS or with GL elimination rules."

Tip: List the causes in order of how often you see them. Timing is usually first.

12How do you configure invoice approval workflow in Fusion Payables?Accounts PayableIn-depth

Why they ask: a hands-on configuration question for senior functional consultants.

Answer:

"I turn on invoice approval in Manage Invoice Options for the business unit, then build the rules in BPM through Manage Invoice Approval Rules. A typical set: PO-matched invoices within tolerance need no approval, non-PO invoices go to the cost center manager by amount, and anything over $100,000 adds the controller. I use approval groups or the supervisor hierarchy, with a fallback for missing approvers. I decide whether approval happens before or after validation, and whether payment requires approval. Then I test the real-life paths: reassign, delegate, request information and reject."

  1. Enable invoice approval (Manage Invoice Options).
  2. Build rules in BPM (Manage Invoice Approval Rules).
  3. Approval groups or hierarchy, with a fallback.
  4. Set approval order and payment requirement.
  5. Test reassign, delegate, request info, reject.

Tip: Mentioning the test scenarios shows you've been through UAT, not just setup.

13What is Intelligent Document Recognition (IDR) / invoice imaging?Accounts PayableIn-depth

Why they ask: most companies want to stop keying invoices. They want to know you can set up Oracle's capture.

Answer:

"IDR is Oracle's AI-based invoice capture. Suppliers email PDF or image invoices to a dedicated address. IDR reads the invoice and creates an incomplete invoice with the header and lines filled in, matched to the PO where it can find one. AP reviews and completes the exceptions in the Invoices work area. It gets more accurate as it learns supplier layouts. On a rollout I watch three things: supplier communication about where to send invoices, duplicate detection for invoices that arrive by email and by mail, and the touchless rate and processing time."

Tip: Bring up duplicates. Invoices arriving by two routes is the most common IDR problem after go-live.

14How do you set up a new payment format, for example a bank's ACH file?PaymentsIn-depth

Why they ask: bank integration is where projects often slip. They want to know you can handle it end to end.

Answer:

"First I get the bank's file specification and their test process. Then I take a seeded BI Publisher template, like NACHA or ISO 20022 pain.001, copy it and adjust it to the bank's requirements. I create the payment format pointing to that template, and a payment process profile that links the format, the payment method, the transmission over SFTP or web service, and the grouping rules. The profile is assigned to the disbursement bank account and payment methods. Then testing with the bank: prenotes, a few small live payments, and making sure we receive and process the acknowledgment files."

  1. Bank specification and test plan.
  2. Copy and adjust the seeded template.
  3. Payment format.
  4. Payment process profile.
  5. Assign to bank account and methods.
  6. Test: prenotes, small live payments, acknowledgments.

Tip: Mention acknowledgment files. Teams forget them, then can't tell whether the bank accepted the payments.

15What is positive pay?PaymentsBasic

Why they ask: a common treasury control, and they want to know you've set it up, not just heard of it.

Answer:

"Positive pay is a fraud control for checks. After each check run, we send the bank a file of the checks we issued: number, amount and payee. The bank only pays checks that match, and anything that doesn't match becomes an exception for treasury to approve or reject. In Fusion, I run the Positive Pay File process with a BI Publisher template in the bank's format, include voided checks so they can't be cashed, and transmit it automatically over SFTP after each payment run."

Tip: Mention voided checks in the file. It shows you understand why the control exists.

16How do you void a payment and what happens to the invoices and accounting?PaymentsIn-depth

Why they ask: voids have side effects on invoices, accounting, 1099 and the bank. They want to see you think about all of them.

Answer:

"I void the payment in Payables with a void date and choose what happens to the invoices: cancel them, put them on hold, or leave them open to pay again. The accounting reverses the payment on the void date. If the payment already cleared the bank, I can't just void it, it becomes a stop payment or a refund. For electronic payments, I only void if the bank actually rejected the payment. And I check the knock-on effects: 1099 amounts and the next positive pay file, which should include the void."

Example: a $3,000 check is voided and the invoice left open.

  • Entry: Dr Cash Clearing 3,000 / Cr AP Liability 3,000
  • The invoice is unpaid again and picked up by the next payment run.

Tip: Covering the 1099 and positive pay impact is what lifts this answer above average.

17How do you set up Lockbox processing?Accounts ReceivableIn-depth

Why they ask: lockbox is the main high-volume cash channel in the US. They want the steps.

Answer:

"I define the lockbox with the bank's transmission format, usually BAI2 or a custom format the bank provides, and map the file fields to Oracle fields. Then I set the matching rules: match on transaction number, PO number, sales order or consolidated bill number. AutoCash rules handle receipts where nothing matches. The process runs import, validate, then post QuickCash and apply. In Fusion that's one program, Process Receipts Through Lockbox. The KPI I'd track is the percentage of lockbox receipts applied automatically. Getting that from 60% to 85% usually takes better customer references, not more setup."

Tip: Name the format and the KPI. It shows you've worked with a bank file, not just the setup screen.

18How does Fusion Revenue Management support ASC 606 / IFRS 15?Accounts ReceivableIn-depth

Why they ask: revenue is a high-risk area. They want to know you understand the accounting as well as the module.

Answer:

"Revenue Management follows the five steps of ASC 606. Source documents, like sales orders, contracts and AR invoices, come in and are grouped into customer contracts, which is step one. The lines become performance obligations, step two. The transaction price, step three, is allocated across the obligations by relative standalone selling price, which finance sets by item or item class, step four. Revenue is then recognized when each obligation is satisfied, step five, either at a point in time, like shipment or acceptance, or over time, like ratably over a support period. The accounting gives contract assets, contract liabilities for deferred revenue, and revenue. It also handles contract changes and discounts."

Tip: Walk through the five steps and say which part of the module does each. That's the best answer they'll hear.

19How does credit management work in Fusion?Accounts ReceivableIn-depth

Why they ask: credit links AR to sales. They want the flow from limit to hold release.

Answer:

"Customers get credit profiles with a limit and a review cycle. When an order is booked in Order Management, a credit check compares exposure, which is open receivables plus uninvoiced orders, to the limit. If it's over, the order goes on credit hold. Analysts then work a credit review in a case folder, which pulls in aging, payment history and external scores like D&B. They recommend releasing the hold or changing the limit, and the change routes for approval. Once approved, the hold is released and the order moves on."

Tip: Describe the path order → check → hold → review → release. It's what they're listening for.

20How do you handle construction-in-progress (CIP) assets?Fixed AssetsIn-depth

Why they ask: CIP sits on the balance sheet and is easy to forget.

Answer:

"Costs are collected as CIP assets, either from AP mass additions or from Projects. CIP doesn't depreciate. When construction is finished, I capitalize the CIP asset: it gets a date placed in service and starts depreciating. With Fusion Projects, capital projects build the assets, generate asset lines and interface them to Assets. The control I always set up is a CIP aging review every month, so a building that's been in use for six months doesn't sit in CIP with no depreciation."

Tip: The CIP aging review is the key control. Say it.

21How do you merge or split mass additions?Fixed AssetsBasic

Why they ask: a practical Assets task that shows you've done the work.

Answer:

"Merge combines several invoice lines into one asset, for example adding the freight and installation lines to the main equipment line. Split turns one line into many assets, like a line for 10 laptops becoming 10 separate assets. Add to Asset puts the cost onto an asset that's already in service. I do all of this in the Mass Additions work area before posting. Merged lines take the parent line's category and details, so I make sure the parent is set up correctly first."

Tip: Give the laptop and freight examples. They cover both actions.

22How do you design reconciliation rule sets for high auto-match rates?Cash ManagementIn-depth

Why they ask: a hands-on design question with a measurable result.

Answer:

"I start by reading real bank statements to see which references each bank actually returns: check number, payment reference, invoice number. Then I build rules from strict to loose: exact reference and amount first, then amount with a date tolerance, then one-to-many and many-to-one for batches. I add tolerance rules for small differences like fees, and transaction creation rules for recurring charges. Then I make sure our outgoing payment files carry a reference the bank sends back, like the ISO 20022 end-to-end ID. After go-live I measure the auto-match rate monthly and adjust. Going from 70% to above 90% is normal with that approach."

Tip: Start with "I read the statements first". It shows you design from data.

23How would you set up a new tax rate, for example a VAT rate change in a country?TaxIn-depth

Why they ask: rate changes happen every year somewhere, and mistakes mean wrong tax on invoices.

Answer:

"In Manage Tax Rates I end-date the current rate and create the new one from the effective date, or add a new rate period under the same rate code. I check whether tax rules need changing, and check the recovery rates and accounts. Then I test AP, AR and PO transactions dated before and after the change. I tell the AP and AR teams what's changing, and check the reports and the tax filing for the switch-over period. The tricky cases are transactions that span the date, like an invoice dated before the change but credited after it."

Tip: Mention transactions that span the date. It's the case that catches people out.

24What is budgetary control and encumbrance accounting?Budgetary ControlIn-depth

Why they ask: important in public sector, higher education and some corporates.

Answer:

"Budgetary control checks spending against budgets in real time. Requisitions, POs and invoices are stopped if they go over the available funds with absolute control, or just warned with advisory control. Encumbrance reserves the funds as spending moves along: a requisition encumbrance, then a PO encumbrance, then the actual on the invoice, with each step relieving the one before. Setup includes control budgets, budgetary control rules, control levels by segment, and loading the budgets. A funds check only validates; a funds reserve actually uses up the funds."

Tip: Explain funds check vs funds reserve. It's the detail that proves hands-on work.

25How would you build a financial statement in Fusion?ReportingIn-depth

Why they ask: financial statements are the end product of GL.

Answer:

"I build the account hierarchies first, for natural account and cost center, because the report depends on them. Then I use Financial Reporting Studio, or Smart View or Narrative Reporting, against the GL balances cube. Rows are hierarchy nodes like Revenue, COGS and Opex; columns are periods and scenarios like Actual, Budget and Prior Year; and the point of view is ledger, company and currency. I add formulas for variances and percentages, formatting, and drill-down to journals. Then I publish to the Financial Reporting Center and schedule batches if needed. Before release, I reconcile it to the trial balance."

Tip: End with reconciling to the trial balance. It's the step that builds trust.

26When would you choose OTBI vs BI Publisher?ReportingBasic

Why they ask: picking the wrong tool wastes time.

Answer:

"OTBI is self-service, drag and drop on subject areas, real-time, and it applies user security automatically. It's best for ad-hoc analysis, dashboards and simple lists, but it can't join everything and formatting is basic. BI Publisher uses SQL data models with RTF, Excel or eText templates for pixel-perfect output. It's best for documents like checks, invoices and statements, bank files, complex joins and extracts, and bursting. It needs more technical skill, and you have to handle data security in the SQL yourself. So I start with OTBI and move to BIP when formatting or data complexity requires it."

Tip: Mention that BIP doesn't apply data security on its own. It's a senior-level point.

27What is a BIP data model and how do you secure a BIP report?ReportingIn-depth

Why they ask: BIP security is often done wrong.

Answer:

"A data model defines the data: SQL queries, parameters, lists of values, links between data sets, and event triggers. The report applies a layout template, RTF, Excel or eText, to that data. To secure it, I put the report in a catalog folder with permissions for specific roles. And in the SQL, I filter the data by the user's access, for example by joining to the business unit security views, because BIP doesn't apply data security automatically."

Tip: The SQL security point is what separates senior from junior. Make sure you say it.

28How do you use the Close Monitor and close tasks in Fusion?Period CloseIn-depth

Why they ask: close visibility is what controllers want.

Answer:

"The Close Monitor shows period status across subledgers and ledgers in one view, so you can see which are open, closed or pending, and drill into each. Many companies use the task manager in EPM Financial Consolidation and Close, or a tool like BlackLine, for the close checklist, with owners, dependencies and due dates. I set the checklist up to follow the real close sequence, with evidence attached for audit."

Tip: Mention attaching evidence. Auditors ask for it every quarter.

29How do you handle period-end accruals for received but not invoiced (RNI) goods?Period CloseIn-depth

Why they ask: RNI accruals are a common audit area.

Answer:

"There are two methods. Accrue at receipt, common for inventory and many expense items, books the accrual when goods are received and clears it when the invoice is matched. Period-end accrual, used for some expense items, runs the accrual process at period end, and the entry reverses next period. Either way, I review the accrual reconciliation report for old or wrong accruals and write them off with approval. Finance decides which method applies by item and destination type."

Tip: Mention reviewing old accruals. Stale RNI balances are a classic audit finding.

30How are exchange rates managed and used in Oracle?Multi-currencyBasic

Why they ask: missing rates block accounting.

Answer:

"There are rate types, like Corporate, Spot and User. Daily rates are loaded by hand, by spreadsheet, or through an integration from a provider like Reuters, Bloomberg or a bank. Transactions use the rate for their date and rate type, and User rate types let someone type a rate. Revaluation uses period-end rates, and translation uses period-end, average and historical rates. The key operational point is that rates must be loaded before processing, because a missing rate stops accounting."

Tip: End with the missing-rate point. It's the problem everyone has hit once.

31How do realized gains and losses arise in AP and AR?Multi-currencyBasic

Why they ask: a basic multi-currency accounting question.

Answer:

"A realized gain or loss happens when a foreign-currency invoice is paid or receipted at a different rate than the invoice rate. For example, a EUR 1,000 supplier invoice booked at 1.10 is $1,100. If we pay it when the rate is 1.12, it costs $1,120, so that's a $20 realized loss. SLA books it to the realized gain or loss account at payment or receipt. Unrealized gains and losses are different: they come from revaluing open balances at period end."

Tip: Work the example out loud. It makes the answer stick.

32What do you know about Oracle Fusion Lease Accounting?Lease AccountingIn-depth

Why they ask: a broad opening question about the lease module.

Answer:

"It supports ASC 842 and IFRS 16 for lessees. You set up lease contracts with payments, terms and options. It calculates the right-of-use asset and the lease liability as the present value of the payments at the discount rate. Under ASC 842 it classifies each lease as finance or operating. It generates the amortization schedules, the journals, and the AP invoices for payments, and it links to Assets for ROU assets. It also handles modifications, impairments and terminations."

Tip: If you've done a lease project, add one number, like how many leases you onboarded.

33How do you configure expense policies and corporate cards?ExpensesIn-depth

Why they ask: a detailed setup question for Expenses projects.

Answer:

"I start with expense templates and expense types, like meals, hotel and mileage, each with a default account. Then the policies: rate limits by location or job level, daily limits, when receipts are required, and when a justification is needed. Each policy can be a warning or a hard error. Mileage rates are set by period and vehicle type. For corporate cards, I set up the card program, the issuer file load for Amex or Visa, matching to employees, and the payment option: company pay or individual pay. Finally the audit rules and receipt management."

  • Templates and expense types with default accounts.
  • Policies: rate and daily limits, receipt and justification rules, warning or error.
  • Mileage rates.
  • Corporate cards: program, issuer file, employee matching, company or individual pay.
  • Audit rules and receipt management.

Tip: Mention warning versus error and why you chose each. That's the design judgment they're looking for.

34How do you design custom roles for a finance implementation?SecurityIn-depth

Why they ask: role design affects audit and SoD.

Answer:

"I start from the seeded job roles and map them to the client's real jobs: AP clerk, AP supervisor, GL accountant, controller. I copy the seeded roles and remove privileges that aren't needed, rather than building from scratch. I check segregation of duties conflicts, like supplier maintenance plus payments. I assign data access per BU or ledger. I document the design and get sign-off from the controller and audit. And I test each role with real scenarios, including negative tests to prove what a user can't do."

Tip: Mention negative tests. Proving what users can't do is half of security testing.

35How do you convert open AP invoices and validate the conversion?Data MigrationIn-depth

Why they ask: conversion errors show up as wrong payments.

Answer:

"I extract open invoices as of cutover and usually load only the remaining unpaid amount. They load through FBDI, Payables Standard Invoice Import, with supplier, site, terms, due dates and the original invoice number. I validate and account them, and the offset goes to a conversion clearing account that's cleared by the GL balance conversion. Then I reconcile count and amount by supplier against the legacy aging, and finance signs off. I do this in at least two mock conversions before production."

Tip: Mention keeping original invoice numbers and due dates. Payment timing depends on them.

36What is MOAC in EBS R12?EBSIn-depth

Why they ask: EBS knowledge still matters for migrations.

Answer:

"Multi-Org Access Control lets a user work with several operating units from one responsibility. It's controlled by a security profile, set in the MO: Security Profile option, listing the operating units, and MO: Default Operating Unit sets the default. Before R12, users needed a separate responsibility for each operating unit. In Fusion, the equivalent is data access by business unit through data roles."

Tip: Link it to Fusion. It shows you can help EBS clients move.

37What are the key differences between EBS and Fusion for Financials consultants?EBSBasic

Why they ask: many clients are moving from EBS to Fusion.

Answer:

"Operating units become business units. Responsibilities become job roles plus data access. Concurrent programs become scheduled processes. Forms and Discoverer give way to OTBI, BIP and Smart View. Custom code and CEMLIs give way to extensions only: Visual Builder, OIC and BIP. Patching by the customer becomes quarterly updates by Oracle. And there are new capabilities: AI agents, Smart View on live balances, embedded analytics, and Intelligent Document Recognition."

Tip: Show you can explain these to an EBS client. That's often the job.

38How do you manage Oracle's quarterly updates?Quarterly UpdatesBasic

Why they ask: every Fusion customer lives with quarterly updates.

Answer:

"I read the release readiness and What's New documents for our modules, and identify opt-in features worth enabling and mandatory changes. Updates reach test two weeks before production, so I run the regression pack, ideally automated, for key flows: P2P, O2C, close, payments, integrations and reports. Issues are logged and service requests raised early. And users get release notes and training."

Tip: Mention the two-week window. It shows you know the real timeline.

39How do you write a good functional test script?TestingBasic

Why they ask: good test scripts make UAT and regression work.

Answer:

"It has a clear purpose, which requirement or scenario it tests. It lists preconditions and data: supplier, item and user role. The steps are written so a business user can follow them. Each step has an expected result, including accounting entries and reports. It includes negative cases, what should fail. It traces back to requirements, and evidence like screenshots is captured. And I reuse the scripts as the regression pack for quarterly updates."

Tip: Mention reuse for regression. It doubles the value of the work.

40What goes into a functional design document?DesignBasic

Integration and data touchpoints. Security and controls. Assumptions, dependencies and open items. And sign-off. A good design document lets someone else configure the module without asking me."

Tip: End with "someone else can configure from it". That's the quality bar.

44How do you design account hierarchies for reporting?General LedgerIn-depth

Why they ask: hierarchies drive every GL report.

Answer:

46How do you prevent supplier bank account fraud in Oracle?Accounts PayableIn-depth

Why they ask: fake bank change requests are one of the most common payment frauds. They want real controls.

Answer:

"The first control is segregation of duties: the people who process invoices can't create or change supplier bank accounts. Supplier changes through the portal go through approval as supplier profile change requests. Before approving any bank change, someone calls the supplier on a contact number we already have on file, never the number in the request. I run a report of bank account changes before every payment run, and the first payment to a new account is held for review. Those four together stop most attempts."

  • Segregation of duties on bank account changes.
  • Approval of supplier profile change requests.
  • Callback to a known contact.
  • Bank change report before each payment run.
  • Hold first payment to a new account.

Tip: Mention a real control and the segregation-of-duties conflicts you checked. Concrete detail makes this answer believable.

48How do you set up AutoMatch for receipts?Accounts ReceivableIn-depth

Why they ask: AutoMatch is how Fusion automates cash application. They want to see you can tune it.

Answer:

"I create AutoMatch rule sets that tell Fusion how to match receipt remittance references to open transactions. The key settings are the thresholds: the customer recommendation threshold, the combined weighted threshold, and how many days of closed invoices to consider. Fusion scores each possible match using exact and partial string matching and weights them. Then application exception rules decide what to do with small differences, like writing off under $5 or putting overpayments on account. After go-live I watch the results for a few weeks and adjust the thresholds. Too strict and nothing applies; too loose and receipts go to the wrong invoices."

Tip: Explain the trade-off of strict vs loose thresholds. It shows you've tuned it, not only set it up.

49What is cash positioning and forecasting in Fusion?Cash ManagementIn-depth

60How does an on-account credit memo differ from a regular credit memo?Accounts ReceivableBasic

Why they ask: a basic question to check you know where credits sit.

Answer:

"A regular credit memo is applied to a specific invoice and reduces that invoice's balance, for example crediting one damaged line. An on-account credit memo isn't tied to any invoice. It sits on the customer's account as a credit, like a goodwill credit or a rebate, and can be applied later to any invoice or refunded.
