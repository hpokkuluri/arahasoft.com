Oracle Functional Consultant – SCM Interview Questions

The questions interviewers ask for an Oracle SCM Functional Consultant role (2–3 years), with why they ask, a full answer you can say in the room, and a tip. Tap a question to see the answer.

1What modules make up Oracle Fusion SCM?SCM BasicsBasic

Why they ask: a warm-up question.

Answer:

"Product Hub for items and structures. Procurement: purchasing, self-service procurement, sourcing, supplier portal and qualification. Inventory Management for orgs, transactions and counts. Order Management with Global Order Promising. Logistics: shipping, receiving and transportation. Cost Management for item cost, receipt accounting and cost accounting. Manufacturing for work definitions and work orders. And Supply Chain Planning for demand, supply and replenishment."

Tip: Then say which ones you've worked on and how deeply. That's what they really want to know.

2What is the difference between an inventory organization, a subinventory and a locator?InventoryBasic

Why they ask: a basic structure question.

Answer:

"An inventory organization is a plant, warehouse or DC that holds items and inventory, with its own item attributes, costing and calendar. A subinventory is an area inside it, physical or logical, like Stores, Finished Goods, Staging or MRB, and it can be quantity-tracked or expense. A locator is a specific bin or shelf inside a subinventory, like Row 3, Rack 2, Bin 5. So the hierarchy is organization, then subinventory, then locator."

Tip: Give a locator example. It makes the hierarchy easy to see.

3What is an item master organization and why do items exist in it?InventoryBasic

Why they ask: item master design avoids duplicate items.

Answer:

"Items are created once in the item master organization, which in Fusion is the master org in Product Hub, and then assigned to the inventory orgs that use them. Master-level attributes, like description and primary UOM, must be the same everywhere. Org-level attributes, like planner, min-max levels and lead times, can differ by org. That avoids duplicate items and keeps definitions consistent. Item classes and templates default attributes when items are created."

Tip: Give one master and one org-level attribute. It shows you know the split.

4What are the main inventory transaction types?InventoryBasic

Why they ask: a basic inventory question.

Answer:

"Receipts from purchase orders or transfers. Issues to work orders, accounts or sales orders. Subinventory transfers within an org. Interorganization transfers between orgs, direct or in-transit. Miscellaneous receipts and issues, adjusting against an account. And cycle count and physical inventory adjustments. Each has a transaction type, source type and action, and each creates cost accounting."

Tip: Mention that each creates accounting. It shows you see the finance side.

5What is lot and serial control?InventoryBasic

Why they ask: traceability is critical in regulated industries.

Answer:

"Lot control tracks a batch of items with the same properties, like made together or with the same expiry date. It's standard in pharma and food, and lots can carry expiry dates, grades and status. Serial control tracks each unit with its own number, like equipment. Serial numbers can be assigned at receipt, predefined, or at sales order issue. Both give full traceability, called genealogy: where a lot or serial came from and where it went. Control is set on the item and is hard to change once transactions exist, so it has to be decided before go-live."

Tip: Stress deciding before go-live. Changing it later is very painful.

6What is the difference between cycle counting and physical inventory?InventoryBasic

Why they ask: a basic accuracy question.

Answer:

"Cycle counting counts a small set of items regularly, often by ABC class, with A items counted most often. It keeps records accurate without stopping operations. A physical inventory counts everything at once, often at year end, usually freezing transactions. The cycle count process is: define the count with classes and frequency, generate schedules and count sequences, enter counts, recount anything out of tolerance, and approve adjustments, which post to inventory and accounting."

Tip: Say cycle counting avoids shutting down. That's why companies prefer it.

7How does min-max planning work?InventoryBasic

Why they ask: a simple, common replenishment method.

Answer:

"I set a minimum and maximum for an item in an org or subinventory. The min-max process checks on-hand plus on-order, and if it's below the minimum, it suggests or creates a replenishment order to bring it up to the maximum. Replenishment can be a purchase requisition, a transfer from another org, or a work order. Fixed lot multiples and minimum order quantities adjust the quantity. For example, min 50, max 200, on-hand plus on-order of 40, so it orders 160. It works well for stable-demand items like spare parts and consumables."

Tip: Do the calculation out loud. It proves you understand it.

8What is a reservation?InventoryIn-depth

Why they ask: reservations explain "why can't I ship this, it's on-hand?"

Answer:

"A reservation holds on-hand quantity for a specific demand, like a sales order or work order, so nothing else can use it. It can be at org, subinventory, lot, locator or serial level. It reduces available to reserve and available to transact. Reservations are created manually or automatically, for example during scheduling. The key difference: on-hand is what's physically there; available is on-hand minus reservations and other holds."

Tip: End with on-hand vs available. It's the confusion users bring to support.

9Explain the Procure-to-Pay flow and the main documents.ProcurementBasic

Why they ask: a warm-up question for every procurement or AP role.

Answer:

"An employee raises a requisition in Self Service Procurement and it's approved. A purchase order is created from it, manually or automatically, approved and sent to the supplier. When the goods or services arrive, they're received, optionally inspected, and delivered to inventory or expense. The supplier's invoice is matched in AP to the PO and receipt. Then AP pays it in a payment run."

Accounting:

  • Receipt: Dr Inventory or Expense, Cr Accrual.
  • Invoice: Dr Accrual, Cr AP Liability.
  • Payment: Dr Liability, Cr Cash.

Tip: Give the three journal entries. It shows you understand procurement from finance's side.

10What types of purchase orders are there?ProcurementBasic

Why they ask: a basic procurement question.

Answer:

"A standard PO is a one-time purchase with specific items, quantities, prices and dates. A blanket purchase agreement sets prices for items over a period, without fixed quantities, and orders are released against it. A contract purchase agreement sets terms with a supplier but no items, and standard POs reference it. EBS also has a planned PO, with committed quantities and tentative dates, released on a schedule. In Fusion, the three types are standard PO, blanket agreement and contract agreement."

Tip: Note the EBS vs Fusion difference. It shows you know both.

11How does a requisition get converted into a purchase order automatically?ProcurementBasic

Why they ask: touchless POs are a key procurement goal.

Answer:

"The requisition line has to reference a blanket agreement or catalog item with an agreed price and supplier. Once the requisition is approved, automated buyer processing creates the PO, as long as the line has a valid source agreement, the supplier site is active, and the agreement allows automatic ordering. The PO is then approved, or auto-approved within limits, and sent to the supplier. Lines without a source go to the buyer's Process Requisitions work area to be converted by hand."

Tip: List the three conditions. They're also the three reasons auto-creation fails.

12How are purchasing approvals configured in Fusion?ProcurementBasic

Why they ask: approvals are the most common source of procurement complaints.

Answer:

"Approvals are BPM rules, set in Manage Requisition Approvals and Manage Purchasing Document Approvals. Rules can use amount, category, cost center, project, the supervisor hierarchy, position or job level, or approval groups. For example: requisitions under $5,000 go to the supervisor; over $5,000 to the supervisor and then the department head; IT categories also go to an IT approver. I test the rules with different amounts and categories, and I always check there's a fallback so no document hits 'no approver found'."

Tip: Give a sample rule set with amounts. It makes the answer concrete.

13What is a procurement catalog?ProcurementIn-depth

Why they ask: catalogs drive compliance and automation.

Answer:

"Catalogs let employees shop like an online store in Self Service Procurement. A local catalog has items and prices loaded into Fusion, from agreements or uploads. A punchout catalog takes the user to the supplier's website to pick items and bring back a cart. An informational catalog gives instructions or links, like how to order business cards. Content zones control which users see which catalogs. And smart forms handle non-catalog requests with guided fields."

Tip: Explain content zones. They're how you show the right catalog to the right people.

14How do you onboard a new supplier in Oracle Fusion?ProcurementBasic

Why they ask: supplier onboarding is both a process and a control.

Answer:

"The supplier registers through the supplier portal, or procurement enters them. The registration goes for approval. Then the supplier record is created: tax ID, addresses, sites per procurement BU, site assignments to the business units that will use them, bank account with verification, contacts, payment terms and method. If needed, qualification questionnaires cover things like diversity or compliance. And I restrict who can change bank details, because that's a common fraud risk."

Tip: Mention site assignments. Missing ones are the top reason new suppliers can't be used.

15What are the receipt routings?ReceivingBasic

Why they ask: routing affects when stock is available and when accruals happen.

Answer:

"Direct delivery receives and delivers in one step to the final destination. Standard receipt receives first to a receiving location, then delivers in a separate step. Inspection required receives, inspects to accept or reject, then delivers. The routing defaults from the PO schedule, item, supplier or receiving options. The choice affects when inventory becomes available and, with accrue at receipt, when the accrual is booked."

Tip: Mention the accrual timing. It shows you see the accounting impact.

16How do you return goods to a supplier?ReceivingBasic

Why they ask: returns touch receiving, inventory and AP.

Answer:

"I create a return to supplier transaction against the original receipt. Inventory goes down and receipt accounting reverses the accrual. If the invoice was already received, AP needs a debit memo, which can be created automatically if RTS debit memos are enabled on the supplier site. I record the supplier's RMA number and track the return shipment. A correction is different: it fixes a data entry mistake on a receipt, without sending goods back."

Tip: Separate returns from corrections. It's a common mix-up.

17Explain the Order-to-Cash flow from the SCM side.Order ManagementBasic

Why they ask: a warm-up question for SCM roles.

Answer:

"A sales order is created manually, by import, or from CPQ or CRM. Orchestration starts: the order is validated, priced and scheduled, with Global Order Promising checking availability. Inventory is reserved, and pick release creates pick tasks. Items are picked and packed. Ship confirm issues inventory and fulfills the line. Billing sends the lines to Receivables through AutoInvoice. Revenue and COGS are recognized, and receipts are applied in AR."

Tip: Name the hand-off to AR. It shows you see the full chain.

18What is order orchestration in Fusion Order Management?Order ManagementIn-depth

Why they ask: orchestration is the core of Fusion OM.

Answer:

"Orchestration is the process engine that drives each order line through fulfillment. Orchestration processes define the steps, like schedule, reserve, ship and invoice, and their order. Fulfillment lines are assigned to a process by process assignment rules, for example by order type or item. Steps call fulfillment systems, like Shipping, Receiving, Manufacturing or a 3PL, through connectors. It also handles jeopardy, change management when an order changes mid-process, and holds. In EBS, the equivalent is Order Management workflows."

Tip: Mention change management. It's what makes orchestration powerful.

19What are holds in Order Management and how are they applied?Order ManagementBasic

Why they ask: holds are a key control.

Answer:

"Holds stop an order or line from moving forward. Credit check holds come from Credit Management when a customer is over their limit. Manual holds cover things like pricing review or export compliance. Each hold definition says where it stops the process, for example before shipping or before invoicing. Holds are applied by hand or by rules, and released by authorized users with a reason. I always check who can release which holds, because that's the control point."

Tip: Mention who can release holds. Auditors look at it.

20How does pricing work for sales orders in Fusion?Order ManagementIn-depth

Why they ask: pricing is complex and different from EBS.

Answer:

"Fusion uses Pricing Administration. Price lists hold the base price by item and UOM. Discount lists hold percentage or amount discounts. Pricing strategies combine price lists and discounts, and are assigned to customers or segments through pricing segments and strategy assignment rules. Pricing algorithms calculate the final price, and charges like freight can be added. Cost-plus pricing is possible too. EBS uses Advanced Pricing modifiers and qualifiers instead."

Tip: Contrast with EBS. Many clients are migrating and want to know the difference.

21What is pick release and what happens during it?ShippingBasic

Why they ask: pick release is the start of outbound fulfillment.

Answer:

"Pick release selects order lines ready to ship and creates pick tasks or move orders. Release rules choose which lines by order, customer, ship date, warehouse or priority. Picking rules decide where to pick from, like FIFO or FEFO by lot expiry. It can allocate and pick confirm automatically. After picking, items go to staging, then they're packed and ship confirmed, which issues inventory."

Tip: Mention FEFO picking rules. It's the practical example for regulated goods.

22What costing methods does Oracle support?CostingBasic

Why they ask: a basic costing question.

Answer:

"Fusion supports standard cost, a fixed cost per item with differences going to variances like PPV; perpetual average cost, which updates with each receipt; and actual cost, FIFO or LIFO, where cost layers are consumed in order. EBS has standard, average, FIFO, LIFO, plus periodic average and incremental LIFO. In Fusion the method is set by cost organization and cost book through cost profiles; in EBS it's by inventory org."

Tip: Mention where the method is set in each. It shows you know both products.

23What is the difference between Receipt Accounting and Cost Accounting in Fusion?CostingIn-depth

Why they ask: these two are often confused.

Answer:

"Receipt Accounting creates the accounting for receipts, deliveries and returns, and manages accruals, both receipt accruals and period-end accruals for expense items, including clearing and write-offs. Cost Accounting costs inventory transactions, like issues, transfers, shipments and production, using the cost method, with entries like Dr COGS, Cr Inventory. Each runs its own Create Accounting process and transfers to GL."

Tip: Say you schedule both regularly. It shows you've run them in production.

24What is purchase price variance (PPV)?CostingBasic

Why they ask: PPV is the most watched cost variance.

Answer:

"Under standard costing, inventory is valued at standard. When we buy at a different price, the difference is PPV: PO price minus standard, times quantity received. For example, standard $10, PO price $11, 100 units gives $100 unfavorable PPV. At delivery the entry is Dr Inventory $1,000, Dr PPV $100, Cr Accrual $1,100. Invoice price variance then captures any difference between the invoice and the PO. Procurement and finance watch PPV to measure buying performance."

Tip: Give the journal entry. It shows you know where PPV lands.

25How does a standard cost update work?CostingIn-depth

Why they ask: a yearly event with P&L impact.

Answer:

"I create a new cost scenario with the new standards, usually yearly or quarterly. I roll up costs for manufactured items: material, resources and overhead. Finance reviews it, then I publish. On publish, Oracle revalues on-hand inventory at the new cost and posts the difference to the revaluation account. Before publishing, I run a what-if report so finance sees the revaluation impact in advance."

Tip: Mention the what-if report. Finance hates surprises in revaluation.

26What are item classes and item templates?Product HubBasic

Why they ask: a basic Product Hub question.

Answer:

"An item class is a hierarchy that groups items, like Raw Materials, then Chemicals. Classes define numbering, attribute groups, templates, NIR rules and data security, and child classes inherit from parents. An item template is a set of default attribute values, like 'Purchased Item' with purchasable, stockable, transactable and costing enabled, applied when items are created so attributes are consistent. EBS uses templates and categories; Fusion adds item classes on top."

Tip: Contrast with EBS. It helps clients moving from EBS understand the change.

27What is an item category and how is it used?Product HubBasic

Why they ask: categories drive many other setups.

Answer:

"Categories group items for a business purpose, and each catalog serves one function. The purchasing catalog drives buyer assignment, approvals and spend reporting. The inventory catalog is for reporting and planning. The costing or GL catalog drives accounts through SLA rules. An item can be in several catalogs, usually one category per catalog. Because so many setups depend on them, I design the catalogs early in the project."

Tip: Say "design catalogs early". Changing them later breaks approvals and accounting.

28What are item status codes and lifecycle phases?Product HubIn-depth

Why they ask: status controls what can be done with an item.

Answer:

"Item status, like Active, Inactive, Prototype or Obsolete, controls key attributes: whether the item can be purchased, stocked, transacted, ordered or used in BOMs. Life-cycle phases, like Design, Prototype, Production and Obsolete, track where the item is in its life, and changing phase can change status. Changing the status is how you stop an item from being ordered without deleting it."

Tip: Make the point about not deleting items. History needs them.

29What is a bill of materials (BOM) and a routing / work definition?ManufacturingBasic

Why they ask: a basic manufacturing question.

Answer:

"A BOM, or item structure, is the list of components and quantities needed to make an item. A routing in EBS, or a work definition in Fusion, is the sequence of operations and resources, machines and labor, with components assigned to the operations where they're used. Together they drive work orders, cost roll-ups and planning. In Fusion, item structures live in Product Hub and work definitions in Manufacturing."

Tip: Mention where each lives in Fusion. It's a common follow-up.

30Describe the work order lifecycle in Fusion Manufacturing.ManufacturingIn-depth

Why they ask: a core manufacturing process question.

Answer:

"A work order is created manually, from planning, or for a sales order. It's released, so components can be picked. Components are issued manually or backflushed. Operations and resources are reported as work is done. The assembly is completed into inventory, with any scrap reported. Then the work order is closed, and Cost Management calculates variances. The statuses go Unreleased, Released, Completed, Closed, and it can also be On Hold or Canceled."

Tip: Stress closing work orders on time. Late closes delay variances and month-end.

31What is the difference between MRP and min-max planning?PlanningIn-depth

Why they ask: to see if you can pick the right method.

Answer:

"Min-max looks only at on-hand and on-order against fixed minimum and maximum levels. No forecasts, no BOM explosion. MRP, in Fusion Supply Planning, uses demand from forecasts and sales orders, supply from on-hand, POs and work orders, plus BOMs and lead times, to calculate what to make or buy and when, exploding demand down to components. I use min-max for simple, stable items like spares, and MRP for manufactured items and variable demand."

Tip: Say when you'd use each. That's the real question.

32What is an interorganization transfer and how is it set up?InventoryIn-depth

Why they ask: transfers between orgs need setup and accounting.

Answer:

"It moves inventory between two inventory orgs. A shipping network defines the from and to orgs, the transfer type, direct or in-transit, the in-transit accounts and whether a receipt is required. Direct moves instantly. In-transit ships, sits in transit, and is received at the destination. In Fusion, transfer orders give a full ship and receive flow with transfer pricing. If the orgs are in different legal entities, transfer price and freight create intercompany accounting."

Tip: Mention transfer orders as the Fusion way. It shows current knowledge.

33How do you investigate an on-hand quantity that doesn't match the physical count?InventoryBasic

Why they ask: a daily warehouse problem.

Answer:

"First I check for pending or unprocessed transactions: interface errors, pending receipts or shipments. Then I review transaction history for the item, subinventory and locator, looking for wrong subinventory, UOM mistakes, or lot and serial errors. Then reservations and staged quantities, picked but not shipped. Then I recount to confirm. Finally I fix it with an approved adjustment and find the root cause, like training, process or scanning."

Tip: Start with pending transactions. They explain most differences.

34What is sourcing (RFQ/auction) in Oracle Procurement?ProcurementBasic

Why they ask: a basic sourcing question.

Answer:

"Oracle Sourcing creates negotiations to get competitive bids. An RFI asks for information, an RFQ asks for quotes, and an auction lets suppliers see where they rank and bid again. The steps are: create the negotiation with lines, requirements and terms; invite suppliers; collect responses; score and analyze; award; and create a PO or agreement from the award. It gets better prices and gives an audit trail for why a supplier was chosen."

Tip: Mention the audit trail. It's often as important as the savings.

35What happens when you need to change an approved purchase order?ProcurementBasic

Why they ask: PO changes are frequent and have rules.

Answer:

"In Fusion I create a change order, which opens a new revision of the PO. Changes to price, quantity or dates may need approval again, depending on the rules. The revised PO is sent to the supplier. Some changes are blocked after receipt or invoicing, for example I can't reduce quantity below what's been received. When the PO isn't needed anymore, I cancel it or close it: closed for receiving, closed for invoicing, or finally closed."

Tip: Mention what's blocked after receipt. It shows real experience.

36What are the PO close statuses?ProcurementIn-depth

Why they ask: close statuses affect matching and accruals.

Answer:

"Open is normal. Closed for receiving means no more receipts but it can still be invoiced. Closed for invoicing means no more invoices but it can still be received. Closed means both are done, and it can be reopened. Finally closed is permanent, releases any encumbrance, and can't be reopened. Canceled means nothing more can happen. Receipt and invoice close tolerances close POs automatically, for example when 95% has been received."

Tip: Warn that finally closed can't be undone. It's the status people regret using.

37What is the difference between receipt tolerance and invoice tolerance?ReceivingBasic

Why they ask: they work together to prevent overpaying.

Answer:

"Receipt tolerances, on quantity and dates, control how much over or under, or how early or late, you can receive against a PO, with actions like warning or reject. Invoice tolerances in AP, on price, quantity and amount, control how far an invoice can differ from the PO and receipt before a matching hold is placed. Tight receipt tolerance stops over-receiving; invoice tolerance stops overpaying."

Tip: Explain how they work together. That's the insight they want.

38What documents are produced during shipping?ShippingBasic

Why they ask: a basic shipping question.

Answer:

"The pick slip, saying what to pick and where. The packing slip, listing what's in the shipment for the customer. The bill of lading, the contract with the carrier. The commercial invoice for international shipments and customs. Shipping and carton labels. And the ASN, the electronic notice to the customer. They're produced through document sets at pick release or ship confirm."

Tip: Mention document sets. It's how they're configured.

39What is drop shipment?Order ManagementIn-depth

Why they ask: a common fulfillment type.

Answer:

"In a drop shipment, the supplier ships straight to our customer, and the goods never enter our warehouse. A sales order line with a drop ship source creates a requisition and PO to the supplier. When the supplier confirms shipment, we record a logical receipt and shipment. AP pays the supplier and AR invoices the customer. Inventory isn't physically touched, but accounting still flows through receipt and cost."

Tip: Mention the logical receipt. It's how the accounting still happens.

40What is back-to-back ordering?Order ManagementIn-depth

Why they ask: often confused with drop ship.

Answer:

"A sales order line triggers a dedicated purchase or work order for that customer, and the supply is reserved to the order when it arrives. Unlike drop ship, the goods come into our warehouse first, then ship to the customer. It's used for items we don't stock but want to inspect or control. The reservation makes sure the incoming supply isn't used for another order."

Tip: Contrast it with drop ship in one line. That's what the interviewer wants.

41How do returns (RMA) work in Order Management?Order ManagementBasic

Why they ask: a basic returns question.

Answer:

"I create a return order referencing the original sales order where possible, which keeps the price and lot or serial information. The customer ships it back, and it's received, with inspection if needed. Inventory goes up, or goes to a reject or MRB subinventory. A credit memo is created in AR when the return line is invoiced. Return reasons are tracked for analysis."

Tip: Mention return reason analysis. It's what turns returns into improvements.

42How does SCM integrate with Financials?IntegrationBasic

Why they ask: good SCM consultants understand the accounting impact.

Answer:

"Procurement feeds AP, because POs and receipts are matched to invoices. Receiving feeds GL through receipt accrual accounting. Inventory and costing feed GL with inventory valuation, COGS and variances. Order Management feeds AR, because shipped lines are invoiced through AutoInvoice. Capital POs can create asset additions from AP. And project-related POs and costs go to Projects. A good SCM consultant knows the accounting each step creates."

Tip: End with that last sentence. It's what finance-minded interviewers want.

43How would you load items or on-hand balances into Fusion?Data LoadsBasic

Why they ask: data loads are part of every SCM go-live.

Answer:

"Items go in through FBDI with the item import template, or Product Hub spreadsheets and import maps, including attributes, categories and org assignments. On-hand balances go in with Inventory Transaction Import as miscellaneous receipts, with lots, serials and locators, at cost. Open POs go through Purchase Order Import. The order matters: items, then org assignments, then costs, then on-hand. And I reconcile quantities and values with the legacy system after each load."

Tip: Stress the load order. Loading on-hand before costs gives zero-cost inventory.

44How would you test a full P2P cycle after a configuration change?TestingBasic

Why they ask: testing the whole chain catches what unit tests miss.

Answer:

"I create a requisition with the affected item or category and check approval routing. I convert it to a PO and check the defaults: supplier site, terms and accounts. I receive it with the right routing and tolerances. I match the invoice in AP and check holds. I run Create Accounting for receiving and AP and check the GL entries. And I test negative cases, like over-receipt and wrong price, plus a return."

Tip: Mention checking the accounting. Interviewers like that you go past the screens.

45Tell me about a time you supported users during a go-live.BehavioralBasic

Why they ask: go-live support shows how you work under pressure.

Answer (example):

"In the first week after a Fusion SCM go-live, warehouse users struggled with pick release and shipping. I was the on-site support for Inventory and Shipping. I sat with users on the floor, logged every issue, fixed the quick ones like a printer setup and a wrong picking rule, made two one-page job aids, and ran a 15-minute daily huddle. Tickets dropped from about 40 a day to under 10 by the end of week two, and shipments met the daily target."

46How do you prioritize when several users need help at once?BehavioralBasic

Why they ask: support work means juggling.

Answer:

"Business impact first: anything stopping shipping, receiving or payments comes first. Then how many users are affected, then deadlines like month-end. I acknowledge everyone quickly so they know they're in the queue. If two critical issues collide, I escalate to my lead. And I track everything in the ticket tool so nothing gets lost."

Tip: Mention acknowledging everyone. It keeps users calm while they wait.

47What is the difference between an inventory organization and a business unit in Fusion SCM?SCM BasicsIn-depth

Why they ask: enterprise structure design depends on it.

Answer:

"A business unit performs business functions like procurement, requisitioning, order fulfillment and billing, with its own setups and data security. An inventory organization is a physical location that holds and transacts inventory. Each inventory org is tied to a profit center BU and a legal entity for accounting. A procurement BU can serve many requisitioning BUs and inventory orgs. Getting these relationships right is central to the structure design."

Tip: Say the BU is about functions, the org is about place. It's a clean way to remember it.

48What is a Procurement agent and why does it matter?ProcurementIn-depth

Why they ask: a classic support question.

Answer:

"A procurement agent is a buyer set up with access to specific procurement BUs and actions, like managing requisitions, POs, agreements, suppliers and sourcing. It's set in Manage Procurement Agents and controls which documents a buyer can see and act on, their own or everyone's in the BU. The common support ticket is a user with the buyer role who still can't create POs, and the answer is almost always that they aren't set up as a procurement agent."

Tip: Tell the support ticket story. It's exactly why they ask.

49What is ABC classification and how is it used?InventoryBasic

Why they ask: ABC drives counting and planning priorities.

Answer:

"ABC ranks items by value, usually annual usage value, quantity times cost. A items are roughly 10–20% of items but 70–80% of value; they're counted most often and controlled most tightly. B items are in the middle. C items are many items with little value, counted least. In Oracle I compile an ABC analysis for the org, assign classes, and use them in cycle count frequency and planning policies."

Tip: Give the percentages. They show you know the principle.

50What is the difference between a requisitioning BU, procurement BU and sold-to legal entity?ProcurementIn-depth

Why they ask: this model is how Fusion supports central procurement.

Answer:

"The requisitioning BU is where the requester works; it owns the requisition and the charge accounts. The procurement BU is the purchasing team that creates and manages the PO and the supplier relationship, and it can serve many requisitioning BUs. The sold-to legal entity is the one financially liable for the purchase, and it appears on the PO. The bill-to BU is where AP processes the invoice. So a central procurement team in one BU can buy for ten operating BUs, with each one still paying its own bills."

Tip: Give the central procurement example. It shows why the model exists.

51What is Oracle Redwood and what changes for SCM users?RedwoodBasic

Why they ask: SCM users feel Redwood changes on the floor.

Answer:

"Redwood replaces the older ADF classic pages module by module. For SCM there are Redwood pages for requisitions, purchase orders, receipts, inventory transactions and Product Management items. They're cleaner and guided, with better search, mobile use and AI help like summaries and suggestions. Classic personalizations don't carry over, so Redwood pages are extended with Visual Builder Studio. Users need short training because screens and navigation change."

Tip: Name a Redwood SCM page you've used.

52How would you prepare warehouse users for a switch to Redwood receiving or inventory pages?RedwoodBasic

Why they ask: warehouse change management is practical and time-sensitive.

Answer:

57What is the difference between a subinventory transfer and an interorganization transfer?InventoryBasic

Why they ask: a basic transaction question.

Answer:

"A subinventory transfer moves stock within the same org, like Stores to Staging. There's no change of owner or cost org, and usually no accounting unless the subinventories are valued differently. An interorganization transfer moves stock between orgs, like plant to DC. It needs a shipping network, can be direct or in-transit, and creates accounting, possibly intercompany."

Tip: Mention the accounting difference. It's the key point.

Because it changes inventory value directly, it's usually limited by role and sometimes needs approval."

Tip: Mention the control. That's why interviewers ask.

59What is the difference between a requisition, a purchase order and an agreement?ProcurementBasic

Why they ask: a basic procurement question.

Answer:

"A requisition is an internal request to buy something, and it needs approval. A purchase order is the legal order to the supplier, with quantities, prices and dates. An agreement, blanket or contract, sets negotiated terms or prices for a period, and POs are created against it."

Tip: Keep it short. Basic questions get the best impression from clear, quick answers.
