Performance Review Interview Questions

How to handle a mid-year or annual performance review: results, feedback, development and rating. Each question has why it comes up, a sample answer and a tip. The sample answers use an example Senior ERP Analyst; swap in your own projects, wins and goals. Tap a question to see the answer.

Senior Oracle ERP Solution Architect with 22+ years in Oracle Fusion Cloud and E-Business Suite: Financials, SCM, HCM and Procurement. Oracle-certified in R11/R12 Financials and OCI AI Foundations. Arahasoft.

1How would you summarize your year?Self-assessmentBasicTop question

Why they ask: your opening frames the whole review.

Sample answer:

"It was a strong year. As Senior ERP Analyst, I delivered cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule, kept operations stable while we worked on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project, and grew in leading a larger team within the next year. The biggest challenge was a tight go-live date and one open role on the team, and I handled it through phased planning and close work with the business."

Tip: Three outcomes, one challenge handled, in under a minute.

Likely follow-up

How long should that summary be?

Three or four sentences: the two or three results that mattered most, with numbers, and one thing you've grown in. For example: 'This year I led the Redwood rollout on time, cut invoice holds by 40% and started mentoring two analysts.'

2How do I write a strong self-assessment?Self-assessmentBasicTop question

Why they ask: (your own question) many managers copy from it.

Sample answer (structure):

  • Results against each goal, with numbers (time saved, cost, incidents reduced).
  • Key projects: the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project — your role and impact.
  • How you worked: collaboration, leadership, values.
  • Growth: what you learned and areas you're developing.
  • Next-year goals.

Tip: Use specific examples and numbers. Keep a running wins document all year.

Likely follow-up

What's the most common self-assessment mistake?

Listing tasks instead of outcomes and their business impact — 'managed the AP system' instead of 'reduced invoice holds from 20% to 12%, saving 600 hours'. Reviewers remember results, not activity.

3Which goals did you meet, and which did you miss?ResultsBasicTop question

Why they ask: honesty and ownership.

Sample answer:

"I met three of four goals: cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule, stable operations, and the milestone on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project. I missed the automation goal by a quarter because we prioritized the urgent business request. It's now on track for next quarter."

Tip: Own misses with reasons and a recovery plan — no excuses.

Likely follow-up

How do I talk about a missed goal?

Own it, explain what changed, what you learned and the plan — 'I missed the June go-live because the vendor fell behind; I should have escalated a month earlier. We went live in August, and I now set escalation points in every plan.'

4What's your biggest accomplishment this year?ImpactBasicTop question

Why they ask: to understand your impact in business terms.

Sample answer:

"cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. The business impact was fewer escalations and faster processing for the teams we support. My role was leading the plan, aligning stakeholders and making sure we tested thoroughly."

Tip: Situation, your actions, measurable outcome.

Likely follow-up

What if my biggest win was a team effort?

Say so, then describe your specific part — 'The close project was a team effort; I designed the reconciliation automation and led the testing.' It shows honesty and lets your contribution stand out.

5How did you contribute beyond your job description?ImpactIn-depth

Why they ask: to see readiness for bigger roles.

Sample answer:

"I mentored two team members, led a cross-team improvement on request intake, and represented our area in leadership reviews. These go beyond the finance applications and the integrations that feed them and show the scope I'm ready for."

Tip: Show leadership behaviors at the next level.

Likely follow-up

How do I show work beyond my role without sounding like I'm complaining?

Frame it as contribution and growth, with the result.

6What are your development areas?DevelopmentBasicTop question

Why they ask: self-awareness.

Sample answer:

"Two areas. First, delegating more so I can focus on strategy — I've started by handing off daily triage. Second, executive communication — I'm practicing shorter, outcome-first updates. I'd value your feedback on both."

Tip: Real areas, with actions already in progress.

Likely follow-up

How many development areas should I name?

One or two real ones, each with a plan — 'I want to get better at presenting to senior leaders; I've signed up for a course and asked to present the Q3 update.' Real areas with plans sound mature, not weak.

7Here's feedback you might not agree with. How do you respond?FeedbackIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: maturity in hard conversations.

Sample answer:

"Thank you. Can you help me understand specific examples? … I see your point on the first one. On the second, I'd like to share some context. Either way, I'll take the feedback seriously and we can review progress next quarter."

Tip: Listen, seek examples, add context calmly, commit to action.

Likely follow-up

What if I still disagree after listening?

Ask for examples, share my view calmly and agree what would show improvement — 'I hadn't seen it that way. Can you give me an example? Here's what I was trying to do. What would good look like next time?'

8What rating do you think you deserve?RatingIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: to calibrate expectations.

Sample answer:

"Based on delivering cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule, meeting most goals, and taking on extra leadership, I believe I've exceeded expectations. I'd like to understand how you see it and what would distinguish the top rating."

Tip: State your view with evidence, then listen.

Likely follow-up

What if my view is higher than my manager's?

Ask what evidence would justify the higher rating next time — 'I see it a bit higher because of the close results. What would you need to see for that rating next year?' It keeps the conversation constructive.

9I received a lower rating than I expected. What do I say?RatingIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: (your own question) reacting well matters.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I was hoping for a higher rating, so I'd like to understand the gap. Which outcomes or behaviors would have earned it? Could we agree specific goals so I'm clearly on track next year?"

Tip: Stay calm, ask for specifics, and turn it into a plan. Escalate only through proper channels if facts are wrong.

Likely follow-up

What should I say in the moment?

Thank them, ask for specifics and take time before reacting — 'Thanks for explaining. Can you give me examples of where I fell short? I'd like to think about it and come back with a plan.' Then follow up within a week.

10How do I challenge a rating that's factually wrong?RatingIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) disagreement must be evidence-based.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I'd like to revisit one point. The review says the project missed its date, but we delivered on the revised date agreed in March. Here's the email confirming that. Could we update the record?"

Tip: Use facts and documentation, not emotion.

Likely follow-up

When should I involve HR?

If facts are wrong and the manager won't review them.

11What are your goals for next year?GoalsBasicTop question

Why they ask: alignment and ambition.

Sample answer:

"Three goals: complete the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project with measurable business benefits, reduce recurring issues by 25%, and develop toward leading a larger team within the next year by leading a cross-team initiative. Each supports a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance."

Tip: Measurable, business-aligned, with one growth goal.

Likely follow-up

How do I make goals measurable?

Define the outcome, a number and a date — 'Reduce month-end close from 6 days to 4 by Q3' rather than 'improve the close'. Measurable goals make next year's review easier for both of you.

12How did you demonstrate our company values?ValuesBasic

Why they ask: many reviews score behaviors as well as results.

Sample answer:

"Customer focus: I worked directly with the business to solve their top pain point. Collaboration: I partnered across teams on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project. Integrity: I raised a risk early even though it meant delaying a date."

Tip: Map one example to each value.

Likely follow-up

How do I avoid generic values answers?

Give one concrete example per value.

13How did you work with other teams?CollaborationBasic

Why they ask: cross-functional effectiveness.

Sample answer:

"On the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project I set up regular reviews with business and technical teams, agreed clear owners for each decision, and resolved conflicts quickly. Stakeholders gave positive feedback in the mid-year survey."

Tip: Show structure and outcomes.

Likely follow-up

What evidence shows good collaboration?

Feedback from other teams and joint results.

14How have you developed others?LeadershipIn-depth

Why they ask: leaders are measured on the team they build.

Sample answer:

"I mentored two junior team members — one now leads incident triage independently. I created knowledge-sharing sessions that reduced single points of failure."

Tip: Name outcomes for people you developed.

Likely follow-up

What if I have no direct reports?

Mentoring, onboarding and knowledge sharing count.

15What was your biggest challenge and how did you handle it?ChallengesBasicTop question

Why they ask: resilience and problem-solving.

Sample answer:

"a tight go-live date and one open role on the team. I broke the work into phases, negotiated priorities with the business, and added weekly risk reviews. We delivered the critical parts on time and moved the rest to the next phase."

Tip: Challenge, actions, result, lesson.

Likely follow-up

What's the best structure?

Situation, what you did and the result — 'Our main developer left during the upgrade; I reorganized the work, brought in a contractor and we still went live two weeks later than planned instead of two months.'

16Tell me about a mistake this year.MistakesBasicTop question

Why they ask: ownership and learning.

Sample answer:

"I underestimated the testing time for one change, which caused a short issue after release. I owned it, fixed it the same day, and introduced a testing checklist that has prevented repeats."

Tip: Choose a real but contained example, with prevention.

Likely follow-up

How do I end that answer well?

With what you changed so it won't happen again — 'Since then, I double-check every production change with a peer before it goes live.' The lesson is what the reviewer remembers.

17Where do you want your career to go?CareerBasic

Why they ask: development planning and retention.

Sample answer:

"I'm working toward leading a larger team within the next year. Next year I'd like experiences that build toward that — broader scope and more exposure to senior stakeholders. Can we include that in my development plan?"

Tip: Tie aspirations to concrete development actions.

Likely follow-up

How specific should I be?

Clear direction, flexible on exact role.

18How do I connect my review to promotion?PromotionIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: (your own question) reviews are evidence for promotion cases.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I'd like this review to reflect progress toward the next level. Which of my accomplishments demonstrate next-level performance, and what gaps remain? Can we agree a timeline?"

Tip: Ask for criteria, evidence and a timeline.

Likely follow-up

How do I make the link explicit?

Ask which results would show next-level readiness — 'What would I need to show this year to be ready for promotion next cycle?' — and write the answer into your goals.

19How do I discuss pay during a review?CompensationIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: (your own question) reviews often link to pay.

Sample answer (what to say):

"Given the results this year — cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule and expanded scope in the finance applications and the integrations that feed them — I'd like to understand how my pay compares to the market and the range for my role. What can we do in this cycle?"

Tip: Base the case on scope, results and market data.

Likely follow-up

Should pay come up in the review?

Yes, briefly and prepared with market data — 'Based on market data for similar roles, I'm below the midpoint. Can we discuss an adjustment?' Keep the focus on value and data, not personal needs.

20Your peers said you can be too direct. How do you respond?Peer FeedbackIn-depth

Why they ask: self-awareness and growth.

Sample answer:

"That's useful feedback. I value directness for speed, but I can adjust how I deliver it. I'll ask more questions first and acknowledge others' views before giving my position, and I'll check in with peers in a few months."

Tip: Acknowledge, adjust behavior, follow up.

Likely follow-up

How do I respond to "too direct"?

Acknowledge it, ask for an example and share how you'll adjust.

21What are your key strengths?StrengthsBasic

Why they ask: to confirm what to leverage.

Sample answer:

"Ownership — I see things through, like cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. Problem-solving under pressure. And building trust with business stakeholders. I'd like to use these more in leading cross-team work."

Tip: Three strengths with evidence.

Likely follow-up

How do I back up strengths?

One example per strength.

22What support do you need next year?SupportBasic

Why they ask: managers want to enable success.

Sample answer:

"Clear priorities from leadership, backup coverage to free time for the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project, and a stretch assignment toward leading a larger team within the next year. A training budget for one targeted course would also help."

Tip: Specific, reasonable asks.

Likely follow-up

What support is reasonable to ask for?

Training, clearer priorities or a stretch project.

23Do you have feedback for me as your manager?Manager FeedbackBasic

Why they ask: a two-way relationship.

Sample answer:

"Your support during escalations made a real difference. One request: more context on leadership priorities earlier, so I can plan the team's work ahead of changes."

Tip: Appreciation plus one constructive request.

Likely follow-up

How do I give upward feedback safely?

Specific, kind and about what helps me work better.

24How should I prepare for my review meeting?PreparationBasicTop question

Why they ask: (your own question) preparation drives better outcomes.

Sample answer (checklist):

  • Re-read your goals and self-assessment.
  • List three top wins with numbers.
  • Prepare one growth area with actions.
  • Decide your ask (rating, development, pay, promotion path).
  • Prepare questions about next year.

Tip: Walk in knowing the one outcome you want.

Likely follow-up

What should I bring?

Your wins list with numbers, your goals and how you did against each, feedback you've received, and two or three questions about next year. Having it on one page keeps the conversation focused.

25How do I use a mid-year review well?Mid-yearBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) mid-year is the time to correct course.

Sample answer (what to say):

"Here's progress on each goal. Two are on track, one is at risk because priorities shifted toward a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. Should we adjust that goal? What would make my year-end review outstanding?"

Tip: Adjust goals and ask what 'outstanding' looks like.

Likely follow-up

How do I use mid-year well?

Check progress and reset goals if needed.

Tip: Metrics that leadership cares about.

Likely follow-up

What metrics should I use?

Outcomes tied to my goals.

Likely follow-up

28What would you do differently this year?ImprovementBasic

Why they ask: reflection.

Tip: Specific, actionable reflections.

Likely follow-up

What's a good answer?

29How do calibration meetings affect my rating?CalibrationIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) understanding the process.

Sample answer (explanation):

Managers compare ratings across teams to make them consistent. Your manager advocates for you with evidence. Give them clear, quantified wins and examples of impact beyond your team to strengthen your case.

Tip: Equip your manager with evidence before calibration.

Likely follow-up

How do I influence calibration?

Make sure my manager has clear evidence.

30Leadership doesn't know my work. How do I fix that before reviews?VisibilityIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) visibility affects ratings.

Sample answer (approach):

Send monthly outcome summaries to your manager, present at a leadership meeting, ask stakeholders to share feedback with your manager, and credit your team publicly.

Tip: Visibility is part of the job.

Likely follow-up

How do I raise visibility?

Share results with leaders through my manager.

31My manager says I'm not meeting expectations. What do I do?UnderperformanceIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) a critical moment.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I want to fix this. Which specific expectations am I missing, and what does success look like? Can we agree a 60-day plan with check-ins every two weeks?"

Tip: Get specifics, agree a plan in writing, and over-deliver on it.

Likely follow-up

What should I ask for?

Specific expectations and a plan with check-ins.
