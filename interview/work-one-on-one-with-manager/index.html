1:1 with Your Manager Interview Questions

How to handle a 1:1 with your manager: priorities, progress, blockers, feedback, workload and growth. Each question has why it comes up, a sample answer and a tip. The sample answers use an example Senior ERP Analyst; swap in your own projects, wins and goals. Tap a question to see the answer.

50 questions 32 basic 18 in-depth
1What's on your mind this week?OpeningBasic

Why they ask: the 1:1 is your meeting. An open question tests whether you come prepared.

Sample answer:

"Three things. First, a quick update on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project — we're on track and I'll show you where. Second, I need your input on a priority call related to a tight go-live date and one open role on the team. Third, I'd like five minutes on my development goals: leading a larger team within the next year."

Tip: Bring a written agenda of 2–4 items. Lead with what needs their decision.

2How are you doing — really?OpeningBasic

Why they ask: good managers check on wellbeing and early signs of burnout.

Sample answer:

"Honestly, busy but energized. The workload around the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project is heavy this month, and I'm managing it by protecting focus time and pushing low-value meetings. If that changes, I'll tell you early."

Tip: Be honest without venting. Pair any concern with what you're doing about it.

3Where are we on your main priorities?ProgressBasic

Why they ask: they need a crisp picture they can repeat upward.

Sample answer:

"Top priority is the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project: on track, next milestone due this month. Second, day-to-day operations: stable. The main risk is a tight go-live date and one open role on the team; my mitigation is already in motion, and I'll flag it if it moves to red."

Tip: Status, next milestone, risk, ask — in under a minute.

4What did you get done since we last met?ProgressBasic

Why they ask: to track momentum and outcomes.

Sample answer:

"The highlight is cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. I also cleared the backlog item that was blocking the business team and agreed the scope for the next phase of the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project. One thing slipped — I'll explain why and the new date."

Tip: Lead with outcomes, not activity. Own anything that slipped.

5What should you stop doing?PrioritiesIn-depth

Why they ask: to see if you can prioritize instead of just adding work.

Sample answer:

"I'd stop attending the weekly status meeting where I'm not needed — I can send a written update instead. I'd also hand routine requests to the support team so I can focus on a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. That frees about five hours a week."

Tip: Quantify the time you'd free and where it goes.

6Two urgent requests landed at once. Which comes first?PrioritiesBasic

Why they ask: to align on judgment and decision rights.

Sample answer:

"I'd prioritize the one that affects revenue or customers first, which is the issue tied to a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. The other can move two days with no business impact — I've confirmed that with the requester. Are you comfortable with that call?"

Tip: Bring your recommendation and check it, rather than asking them to decide for you.

7Is anything blocking you?BlockersBasic

Why they ask: removing obstacles is one of their main jobs.

Sample answer:

"Yes, one. We're waiting on another team's decision for the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project, and it's been two weeks. I've followed up twice. Could you raise it with their manager? A decision by Friday keeps us on schedule."

Tip: Be specific: the blocker, what you've tried, exactly what you need, and by when.

8What support do you need from me?BlockersBasic

Why they ask: they want to help but need a clear ask.

Sample answer:

"Two things: air cover with leadership on the timeline for the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project, and approval to bring in temporary help for a tight go-live date and one open role on the team. Both protect delivery for a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance."

Tip: Ask for things only they can do — decisions, introductions, air cover, budget.

9Do you have any feedback for me?FeedbackIn-depth

Why they ask: a healthy relationship goes both ways, and it tests whether you can give feedback respectfully.

Sample answer:

"One suggestion: when priorities change, a quick heads-up before the team meeting would help me prepare the team. Otherwise, the way you shielded us during the last escalation made a real difference — thank you."

Tip: One specific, constructive point plus genuine appreciation.

10How do you think you're doing?FeedbackBasic

Why they ask: to calibrate your self-awareness with their view.

Sample answer:

"I think I'm strong on delivery — cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule is a good example. Where I want to grow is leading a larger team within the next year. I'd like to hear how that matches what you're seeing, and what one thing would make the biggest difference."

Tip: Name a strength with evidence and a growth area; then ask for their view.

11Here's some critical feedback. How do you respond?FeedbackIn-depth

Why they ask: your reaction to feedback matters more than the feedback itself.

Sample answer:

"Thank you for telling me directly. Can you share a specific example so I understand the impact? … That makes sense. Here's what I'll do differently starting this week, and I'll check back with you in a month to see if you've noticed a change."

Tip: Listen, ask for an example, don't argue in the moment, commit to an action.

12How should I ask my manager for more feedback?FeedbackBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) regular feedback prevents surprises at review time.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I want to keep improving and avoid surprises at review time. Could we spend five minutes every other 1:1 on one thing I'm doing well and one thing to change? Specific examples help me most."

Tip: Make it easy: small, regular, specific.

13Can you take on this extra project?WorkloadIn-depth

Why they ask: to see if you can say yes thoughtfully or push back with data.

Sample answer:

"I'd like to help. Right now I'm focused on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project and the finance applications and the integrations that feed them. If I take this on, something else needs to move — I'd suggest pausing the lower-priority item so I can give this the attention it deserves. Which would you prefer?"

Tip: Don't say a flat no. Make the trade-off visible and let them choose.

14You seem stretched. What can we change?WorkloadBasic

Why they ask: retention and quality risk.

Sample answer:

"Thanks for noticing. The biggest drain is unplanned requests during project time. Two changes would help: a single intake channel with weekly triage, and moving routine work to the support team. That would protect focus on a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance."

Tip: Offer specific fixes, not general complaints.

15How is the team doing?TeamIn-depth

Why they ask: early warning on morale, conflict and attrition risk.

Sample answer:

"Overall solid. People are proud of cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. Two watch points: one person is close to burning out from on-call load, and we have a single point of failure on a critical system. I'm rotating on-call and starting cross-training."

Tip: Share risks with the actions you're taking, and protect individuals' confidences.

16Anyone on the team I should know about?TeamBasic

Why they ask: recognition, development and risk.

Sample answer:

"Yes — one of our engineers drove most of the work behind cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. A note from you would mean a lot. Also, someone has asked about growth opportunities; I'm building a development plan with them and may need your help finding a stretch assignment."

Tip: Make others visible. It reflects well on you as a leader.

17Where do you want to be in two years?CareerBasic

Why they ask: to support your growth and plan succession.

Sample answer:

"I want to move toward leading a larger team within the next year. To get there I need broader scope — leading a cross-team initiative and more exposure to senior leadership. Could we identify one opportunity this quarter that builds toward that?"

Tip: Be specific about the role and the experiences you need.

18What would you like to learn this year?CareerBasic

Why they ask: development planning.

Sample answer:

"Three areas: deeper financial and business acumen, leading people through change, and one technical area tied to a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. I'd like one formal course and one on-the-job stretch assignment."

Tip: Combine formal learning with real-work stretch.

19How do I bring up promotion with my manager in a 1:1?CareerIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) timing and framing matter.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I'd like to talk about my path to the next level. What does success look like for that role, and where do you see gaps in my readiness? I'd like us to agree a plan and timeline so I can work toward it."

Tip: Ask for the criteria and a plan before asking for the title.

20How do I make my wins visible without bragging?RecognitionBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) good work that nobody sees doesn't count at review time.

Sample answer (what to say):

"Quick update for your leadership report: cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. The business impact was faster processing and fewer escalations. The team did great work — happy to share details if useful."

Tip: Frame wins as business outcomes and credit the team. Send a short monthly summary.

21I disagree with your decision. How do I raise it?DecisionsIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) disagreeing well builds trust.

Sample answer (what to say):

"Can I share a concern about the decision on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project? My worry is the risk to timeline and quality. One alternative is to phase it. If you still prefer the original plan, I'll fully commit to making it work."

Tip: Disagree privately, with data and an alternative — then commit.

22We're going with a direction you argued against. Can you support it?DecisionsBasic

Why they ask: to confirm you'll commit publicly.

Sample answer:

"Yes. I've shared my concerns and you've heard them. I'll support it fully with the team and track the risks I raised, so we can adjust early if needed."

Tip: Disagree and commit. Never undermine a decision with your team.

23Something went wrong. How do you tell me?EscalationBasic

Why they ask: they never want to be surprised by their boss.

Sample answer:

"Heads-up before you hear it elsewhere: we had an issue this morning affecting the business. Impact was limited to two hours. We've restored service, found the cause, and I'll send a short write-up today with prevention steps."

Tip: Bad news early, impact, action taken, next update.

24When should you escalate to me?EscalationBasic

Why they ask: to align on decision rights and expectations.

Sample answer:

"I'll handle anything within my scope and budget. I'll escalate when there's a risk to a committed date, a customer or revenue impact, a cross-team conflict I can't resolve, or a people issue involving policy. Does that match your expectations?"

Tip: Agree the rules explicitly so you both know when to involve them.

25How do you prefer to communicate?RelationshipBasic

Why they ask: working styles affect trust.

Sample answer:

"I prefer a short written update before our 1:1 so we use the time for decisions. For urgent issues, a call or message. How do you prefer to receive updates and escalations?"

Tip: Ask for their preferences and adapt.

26How can I make your job easier?RelationshipIn-depth

Why they ask: a partnership mindset.

Sample answer:

"Keep me informed early on business priorities so I can prepare the team, and let me represent our work directly in a leadership meeting occasionally — I'll keep you fully briefed."

Tip: Ask for things that help you help them.

27Let's set your goals for the next quarter.GoalsBasic

Why they ask: alignment and accountability.

Sample answer:

"I propose three goals: deliver the next milestone of the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project by the agreed date, reduce recurring issues by 20%, and progress on leading a larger team within the next year through one stretch assignment. Each has a clear measure."

Tip: Make goals measurable and tied to business priorities: a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance.

28Are your goals still the right ones?GoalsIn-depth

Why they ask: priorities change mid-year.

Sample answer:

"Mostly. One goal is less relevant since the business moved toward a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. I suggest we replace it with a goal tied to that, so my review reflects the work that matters now."

Tip: Update goals when priorities shift — it protects your rating.

29How do I tell my manager I'm burning out?WellbeingIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) raising it early prevents bigger problems.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I want to be open: the sustained workload over the past few months is affecting my energy. I'm committed to the work, and I'd like to find a sustainable approach — maybe redistributing one area and protecting a few no-meeting blocks."

Tip: Be factual, suggest solutions, and stay focused on sustaining good work.

30How do I ask for time off during a busy period?Time OffBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) planning and coverage.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I'd like to take three days off in two weeks. I've arranged coverage, and the critical work on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project is ahead of schedule. I'll leave a handover note and be reachable for real emergencies."

Tip: Show the coverage plan before asking.

31How do I raise a conflict with a colleague?ConflictIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) managers want you to try first, then involve them.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I'd like your advice. A colleague and I keep clashing over priorities for a shared deliverable. I've talked with them once and we agreed some steps, but it's still slowing us down. Could you help clarify who decides priorities?"

Tip: Show you tried to resolve it, focus on the work impact, and ask for clarity.

32How do you feel about the reorganization?ChangeIn-depth

Why they ask: to gauge your support and risks to the team.

Sample answer:

"I understand the goals and I'm supportive. My questions are about decision rights and how the finance applications and the integrations that feed them will be covered during the transition. I'll help the team through it — clear answers on those points would help."

Tip: Be supportive, raise practical concerns, and offer to help.

33Can you present this to leadership?VisibilityBasic

Why they ask: a growth and visibility opportunity.

Sample answer:

"Yes, I'd welcome that. I'll prepare a one-page summary focused on business outcomes and decisions needed, and I'd like 15 minutes with you to rehearse."

Tip: Say yes, prepare carefully, and rehearse.

34What did you learn from that project?LearningBasic

Why they ask: reflection and growth.

Sample answer:

"Two lessons from the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project: involve the business earlier in testing, and agree decision owners at the start. I've built both into our plan for the next phase."

Tip: Share lessons and how you're applying them.

35Any ideas to improve how we work?IdeasBasic

Why they ask: initiative and ownership.

Sample answer:

"One idea: a monthly review of our top recurring issues to fix root causes. I estimate it would cut repeat tickets by a quarter. I can pilot it next month and report back."

Tip: Offer an idea with estimated impact and volunteer to lead.

36I made a mistake. How do I tell my manager?MistakesBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) ownership builds trust.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I need to flag a mistake I made. I missed a step in yesterday's change, which caused a short issue for the business. It's fixed, the impact was small, and I've added a checklist step so it won't happen again."

Tip: Own it quickly, explain impact and fix, show prevention.

37What does great look like in your role?ExpectationsBasic

Why they ask: to align on standards.

Sample answer:

"For Senior ERP Analyst, I see great as: business stakeholders trust us, operations are stable, projects like the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project land on time, and the team is growing. Is that how you'd define it, or would you add anything?"

Tip: Propose your definition and confirm theirs.

38How do we stay aligned when working remotely?RemoteBasic

Why they ask: communication gaps grow in remote work.

Sample answer:

"A short written weekly update on priorities, progress and risks, our regular 1:1, and a quick message for anything urgent. I'll over-communicate on decisions so nothing falls through the cracks."

Tip: Make your work visible in writing.

39How do I raise pay concerns in a 1:1?CompensationIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) pay conversations need preparation.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I'd like to discuss my compensation. Based on my scope — the finance applications and the integrations that feed them — and market data for similar roles, I believe I'm below market. Could we review this together, and what's the process and timing?"

Tip: Use scope and market data, not personal needs. Ask about the process.

40Anything else before we wrap up?ClosingBasic

Why they ask: a last chance to raise something important.

Sample answer:

"Just to confirm next steps: you'll raise the blocker with the other team's manager, and I'll send the updated plan for the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project by Thursday. Thanks — this was helpful."

Tip: Recap actions and owners. Send a two-line follow-up note.

41How should I prepare for a 1:1?PreparationBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) preparation makes the meeting useful.

Sample answer (approach):

  • Update a running document with wins, priorities, risks and asks.
  • Pick 2–4 items, decisions first.
  • Bring one development or feedback topic each month.
  • Note action items and follow up in writing.

Tip: A shared running doc builds a record you can use at review time.

42How do I rebuild trust after a rough patch?TrustIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) relationships can recover with consistency.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I know the last few weeks haven't gone smoothly, and I want to fix that. I'll send a short status update every Friday and flag issues early. Is there anything specific that would help rebuild your confidence?"

Tip: Acknowledge, commit to visible behaviors, and follow through consistently.

43My manager is new. What should our first 1:1 cover?New ManagerBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) first impressions with a new manager matter.

Sample answer (what to say):

"Welcome. Let me give you a quick picture: I'm Senior ERP Analyst, I own the finance applications and the integrations that feed them, current priorities are the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project, and our recent win was cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. I'd love to understand your priorities and how you like to work."

Tip: Brief them on scope, priorities and wins, then listen.

44What are you delegating to your team?DelegationIn-depth

Why they ask: leaders scale through others.

Sample answer:

"I've delegated day-to-day issue triage to a senior team member, with a weekly check-in. That frees me to focus on the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project and on stakeholder management, and it gives them a growth opportunity."

Tip: Show delegation as both scaling and developing people.

45What do you think we should focus on next year?StrategyIn-depth

Why they ask: strategic thinking beyond your current tasks.

Sample answer:

"Three areas: stabilizing and automating operations, finishing the supplier onboarding upgrade and the month-end close automation project so the business sees the benefits, and building skills in the areas that support a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance. That sets us up well for growth."

Tip: Tie your view to business priorities.

46How are things with the business teams?StakeholdersBasic

Why they ask: relationships with stakeholders affect your team's reputation.

Sample answer:

"Mostly strong — they appreciated how we handled cutting invoice processing time by about 30% and closing the last two quarters on schedule. One stakeholder is frustrated about delivery timelines; I've set up a biweekly review with them to make priorities transparent."

Tip: Share the good and the friction, with your plan.

47How do I thank my manager without seeming fake?RecognitionBasic

Why they ask: (your own question) specific appreciation strengthens relationships.

Sample answer (what to say):

"I wanted to say thanks for backing me in that leadership meeting last week. It helped the team see their work valued, and it made the decision much easier to land."

Tip: Be specific about what they did and its impact.

48What's one thing you'd change if you were in my shoes?OwnershipIn-depth

Why they ask: perspective and leadership thinking.

Sample answer:

"I'd create clearer intake and prioritization across teams, so urgent requests don't keep disrupting planned work. It would help everyone deliver a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance more predictably."

Tip: Offer a constructive, system-level idea.

49My manager mentioned a retention bonus. What should I ask?Retention BonusIn-depth

Why they ask: (your own question) retention bonuses come with conditions.

Sample answer (what to say):

"Thank you, I appreciate it. Could you share the amount, when it's paid, and any conditions — for example, if I'd need to repay it if I leave within a certain time? I'd also like to keep discussing my path toward leading a larger team within the next year."

Tip: Ask for the terms in writing. Remember bonuses are taxed as supplemental wages.

50How do you decide what to say no to?PrioritiesBasic

Why they ask: judgment under competing demands.

Sample answer:

"I check each request against a reliable close, fewer manual steps and faster delivery for finance and the commitments we've already made. If something doesn't move those forward and isn't urgent, I propose a later date or a lighter alternative, and I tell the requester why."

Tip: Use agreed priorities as the filter.
