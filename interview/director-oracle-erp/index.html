1What are the key responsibilities of a Director of Oracle ERP?RoleBasicTop question

Why they ask: to check your view of the role's scope.

Answer:

"I own the ERP platform, both delivery and run: Financials, SCM, and often the HCM and EPM interfaces. I own the roadmap and portfolio, prioritized with business leaders. I lead the organization: managers and associate directors, analysts, architects and partners. I own the budget for run and change, and the vendor contracts. I run governance: change control, release management, SOX ITGCs and security. I partner with the CFO organization and supply chain and procurement leaders. And I'm accountable for service: SLAs, stability and user satisfaction."

Tip: Mention both delivery and run. Directors own the whole life cycle.

Likely follow-up

What do you delegate and what do you keep?

I delegate delivery and day-to-day run to my managers, and keep the roadmap, budget, governance, vendor strategy and executive relationships. I still stay close enough to delivery to spot risk early — a monthly deep-dive on the biggest program — but I don't run the project plans myself.

2How would you build a 2-year ERP roadmap?StrategyBasicTop question

Why they ask: strategic planning.

Answer:

"Inputs are the business strategy, pain points, support data, Oracle's roadmap, technical debt and audit findings. I'd organize it in themes: stabilize, automate AP, close and procurement, analytics with Fusion Data Intelligence, AI agents, new capabilities like revenue or planning, and M&A readiness. I prioritize by value, risk and dependencies, with quick wins in the first six months. There's a capacity and budget plan. Business leaders review it quarterly. And each initiative has metrics."

Tip: Mention quick wins in six months. They earn trust for the rest of the plan.

Likely follow-up

How do you keep the roadmap from becoming a wish list?

Every item needs a named business owner, a measurable benefit and a place in our capacity plan. If something has no owner or no benefit, it goes to a backlog, not the roadmap. Then the roadmap is reviewed each quarter with business leaders, so it reflects real priorities.

3What would you do in your first 100 days as Director of ERP?StrategyBasicTop question

Why they ask: a plan shows leadership maturity.

Answer:

"First I listen: business leaders, the team, partners and audit, and I sit in on close and key operations. Then I assess stability, the backlog, the roadmap, team capabilities, vendor contracts, budget and risks. I deliver quick wins on the pain points the business sees most. I clarify roles, governance and intake. And I agree 12-month priorities and KPIs with the CIO and CFO."

Tip: Mention sitting in on close. It shows you'll learn the business, not just IT.

Likely follow-up

What would you deliver by day 100?

A current-state assessment — platform health, team, vendors, spend — a draft roadmap agreed with the CFO and CIO, and two or three visible quick wins on the top pain points, like cutting invoice holds or fixing a recurring close issue. Quick wins buy credibility for bigger changes.

4How would you structure an ERP team of 25 people?OrganizationIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: organization design.

Answer:

"I'd align teams to processes: Record-to-Report, Procure-to-Pay, Order-to-Cash, Supply Chain, and HCM if in scope. A platform team covers shared services: integrations on OIC, reporting and analytics, security and release management. An architecture role spans the teams. Partners provide surge or specialist skills, and AMS covers L2 where it's economical. Each team has a manager or lead with clear ownership and career paths."

Tip: Explain process alignment. It matches how the business thinks.

Likely follow-up

Why align teams to processes instead of modules?

Processes cross modules — procure-to-pay touches purchasing, receiving, payables and GL — so process teams own outcomes end to end and reduce handoffs between module silos. A P2P team can fix invoice holds properly instead of AP and purchasing teams blaming each other.

5Insource vs outsource: how do you decide the right mix for ERP support?OrganizationIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a sourcing strategy question.

Answer:

"I keep core knowledge in-house: architecture, business process ownership, critical modules and vendor management. I outsource routine L1 and L2 support, surge capacity, specialist skills like payroll legislation, and 24-by-7 coverage. I weigh cost, quality, knowledge risk, speed and compliance. Contracts include SLAs, knowledge transfer and exit clauses. And I review the mix every year."

Tip: Mention exit clauses. They keep vendors honest.

Likely follow-up

What should never be outsourced?

Architecture, process ownership, and knowledge of critical customizations and integrations. Partners can provide capacity and specialist skills, but if they own the design knowledge, you can't change partners or make good decisions without them. Keep the brains in-house.

6How do you partner with the CFO organization?Business PartnershipBasic

Why they ask: CFO partnership is central to the role.

Answer:

"Regular forums: monthly with the controller or VP Finance, quarterly roadmap reviews with the CFO. I understand finance goals: close speed, controls, cost and forecast accuracy. We share KPIs and track value together. Ownership is clear: process owners sit in finance, IT owns the platform. I bring proactive ideas, automation, analytics and AI. And I'm visible during close and audit."

Tip: Mention joint ownership. It prevents the "IT's system" problem.

Likely follow-up

How do you know the CFO relationship is healthy?

When finance brings problems to you early instead of working around IT.

7How do you say no to a senior business leader?Business PartnershipBasic

Why they ask: directors must protect priorities.

Answer:

"I start by understanding the underlying need. I explain the constraints and trade-offs: cost, risk and other priorities. I offer alternatives, like a phased approach, a standard feature or a workaround. I use governance for prioritization, so it's not a personal rejection. And I follow up to keep the relationship strong."

Tip: Say "use governance, not personal rejection". It protects relationships.

Likely follow-up

How do you keep the relationship after saying no?

Offer an alternative or a timeline, so it's not a flat refusal.

8How do you set up ERP governance?GovernanceIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: governance structure.

Answer:

"A steering committee of business and IT executives for the roadmap and budget. A design authority for solution decisions. A change advisory board for production changes. An intake and prioritization process with business process owners. Release management for quarterly updates and enhancements. Data governance for master data. And security and compliance reviews."

Tip: Keep each forum's purpose distinct. Overlapping forums slow decisions.

Likely follow-up

Which governance body matters most?

The design authority, because it protects the platform from uncontrolled customization. Steering committees decide priorities and money, but the design authority decides how things are built — and that's what keeps quarterly updates and support costs manageable for years.

9How do you manage SOX compliance for the ERP environment?GovernanceIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: compliance accountability.

Answer:

"IT general controls: access management with provisioning, reviews and privileged access; change management with approval, testing, segregation and an emergency process; and operations with job and interface monitoring. Application controls in configuration, documented and tested. SoD monitoring and remediation. An evidence repository and an audit calendar. And a good relationship with internal and external audit, with findings remediated quickly."

Tip: Mention the audit calendar. It turns compliance into routine.

Likely follow-up

What's the most common ERP SOX finding?

Access issues — terminated users still active, generic accounts, and segregation-of-duties conflicts like a user who can create suppliers and pay them. Automating deprovisioning from HR and running quarterly access reviews fixes most of them.

10How do you build and manage the ERP budget?BudgetBasic

Why they ask: financial ownership.

Answer:

I optimize through license rationalization, vendor rates and automation that cuts support costs. And I communicate the value delivered against spend."

Tip: Mention showing value against spend. Budgets are easier to defend that way.

Likely follow-up

How do you defend the budget?

By linking spend to business outcomes and risk reduction.

11How do you select and manage a systems integrator for an Oracle program?VendorsIn-depth

Why they ask: SI selection is high-stakes.

Answer:

"An RFP with clear scope, requirements and evaluation criteria: industry and module experience, team quality, methodology, price and references. Orals with the proposed team members, using scenario-based questions. A contract choice between fixed price and time and materials, with milestones tied to deliverables, key personnel commitments, quality gates and warranty. Governance through a joint PMO with weekly and monthly reviews and KPIs. And knowledge transfer and transition to support."

Tip: Mention meeting the proposed team. Proposals are sold by A-teams and staffed by others.

Likely follow-up

What's the most important SI selection criterion?

The actual team they'll staff, not the sales team.

13How do you ensure large ERP projects deliver value, not just go-live?DeliveryBasicTop question

Why they ask: value delivery.

Answer:

"A business case with measurable benefits and named owners. Benefit tracking after go-live, like close days and touchless rate. A focus on adoption: training, process changes and KPIs. A post-implementation review at three to six months. And a continuous improvement backlog."

Tip: Mention named benefit owners in the business. IT can't own business benefits alone.

Likely follow-up

Who owns the benefits?

Named business owners who sign up for the benefits, with targets and dates, and track them for 6 to 12 months after go-live — for example close days, touchless invoice rate or inventory turns. IT can deliver capability; only the business can deliver the benefit.

14A major ERP project is red: over budget and behind schedule. What do you do?DeliveryIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: program recovery.

Answer:

"I start with an independent health check of scope, plan, quality, team and vendor performance. I find the root causes: unclear requirements, poor estimates, data issues or resourcing. Then options: de-scope, phase, re-baseline, add or replace resources, or change the vendor's approach. The decision is made with the steering committee and sponsor. We re-plan with tighter governance and leading indicators. And I communicate transparently."

Tip: Mention an independent health check. Teams in trouble rarely see the whole picture.

Likely follow-up

What's the hardest decision on a red project?

Whether to reset scope, change the vendor team, or move the date — usually some mix. It's hard because each choice has a cost and someone loses face. I'd decide based on an independent assessment, not on who argues loudest, and communicate it quickly and openly.

16How do you prepare for and run quarterly Oracle updates across ERP?OperationsBasic

Answer:

A go/no-go before the production update, with any issues raised to Oracle. Communication and training. And hypercare and a review after the update."

Tip: Mention the go/no-go. It's how you stop a bad update reaching production.

Likely follow-up

Month-end close and payroll dates.

Tip: Mention working with the CISO. Security isn't the ERP team's alone.

19What is your AI strategy for the ERP platform?AIIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a leadership vision question.

Answer:

"Use Oracle's embedded AI first, agents, document recognition and predictions, because it's lower cost and risk. Prioritize use cases with measurable value: AP processing, collections, close insights and procurement assistance. Govern it with risk assessments, human-in-the-loop for financial actions, auditability and security. Run pilots with metrics and scale the ones that work. Upskill the team on AI Agent Studio and prompt design. And manage the change for users."

Tip: Start with "embedded first". It shows cost and risk awareness.

Likely follow-up

Where would you start with AI?

Embedded Oracle AI on high-volume processes like AP invoice capture, coding and matching, where accuracy is easy to measure and the benefit shows up in cost per invoice. Then expand to collections, close and procurement, with clear data, security and human-approval rules.

20How would you integrate an acquired company's ERP?M&AIn-depth

Why they ask: M&A is common.

Answer:

"Day one is reporting through a consolidation mapping, with no system changes. Then I assess systems, processes, data, contracts and people. The decision is to migrate to the Oracle template, use a TSA, or rarely keep their system. The plan is a template-based migration covering data, integrations, security and controls. The timeline follows synergy targets and the close calendar. And I retain key people with knowledge from the acquired company."

Tip: Mention retaining their key people. Knowledge walks out the door otherwise.

Likely follow-up

What's the day-one priority?

Consolidated reporting through mapping, not migration.

26How do you decide between EBS upgrade/extension and moving to Oracle Fusion Cloud?StrategyIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a major strategic decision.

Answer:

"I look at business drivers: the pace of innovation, AI, user experience, the cost of running EBS and talent availability. EBS support timelines and infrastructure costs. The level of customization in EBS, which raises migration effort but also run cost. Total cost of ownership over five to seven years for both paths. And the organization's risk appetite and capacity for change. A hybrid is an option, for example Fusion Financials first and EBS SCM later. Then I present a recommendation with a phased plan."

Tip: Present it as a recommendation with phases. Executives want a path, not a debate.

Likely follow-up

What's the strongest reason to move to Fusion?

Access to continuous innovation — quarterly updates, embedded AI and agents — without large upgrade projects every few years. EBS can keep running, but every new capability is something you'd build yourself. The case is strongest when EBS is heavily customized and nearing a major upgrade anyway.

27How do you build the business case for an ERP investment?StrategyIn-depth

Why they ask: investment justification.

Answer:

"A problem statement and strategic alignment. Benefits: efficiency through freed capacity, working capital through DSO and inventory, cost avoidance from retiring legacy, risk reduction through controls, and revenue enablement. Costs: subscriptions, implementation, the internal team, change management and run costs. NPV, ROI and payback, with sensitivity analysis. Non-financial benefits. And named benefit owners in the business."

Tip: Mention sensitivity analysis. CFOs test the assumptions.

Likely follow-up

What benefit is most credible?

Freed capacity measured against a baseline.

29What are your go/no-go criteria for an ERP go-live?DeliveryIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: go-live discipline.

Answer:

"Testing: SIT and UAT completed, no open critical or high defects, and workarounds documented for mediums. Data: final mock reconciled with business sign-off. Cutover rehearsal within the planned time. Integrations tested with partners. Security: roles provisioned and tested, and SoD reviewed. Training completed and super users ready. A support model ready with hypercare and a war room. Business readiness confirmed by process owners. And a rollback plan."

Tip: Mention business readiness confirmed by process owners. IT readiness alone isn't enough.

Likely follow-up

Which criterion is non-negotiable?

Successful payment and payroll tests — payments reaching the bank in the right format and payroll calculating to the cent. If people or suppliers aren't paid correctly on day one, nothing else matters. Other criteria can sometimes be accepted with workarounds; those can't.

30How do you run hypercare after a major go-live?OperationsBasic

Why they ask: stabilization leadership.

Answer:

"A war room with business and IT and daily stand-ups. Triage by business impact with fast decisions. Monitoring of critical processes: payments, shipping and close. A KPI dashboard of tickets, backlog and critical process status. Communication to users and leadership. And exit criteria for moving to steady-state support."

Tip: Mention exit criteria. Hypercare without an end becomes permanent.

Likely follow-up

When does hypercare end?

After stable closes and incident volumes back to normal.

Only with a legal or strong business case.
