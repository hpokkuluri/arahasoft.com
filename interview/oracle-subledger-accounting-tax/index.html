Oracle Subledger Accounting and Tax Interview Questions

The questions interviewers ask for Oracle Subledger Accounting, Accounting Hub and Fusion Tax work, with why they ask, a full answer you can say in the room, and a tip. Tap a question to see the answer.

1Explain the SLA hierarchy: accounting method, rule sets and rules.SLAIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: SLA is the heart of Fusion accounting. They want to know you can find your way around it.

Answer:

"At the top is the subledger accounting method, like Standard Accrual, assigned to a ledger. It groups the rules for every subledger. Under it are journal entry rule sets, one per event class, for example Payables Invoices. Inside a rule set are journal line rules that say debit or credit, the accounting class and conditions; account derivation rules that build each account; description rules; and supporting references. Sources and mapping sets feed the rules with data. After any change I activate the rule set, which validates and compiles it. And I never edit the seeded method; I copy it first."

Tip: Say you copy the seeded method. Interviewers listen for it.

Likely follow-up

Why copy the seeded method instead of editing it?

Seeded methods can't be edited and are maintained by Oracle in each update. A copy lets you change just the rules you need while keeping the rest close to standard. When Oracle adds new event classes to the seeded method, you review and bring them into your copy.

2What is the difference between "Create Accounting" draft, final and final post?SLABasicTop question

Why they ask: a basic SLA question, but using the wrong mode in production causes real problems.

Answer:

"Draft creates entries you can review. They aren't transferred to GL and can be deleted and recreated, so I use draft to test a rule change. Final locks the entries and, if transfer is selected, sends them to GL. Final post does that and also posts them in GL in the same run. In production most companies schedule final with transfer, and post in GL as a separate controlled step, so someone reviews the batch first."

Tip: Explain why many companies don't use final post. It shows you've seen real close controls.

Likely follow-up

When would you use final without posting?

When GL posting is controlled by GL users. The entries transfer to GL as unposted journals, so the GL team can review, for example, large AP or AR batches before posting. Final post is more common when you trust the subledger and want a faster close.

3How do account derivation rules build an account?SLAIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: account derivation rules are the most common SLA customization.

Answer:

"An ADR can build the whole account combination, one segment, or use a value set. Conditions decide which line of the rule applies, for example by supplier type or item category, and the first condition that matches wins, so the order matters. The value can come from a constant, a source like a transaction attribute or a descriptive flexfield, or a mapping set. The pattern I use most is to start from the default account, for example from the invoice distribution, and override only the segment that needs to change, like the natural account from a mapping set, while the cost center still comes from the transaction."

Tip: Say "override only the segment that needs to change". It's the cleanest design and interviewers know it.

Likely follow-up

What happens if no condition in an ADR matches?

The rule returns no value, so accounting errors out for that transaction. I always end an ADR with a default line without conditions — often pointing to a suspense or review account — so accounting completes and the exception is easy to find and correct.

4How do you add a custom source to SLA rules?SLAIn-depth

Why they ask: custom sources come up when the business needs accounting by a field Oracle doesn't provide.

Answer:

"The common way is a descriptive flexfield on the transaction, for example on the invoice line, which SLA can expose as a source. Where it's supported, I can also create a custom source built from existing sources or a formula. Then I assign it to the event class and use it in a condition or an account derivation rule. In testing I always include transactions where the field is filled and where it's blank, because the blank case is what breaks accounting in production."

Tip: Mention testing the blank case. It shows experience.

Likely follow-up

Why test a custom source in draft first?

To confirm the value flows correctly before it affects final accounting that can't be edited.

5What are accounting classes and why do they matter?SLABasic

Why they ask: a basic SLA concept that shows up in reporting and rule design.

Answer:

"Every journal line gets an accounting class, like Liability, Item Expense, Receivable, Revenue, Gain, Loss or Cash Clearing. They're used in reports, in third-party balances, in reconciliation reports, and in rule conditions, for example changing the account only for the Liability class. In testing I check the accounting class on every line, because it tells me the rule fired on the right line."

Tip: Mention using them in testing. It turns a definition into practice.

Likely follow-up

Where do accounting classes help in reconciliation?

Reconciliation reports filter by class, like Liability for AP, so you compare subledger and GL for the right lines.

6How do you correct accounting that was wrong after Create Accounting in final mode?SLAIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a real-world problem. They want the right order of fixes.

Answer:

"Final entries can't be edited, so I have three options. The best is to fix the source transaction, for example adjust or reverse the invoice distribution, which creates new events and correct accounting with a full trail. If that's not possible, I create a manual subledger journal entry in SLA, which keeps the audit trail in the subledger. A GL journal is the last resort, because it breaks the subledger-to-GL reconciliation unless it's carefully documented. Then I fix the rule so future transactions are right."

Tip: Give the fixes in order of preference and finish with fixing the rule.

Likely follow-up

Why not just post a GL journal to fix it?

A GL journal fixes the ledger but not the subledger, so the subledger and GL stay out of sync and the AP or AR to GL reconciliation shows a permanent difference. It's better to correct the transaction or use a subledger adjustment so both sides agree.

7What is the purpose of supporting references and third-party balances?SLAIn-depth

Why they ask: these features help reconciliation, and many consultants don't use them.

Answer:

"Supporting references store extra attributes on journal lines, like contract, project or supplier, and SLA keeps balances by those values. That lets me reconcile or analyze an account by something that isn't in the chart of accounts. Third-party balances are balances by supplier or customer on control accounts, so I can report and reconcile what we owe each supplier or what each customer owes us directly from the accounting. I've used supporting references to reconcile intercompany accounts by counterparty."

Tip: Give one real use, like intercompany reconciliation.

Likely follow-up

Give an example of using supporting references.

Tracking balances by supplier on an accrual account, so you can see which supplier's receipt accrual is still open.

8What diagnostics help with SLA issues?SLAIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: troubleshooting SLA is a daily support task.

Answer:

"I start with the Create Accounting execution report, which lists errors by event. Then I look at the subledger journal entry, with view accounting and the account derivation details, to see which rule built which account. If that's not enough, I enable the SLA diagnostics profile and run the accounting event diagnostics report, which shows the actual source values the rules used. That usually answers why a rule didn't fire. And I run the unaccounted transactions reports by subledger to see the full scope of the problem."

Tip: Mention the diagnostics report showing source values. It's the tool that actually solves the hard cases.

Likely follow-up

What's the most common SLA error you see?

Invalid account combinations from cross-validation rules or inactive or end-dated segment values. The fix belongs in the value or the rule, not the transaction. I check the Create Accounting execution report, then the specific segment, then rerun accounting for the failed items.

9Explain the Fusion Tax configuration hierarchy.TaxIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: tax setup is complex, and they want to see you understand how the pieces nest.

Answer:

"It goes tax regime, then tax, then tax status, then tax rate, with tax jurisdictions defining the geography. For example, the UK VAT regime has a VAT tax, with statuses like standard, reduced and zero, each with rates and effective dates. Tax rules then decide place of supply, whether tax applies, which status and rate, the taxable basis, the calculation and recovery. Parties need tax profiles and registrations, legal entities, suppliers and customers. Items and services need product tax classifications. And the configuration owner decides whether the setup is global or per legal entity."

Tip: Use one real regime as the example. It makes the hierarchy easy to follow.

Likely follow-up

What's tax determination used for?

Tax rules decide which regime, tax, status and rate apply based on the parties, place of supply, product and transaction type, so users don't pick tax manually. For example, a US company selling to a German business customer gets reverse-charge VAT automatically.

10How do you handle VAT recovery on purchases?TaxIn-depth

Why they ask: recovery affects cost and the VAT return.

Answer:

"Each tax rate has a recovery rate, 100%, partial or zero, and rules can override it, for example entertainment is non-recoverable in many countries. Recoverable VAT goes to the input VAT account and is claimed on the return. Non-recoverable VAT goes to the expense or becomes part of the asset cost. For mixed-use businesses, like a company with both taxable and exempt sales, partial recovery rates apply. The VAT return is built from the tax reporting ledger."

Tip: Give the entertainment example. It shows you've set up a real recovery rule.

Likely follow-up

How is non-recoverable VAT accounted?

It's added to the expense or asset cost of the line instead of going to input VAT.

11What is the tax reporting ledger and how is it used?TaxIn-depth

Why they ask: tax returns depend on it.

Answer:

"The tax reporting ledger stores tax lines from AP, AR, GL and other sources, with all the attributes needed for tax returns: VAT returns, sales and purchase registers, and EC sales lists. I run Select Tax Reporting Data or the extract, then the country reports. It's also how I reconcile the tax accounts in GL to what's reported, which is the check every tax team wants before filing."

Tip: Mention the reconciliation to GL. It's what tax teams actually care about.

Likely follow-up

What do you check before filing a VAT return?

That all transactions are accounted and in the tax reporting ledger for the period, and that the return reconciles to GL tax accounts.

12How do withholding taxes work in Fusion Payables?TaxIn-depth

Why they ask: important for Latin America, India and parts of Europe.

Answer:

"I set up a withholding tax regime with codes and rates, and mark which supplier sites are subject to it. At invoice validation or at payment, depending on the setup, Oracle withholds the tax from what we pay the supplier and creates a withholding tax invoice payable to the tax authority, which is set up as a supplier. Then certificates are issued to suppliers and reports are produced for each jurisdiction. In India, for example, TDS withholding is standard on many services."

Tip: Mention that the tax authority is a supplier. It's a detail that shows you've set it up.

Likely follow-up

When is withholding calculated at payment instead of invoice?

When local rules require withholding when payment is made, often the case in several Latin American and Asian countries.

13What are e-invoicing mandates and how do they affect Oracle?TaxIn-depth

Why they ask: e-invoicing is spreading fast, and global projects now plan for it from day one.

Answer:

"More and more countries require invoices to be sent to, or cleared by, the tax authority electronically: Mexico CFDI, Italy SDI, India IRN, Saudi ZATCA, PEPPOL-based regimes, and the EU's ViDA requirements coming in. In Oracle that means localizations or partner integrations that generate the invoice in the required format, submit it, receive the acknowledgment and store the legal document. I plan it country by country with the tax and legal teams, and test it early, because in some countries an invoice isn't legally valid until it's cleared."

Tip: Say "test early". In clearance countries, a late failure means you can't bill.

Likely follow-up

How does Oracle support these mandates?

Through its e-invoicing features for some countries, or partner solutions connected through OIC, depending on the country and release.

14How do you implement Fusion Accounting Hub for a non-Oracle source?Accounting HubIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: Accounting Hub projects are specialist work. They want the steps.

Answer:

"First I register a subledger application and define the event model: entities, event classes and event types. Then I define the sources, the transaction attributes the source system will send, and assign them to the event classes. Then I build the accounting rules, journal line rules, account derivation rules and mapping sets, and an accounting method. Transactions load through FBDI or REST, and Create Accounting turns them into journals for GL. Finally I reconcile source system to Accounting Hub to GL, and set up drill-down so finance can trace a GL balance back to the source transaction."

Tip: Name the event model first. Everything else depends on getting it right.

Likely follow-up

What's the hardest part of an Accounting Hub implementation?

Defining the event model and the sources with the source-system team, so every transaction carries the attributes the accounting rules need — product, region, customer type. If a needed attribute isn't in the feed, no amount of rule design can produce the right account.

15Why use Accounting Hub instead of summarized GL journal imports?Accounting HubIn-depth

Why they ask: a design trade-off question.

Answer:

"With Accounting Hub, finance controls the accounting rules in one place instead of having them hard-coded in each source system. The same transactions can produce accounting for more than one GAAP. You get a detailed audit trail, drill-down and supporting references for reporting, and reconciliation to the source is much easier. The trade-off is more design work and more data volume. If a source system already produces clean accounting and only needs a monthly summary, a GL journal import is simpler."

Tip: Give the case where you wouldn't use it. It shows balanced judgment.

Likely follow-up

When would summarized journals be fine?

When the source has simple, stable accounting and nobody needs drill-down to transactions.

16Give an example of an SLA rule change you made and how you tested it.InterviewBasicTop question

Why they ask: they want proof you've changed SLA in a controlled way.

Answer:

"The business wanted AP liability for intercompany suppliers on a separate account from third-party suppliers. I copied the seeded accounting method, created a mapping set from supplier type to natural account, and an account derivation rule on the liability line that overrides only the natural account. I tested in draft first, then final in the test environment, covering invoice, payment, cancellation, prepayment application and credit memo, because each event touches the liability. Then I documented the change, got approval, and kept the test evidence for audit."

Tip: List the events you tested. That's what separates a real answer from a theoretical one.

Likely follow-up

How did you prove the change didn't affect other suppliers?

I ran draft accounting for a mix of intercompany and third-party invoices — before and after the change — and compared the accounts line by line. Only the intercompany invoices changed; the others matched exactly. That comparison went into the change ticket as evidence.

17What is Fusion Accounting Hub (FAH) and when would you use it?Accounting HubIn-depth

Why they ask: to see if you know when to bring in Accounting Hub.

Answer:

"Accounting Hub lets you create subledger accounting from non-Oracle systems, like banking, insurance policy administration, billing or loans, using the same SLA rules engine Fusion uses. You define a subledger application, an event model, sources and accounting rules, load transactions through FBDI or REST, and run Create Accounting to produce journals in GL with drill-down to the source details. It supports more than one GAAP, for example IFRS 17 and US GAAP, through separate accounting methods. I'd use it when the source system can't produce good accounting, or when finance needs to control the rules in one place."

Tip: Give an industry example, like insurance or banking. It anchors the answer.

Likely follow-up

What's the alternative to Accounting Hub?

Importing summarized GL journals directly from the source systems. It's simpler and cheaper, but you lose rule-based control over accounting, consistent account derivation and drill-down from GL to the source transaction. For high-volume billing or insurance systems where auditors need that trail, Accounting Hub is the better design.

18How would you architect indirect tax for a global Fusion implementation?TaxIn-depth

Why they ask: a senior design question across many countries.

Answer:

"I'd use Fusion Tax natively for VAT and GST countries, where rules-based setup works well, and a partner engine like Vertex, Avalara or Sovos for US sales and use tax, and possibly worldwide if the tax team prefers one engine. E-invoicing mandates, like Italy SDI, Mexico CFDI, India e-invoicing, PEPPOL and EU ViDA, go through Oracle localizations or partners. Tax returns and SAF-T come from the tax reporting ledger or the partner. Master data matters: party tax profiles, registrations and product tax classifications. And governance: the tax team owns the rules, changes go through change control, and every rate change gets a regression test."

Tip: Separate US sales tax from VAT. It shows you understand why most companies use a partner for the US.

Likely follow-up

Why mix native tax and a partner engine?

Native works well for VAT regimes, while US sales tax with thousands of jurisdictions needs a partner engine.

19How would you design SLA for complex revenue and cost accounting across multiple GAAPs?Subledger AccountingIn-depth

Why they ask: a senior SLA design question.

Answer:

"I'd start by listing every event class that needs non-standard accounting. Then I'd copy the seeded method once per GAAP, say US GAAP and IFRS, and reuse as much as possible between them, like shared account derivation rules and mapping sets. The second GAAP runs in a secondary ledger with its own method at subledger level. I'd use custom sources sparingly and prefer seeded sources and descriptive flexfields. Supporting references, like contract ID or product, help reconciliation. Every rule gets documented with the business reason and tested with an accounting matrix. And governance is clear: finance owns the rules, IT owns deployment."

Tip: Mention the accounting matrix. It's the test document auditors and controllers want to see.

Likely follow-up

How do you keep multi-GAAP SLA maintainable?

One method per GAAP copied from seeded, with shared mapping sets and clear documentation.

20What is Create Accounting and why does it matter?Subledger AccountingBasic

Why they ask: a basic question for anyone working in Fusion finance.

Answer:

"Subledger transactions, like invoices, receipts and asset additions, don't reach GL by themselves. Create Accounting runs Subledger Accounting to build the journal entries from those events, and can then transfer and post them to GL. It runs in draft, which you can review and change, or final, which is locked. Errors, like an invalid account, show up in the execution report, and you fix them and rerun. I schedule it daily rather than only at month-end, so problems surface early instead of on the last day of close."

Tip: Say "daily, not month-end". It's the practical point that shows experience.

Likely follow-up

What's the difference between Create Accounting in draft and final?

Draft accounting creates entries you can review but doesn't send anything to GL, and you can rerun it after fixing data. Final accounting locks the entries, so the transaction can't change, and can transfer and post to GL. Most period-end runs are final; draft is for checking first.

21How do you find out why a transaction did not create accounting?Subledger AccountingIn-depth

Why they ask: a common support question with a clear method.

Answer:

"I check the Create Accounting execution report for the error, and the transaction's accounting status, for example an invoice showing Unaccounted. The common causes are: the transaction isn't validated or approved; the subledger period is closed; the account combination is invalid or disabled; a cross-validation rule failed; or a mapping set has no value for this case. Review Subledger Journal Entries shows any draft entries with errors. Once I fix the cause, I rerun Create Accounting for that transaction or batch."

Tip: Give the list of causes in the order you'd check them. It's the method they want.

Likely follow-up

What if the error is an invalid account combination?

Look at cross-validation rules and whether a segment value is inactive, end-dated or not allowed for posting. For example, a cost center created in GL but not enabled for the account combination. Fix the value or the rule, then rerun Create Accounting for just those transactions.

22How is tax calculated on transactions in Oracle Fusion?TaxIn-depth

Why they ask: a basic tax question for finance consultants.

Answer:

"Fusion Tax uses a hierarchy: tax regime, like US Sales Tax or UK VAT, then tax, tax status, tax rate and tax jurisdiction. Tax rules decide whether a tax applies and which rate, based on the parties, locations, product and transaction details. Parties need tax profiles and registrations. Tax is calculated on AP invoices, AR transactions and purchase orders and shown on separate tax lines with their own accounts. For US sales tax, many companies use a partner like Vertex or Avalara instead."

Tip: Mention the partner option for the US. It's the common real-world setup.

Likely follow-up

How do you test a new tax rule?

Use the tax simulator with sample transactions to see which rule and rate apply before going live.

23Explain Subledger Accounting (SLA) and give an example of a rule you customized.Subledger AccountingIn-depth

Why they ask: SLA is a favorite senior question, and the example proves you've done it.

Answer:

"SLA turns subledger events, like an AP invoice being validated or a receipt being applied, into accounting entries before they go to GL. It separates what happened from how we account for it, so rules can change without touching transactions. The pieces are event classes and types, journal line rules, account derivation rules, description rules and supporting references, all grouped in an accounting method assigned to the ledger. My example: the business wanted AP liability on a different natural account for intercompany suppliers. I copied the standard method, built a mapping set from supplier type to natural account, created an ADR that overrides only the natural account segment, attached it to the liability line, activated it, and tested invoice, payment and cancellation."

Tip: Always say you copied the seeded method and tested the full life cycle: create, adjust, cancel, pay.

Likely follow-up

How do you make sure SLA changes are safe?

Copy the seeded method, change only the rules that need changing, and test with draft accounting across every transaction type that uses the method — invoices, credits, prepayments, payments. Then compare results with the old method and migrate through change control. Never edit seeded rules directly.

24How do you integrate a third-party tax engine like Vertex or Avalara?TaxIn-depth

Why they ask: most US Fusion customers use a partner engine.

Answer:

"Fusion Tax has a tax partner integration. I set up the tax regime for partner services, the provider configuration, and tax partner subscriptions for the business units or legal entities. Item tax codes and ship-from and ship-to locations must be accurate, because the partner uses geocodes to find jurisdictions. AR invoices, AP invoices and POs then call the partner to calculate tax, and the results are stored on the tax lines. Filing happens in the partner tool, and we reconcile tax accounts in GL. In testing I focus on exempt customers with certificates, use tax on AP, and jurisdiction edge cases like addresses near city limits."

Tip: Mention address quality and geocodes. They cause most partner tax errors.

Likely follow-up

What do you test in a Vertex integration?

Tax calculation for each nexus, exemptions and the tax returned on invoices and credit memos.

25How would you manage US sales tax exemptions in Fusion?TaxIn-depth

Why they ask: exemptions are an audit risk if certificates aren't managed.

Answer:

"I capture exemption certificates per customer and jurisdiction, either in Fusion Tax or in the partner engine like Vertex or Avalara, and link them to the customer's tax profile with effective dates. Then I set up a process to track expiring certificates and remind customers before they expire, because an expired certificate means we should have charged tax. In testing I include invoices with both exempt and taxable lines for the same customer."

Tip: Focus on expiring certificates. That's where audit findings come from.

Likely follow-up

What happens if a certificate expires?

Tax is charged again, so tracking expiry dates and renewals is important.

26How would you change the liability account on AP invoices based on supplier type using SLA?Subledger AccountingIn-depth

Why they ask: a hands-on SLA question. They want the exact steps.

Answer:

"In Manage Accounting Methods I copy the seeded Standard Accrual method; I never edit seeded. I create a mapping set with supplier type as input and natural account value as output. Then I create an account derivation rule for the liability that starts from the default account and overrides the natural account segment with the mapping set result. I copy the seeded journal entry rule set for the Invoices event class and attach the new rule to the Liability journal line. I activate the rule set and assign the new method to the ledger. Then I test invoice, payment, cancellation and prepayment application."

Tip: Keep the steps in order. It's a test of whether you've actually clicked through it.

Likely follow-up

How would you test that rule change?

Run draft Create Accounting for a few invoices from each supplier type — for example employee, intercompany and standard — and confirm each lands on the right liability account. Then check the AP to GL reconciliation report still ties. Only after sign-off do I move the method to production through change control.

27What are mapping sets, sources and supporting references in SLA?Subledger AccountingIn-depth

Why they ask: these three are the building blocks of SLA rules.

Answer:

"Sources are the transaction data SLA can use in rules, like supplier type, item category or project; custom sources can be built from attributes and descriptive flexfields. Mapping sets are lookup tables that turn an input value into a segment value or a full account, for example item category to natural account. Supporting references are extra attributes stored on journal lines, like supplier or invoice number, so you can report balances by them and reconcile. I find supporting references most useful for intercompany and tax account reconciliations."

Tip: Give one use for each. It keeps the answer practical.

Likely follow-up

Give an example where supporting references solved a reporting problem.

A client used one shared AP liability account for all suppliers. By adding the supplier as a supporting reference in the SLA journal line, the team could report and reconcile balances by supplier straight from GL — without creating hundreds of natural accounts.

28How would you set up a new tax rate, for example a VAT rate change in a country?TaxIn-depth

Why they ask: rate changes happen every year somewhere, and mistakes mean wrong tax on invoices.

Answer:

"In Manage Tax Rates I end-date the current rate and create the new one from the effective date, or add a new rate period under the same rate code. I check whether tax rules need changing, and check the recovery rates and accounts. Then I test AP, AR and PO transactions dated before and after the change. I tell the AP and AR teams what's changing, and check the reports and the tax filing for the switch-over period. The tricky cases are transactions that span the date, like an invoice dated before the change but credited after it."

Tip: Mention transactions that span the date. It's the case that catches people out.

Likely follow-up

What do you test after a rate change?

Transactions dated before and after the effective date to confirm each picks the right rate.
