1What is Supply Chain Financial Orchestration (SFO) and when do you need it?Supply Chain OrchestrationIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: SFO is needed in most global supply chains, and many people find it confusing.

Answer:

"SFO handles the financial side of supply chain flows that cross legal entities or business units. For example, a US company buys goods that are received in its Mexican warehouse, or a sales BU sells goods shipped from another entity's warehouse. Financial orchestration flows, procurement or shipment flows, define the chain of entities, the transfer pricing rules and the documents, like intercompany AR and AP invoices. The goods move once physically, and SFO creates the logical intercompany transactions and accounting in each entity. It's common for global companies with central procurement or sales entities."

Tip: Describe one flow you configured and how you tested the intercompany invoices.

Likely follow-up

What breaks if you implement intercompany shipments without SFO?

Each entity's books don't reflect the internal sale and purchase, so intercompany receivables, payables and margins have to be booked manually.

2How do you design orchestration processes for different order types?Order ManagementIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a hands-on design question.

Answer:

"I group the fulfillment patterns: standard ship, drop ship, back-to-back, configured items, services or subscriptions, and returns. Each gets an orchestration process, often copied from a seeded one, with its steps, task types and change management rules. Process assignment rules route lines by order type, item type, BU or source. I set planned lead times per step so jeopardy works. Then I test change scenarios: quantity change after scheduling, cancel after pick, and change of ship-to after release."

Tip: List the change scenarios you'd test. They're where orchestration breaks.

Likely follow-up

How many orchestration processes is too many?

When processes differ only in minor steps. I keep a small set per fulfillment pattern and use assignment rules and branches rather than near-duplicates.

3What is Global Order Promising (GOP) and how do sourcing rules affect it?Order ManagementIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: GOP is behind every promised date.

Answer:

"GOP checks availability and promises a date for order lines. It considers on-hand, supply like POs, work orders and transfers, lead times and allocations. Sourcing rules and assignment sets tell GOP which warehouses and suppliers can fulfill, with ranks and percentages. It supports ATP, available to promise, and CTP, capable to promise, which checks components and resources for make items. Data comes into the planning repository through data collections, which must run regularly. A common issue is GOP not scheduling because collections didn't run or sourcing isn't assigned."

Tip: End with the common issue. It shows support experience.

Likely follow-up

Why might GOP promise a date the warehouse can't meet?

Stale collections, wrong lead times or missing sourcing rules. GOP is only as good as its supply data.

4How do you handle changes to a sales order that is already in fulfillment?Order ManagementIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: order changes are where orchestration earns its keep.

Answer:

"Fusion OM uses change management in the orchestration process. Each step defines whether a change is allowed and what compensation runs, for example cancel the reservation and reschedule, update the shipment line, or reject the change. Changes from source systems come in as revisions, and OM compares and applies them. After ship confirm, changes are blocked, so we use returns or credit memos instead. Processing constraints can also block specific changes by role or status."

Tip: Explain compensation patterns. It's the key Fusion concept here.

Likely follow-up

What happens if a customer cancels a line that's already picked?

The compensation pattern unpicks and returns stock to the subinventory, or blocks the change if it's past ship confirm.

5What are processing constraints?Order ManagementIn-depth

Why they ask: a control question.

Answer:

"Processing constraints are rules that stop changes or actions on orders under certain conditions. For example, 'the ship-to can't change once the line is pick released', or 'only order administrators can change the price after booking'. They're defined on entities, like the order header or line, and attributes, with conditions such as status, and the roles they apply to. There are seeded constraints, and I add custom ones for business rules. They protect data in the middle of fulfillment and enforce controls."

Tip: Give one example. It makes the concept clear right away.

Likely follow-up

Give an example of a constraint you'd add.

Block quantity changes after a configured item's work order is released, because changing it would waste production.

6How would you set up customer-specific pricing with volume discounts?PricingIn-depth

Why they ask: a hands-on pricing question.

Answer:

"I create a price list with the base prices. Then a discount list with tiered volume adjustments, for example 1 to 99 units 0%, 100 to 499 units 5%, and 500 or more 10%. Then a pricing strategy that combines the price list and discount list. Then a pricing segment for the customer or customer class, and I assign the strategy to that segment. Then I test with orders at each tier and check the price breakdown on the order line."

Tip: Give the tiers with numbers. It shows you've built one.

Likely follow-up

How do you give one customer a special price only for a quarter?

A customer-specific price list or discount with effective dates, assigned through the pricing strategy rules.

7How do blanket agreements with price breaks work?ProcurementIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: price breaks are a common agreement feature.

Answer:

"I create a blanket agreement with items, prices and price breaks, by quantity, ship-to organization or dates. Requisitions and POs that reference the agreement get the right price automatically. Cumulative price breaks look at the total quantity released so far; non-cumulative look at each release on its own. For example, $10 each for the first 1,000 units, $9 after that, cumulative. Amount limits and effective dates control how the agreement is used, and automatic sourcing makes requisitions pick it up."

Tip: Give the cumulative example with numbers. It's the part people get wrong.

Likely follow-up

How does a price break apply on a PO?

When the PO quantity or cumulative quantity reaches the break, the lower price defaults on the line.

8How do you configure supplier-managed price changes through the supplier portal?ProcurementIn-depth

Why they ask: supplier self-service saves buyers time.

Answer:

"I let suppliers request agreement changes through the Supplier Portal as change orders on the agreement. Each change goes for approval by the buyer, and rules can add more approval if the price change is above a percentage. The supplier needs portal access with the right roles. Once approved, the new prices apply to new orders from the effective date, and the agreement revision history keeps the audit trail."

Tip: Mention the percentage-based approval. It's the control that makes buyers comfortable.

Likely follow-up

Why route supplier price changes through approval?

So suppliers can't change prices without the buyer agreeing, which protects against cost creep.

10How does supplier acknowledgment of purchase orders work?ProcurementIn-depth

Why they ask: acknowledgments confirm supplier commitment on critical orders.

Answer:

"On the PO, I set acknowledgment required, at document or schedule level, with a due date. The supplier accepts, rejects or proposes changes in the Supplier Portal or through B2B. The buyer reviews any proposed changes and accepts or rejects them, which creates a change order. Pending acknowledgments are monitored with OTBI or infolets. The buyer reviews and accepts or rejects; acceptance updates the PO schedule.

12What is an ASN and how is it used in receiving?ReceivingBasic

Why they ask: a basic inbound question.

Answer:

"An Advance Shipment Notice tells us what a supplier has shipped, items, quantities, lots or serials, and expected date, before it arrives. The supplier creates it in the portal or sends it by B2B, as EDI 856. Receivers receive against the ASN, which is faster and more accurate. An ASBN, advance shipment and billing notice, can also create the invoice. And the PO can require an ASN reference, forcing suppliers to send them."

Tip: Mention requiring ASNs on the PO. It's how you get suppliers to comply.

Likely follow-up

What's the benefit of ASN-based receiving?

Receivers confirm against the ASN instead of keying lines, which is faster and more accurate.

13How does receipt accounting create accruals and how do you clear old accruals?ReceivingIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: stale accruals are a common audit finding.

Answer:

"With accrue at receipt, delivery to inventory or expense books Dr Inventory or Expense, Cr Receipt Accrual. When AP matches the invoice, AP books Dr Accrual, Cr Liability, and differences go to variance accounts. To find problems, I use Accrual Reconciliation in Receipt Accounting and the accrual balances report, which show accruals with no invoice or mismatches. After investigation, I write them off with approval or clear them, often because the PO should have been closed. And I close POs regularly so accruals don't build up."

Tip: Say "close POs regularly". It's the prevention step.

Likely follow-up

How do you decide an old accrual should be written off?

When it's confirmed no invoice will come, like the supplier confirms it's fully billed, with approval and documentation.

14What is consigned inventory and how is it handled in Fusion?InventoryIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: consignment changes ownership and accounting.

Answer:

"With supplier consignment, the supplier owns stock sitting in our warehouse until we use it. There's a consignment agreement, a blanket agreement with consignment terms. Receipts go into inventory as consigned, so there's no liability. When we consume it, by issue, transfer or after an aging period, ownership transfers: a consumption advice is created and the supplier is paid through AP or pay-on-use. On-hand shows both owned and consigned quantities. Customer consignment is the reverse: our stock at a customer's site."

Tip: Explain when ownership transfers. It's the heart of consignment.

Likely follow-up

When does ownership of consigned stock transfer?

When we consume it, triggering a consumption advice and the supplier's invoice.

15How do you set up and use transfer orders in Fusion?InventoryIn-depthTop question

"Transfer orders move inventory between orgs with a full flow: request, approve, ship, receive. They're created from transfer requests, planning, min-max or the internal material transfer page. Sourcing rules pick the source, and the shipping network and transfer pricing, at cost or a price list, are defined. If the orgs are in different legal entities, supply chain financial orchestration creates intercompany accounting. I track status in the Transfer Orders work area."

16How do you configure cycle counting with approvals and tolerances?InventoryIn-depth

Why they ask: a hands-on setup question.

Answer:

"I create the cycle count for an org with subinventories, ABC classes and frequency. I set count tolerances, a quantity percentage and value thresholds. Counts within tolerance post automatically; outside, they need a recount or approval. Approval can be always, never, or only out of tolerance. Count sequences are generated by schedule, and counters enter counts on mobile or web. Adjustments post to a cycle count adjustment account and create cost accounting."

Tip: Give example tolerances, like 2% for A items and 5% for C.

What should happen to counts outside tolerance?

They go for recount or approval, so large adjustments are checked before posting.

17What is the difference between available to transact, available to reserve and on-hand?InventoryBasic

Why they ask: a classic support question.

Answer:

"On-hand is the total physical quantity. Available to reserve is on-hand minus existing reservations and some statuses. Available to transact is what you can actually move or issue right now, excluding reserved quantities, stock in non-transactable subinventories, and stock with a restrictive material status, like a lot on hold. Material status, at subinventory, locator, lot or serial, decides which transactions are allowed, so a quarantine status blocks shipping."

Tip: Use a quarantined lot as the example. It explains why on-hand isn't always usable.

Likely follow-up

Why might available to transact be less than available to reserve?

Material statuses or holds can block transactions even if the stock isn't reserved.

18What are cost organizations, cost books and valuation units in Fusion Cost Management?CostingIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: the core costing structure in Fusion.

Answer:

"A cost organization is a group of inventory orgs that share costing, like a plant and its nearby warehouses. A cost book is a set of costing policies; the primary book is for financial reporting, and others can be added for management or local GAAP. The valuation structure and valuation unit set the level costs are kept at, for example by cost org, by cost org and subinventory, or by lot. Cost profiles assign the cost method to items through item cost profiles and defaults. Together they replace EBS's per-org cost method."

Tip: Give an example of a cost org grouping. It makes it concrete.

Likely follow-up

Why might you have more than one cost book?

To value inventory under different methods for different reporting needs.

19How do you troubleshoot an inventory transaction that hasn't been costed?CostingIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: a daily support issue.

Answer:

"I check that Transfer Transactions from Inventory to Costing ran and succeeded. Then Review Cost Accounting Processes for cost processor errors: missing cost, missing item cost profile, missing valuation unit, or a closed cost period. Then the cost accounting distributions for SLA account errors in Create Cost Accounting. Tip: Follow the chain in order. That's the method they want.

Likely follow-up

What's the most common costing error you see?

Missing standard cost for an item, so the cost processor can't value its transactions.

20How do landed costs work in Fusion?CostingIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: landed cost improves margin accuracy.

Answer:

"Landed Cost Management adds freight, duties, insurance and other charges to item cost. I define charge references and allocation bases, quantity, weight, value or volume. Charges are estimated on the PO through landed cost charge templates, and actuals come from trade operations and AP invoices. They're allocated to receipt lines and included in inventory value and COGS when sold. Differences between estimate and actual are adjusted."

Tip: Say why it matters: true margin on imported goods.

Likely follow-up

What if actual freight costs come after the goods are sold?

The difference goes to COGS for consumed units rather than inventory.

21What is a New Item Request (NIR) and how is it configured?Product HubIn-depth

Why they ask: a core Product Hub question.

Answer:

"A New Item Request is a governed workflow for creating items. Item classes are configured for NIRs: which entities are required, like attributes, structures and the approved manufacturer list, and the workflow steps, definition and approval, with assignees. The requester submits; definition steps route to teams to fill in their attributes, like costing, planning and purchasing; approval steps approve; and then the item is created and released. Validation rules enforce quality and notifications keep everyone informed. The benefit is clean item data and an audit trail instead of anyone creating items freely."

Tip: Say what it replaces: uncontrolled item creation. That's the business case.

Likely follow-up

How do you keep NIRs moving quickly?

Parallel definition steps, role-based assignment and due dates with reminders.

25What are sourcing rules, bills of distribution and assignment sets?PlanningIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: sourcing drives planning and promising.

Answer:

"A sourcing rule says where supply comes from for an org or item, for example buy 60% from supplier X and 40% from Y, or transfer from org A, with ranks for priority. A bill of distribution handles sourcing across several orgs, a global source chain. An assignment set assigns sourcing rules at different levels: global, org, category, item, or item-org, and the most specific level wins. Planning, GOP, min-max and automatic requisition sourcing all use them."

Tip: Say "the most specific level wins". It's how you debug sourcing.

Likely follow-up

What's an assignment set for?

It ties sourcing rules to items, categories or orgs, telling planning which rules apply where.

26What is the difference between a forecast and a demand plan?PlanningIn-depth

Why they ask: a basic demand planning question.

Answer:

"A forecast is a prediction of future demand, from history, statistical models or input. A demand plan, in Demand Management, manages forecasts across levels, like product family, region and customer, blends statistical forecasts with sales input into a consensus, and measures forecast accuracy. The approved demand plan feeds the supply plan. And forecast consumption matters: actual sales orders consume the forecast so demand isn't counted twice."

Tip: Mention forecast consumption. It's what separates a good answer.

Likely follow-up

Who owns the consensus demand plan?

Usually demand planning with sales and finance input, signed off in the S&OP cycle.

30What is Warehouse Management (WMS) and how is it different from standard Inventory?LogisticsIn-depth

Answer:

"Standard Inventory tracks quantities by org, subinventory and locator. 31What is backflushing?ManufacturingIn-depth

Why they ask: a common manufacturing concept with risks.

Answer:

"Backflushing issues components automatically when an operation or the assembly is reported complete, instead of issuing them beforehand. It's set by the component's supply type: assembly pull at completion, or operation pull at operation completion. It cuts transactions for high-volume, low-value components. The risk is inaccurate on-hand if actual usage differs, for example from scrap, so it needs accurate BOMs and good scrap reporting."

Tip: Name the risk. It shows judgment.

Likely follow-up

What must be accurate for backflushing to work?

BOM quantities and scrap factors, otherwise on-hand drifts.

32How is work order cost calculated and how are variances recognized?ManufacturingIn-depth

Why they ask: manufacturing cost is a finance and operations meeting point.

Answer:

"Under standard cost, the assembly is valued at standard, and actual material, resources and overhead are charged to the work order. At close, variances are calculated: material usage, resource efficiency and rate. Under actual or average cost, the assembly cost is the actual cost charged. The accounting is Dr WIP for issues and resources, Cr WIP and Dr Inventory at completion, and variances to variance accounts at close. Closing work orders on time keeps month-end costing clean."

Tip: End with closing on time. It's the operational lesson.

Likely follow-up

What does a large material usage variance tell you?

Either the BOM is wrong or there's excess scrap on the floor.

33What accounting does a sales order shipment create, and where?IntegrationIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: SCM consultants must understand O2C accounting.

Answer:

"At ship confirm, Cost Management books Dr COGS, or Deferred COGS, Cr Inventory at the item cost. At invoicing, AR books Dr Receivable, Cr Revenue, or Deferred Revenue, Cr Tax. If revenue is deferred, for example because of an acceptance clause, COGS is deferred too and recognized in line with revenue. SLA creates the accounting in each subledger, and it's transferred to GL."

Tip: Mention deferred COGS matching deferred revenue. It's the detail that impresses.

Likely follow-up

What's deferred COGS for?

When revenue is deferred, cost waits too, so revenue and cost match.

34How do you integrate a third-party logistics (3PL) provider with Fusion?IntegrationIn-depth

38What is your approach to converting open sales orders and POs?Data MigrationIn-depth

55What is the difference between a workflow agent and a hierarchical (supervisor) agent?AI Agent StudioIn-depth

Why they ask: a design concept question.

Answer:

"A workflow agent is a defined sequence of nodes: business object, code, email trigger, human approval and agent nodes. It's predictable and auditable, so it's best for business-critical processes. A hierarchical or supervisor agent decides which specialist agents to call, which is more flexible but less predictable. Oracle has been moving toward workflows, with agent nodes inside them for steps that need judgment. I choose workflows when the steps are known and controls matter."

Tip: Say which you'd choose for finance and why.
