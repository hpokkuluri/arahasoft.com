Oracle Senior Solution Architect – SCM Interview Questions

The questions interviewers ask for an Oracle SCM Senior Solution Architect role (10–15 years), with why they ask, a full answer you can say in the room, and a tip. Tap a question to see the answer.

61 questions 16 basic 45 in-depth
1What does an SCM Solution Architect own on an Oracle program?ArchitectureBasic

Why they ask: to see if you understand the architect's scope in SCM.

Answer:

"I own the end-to-end supply chain design: plan, source, make, deliver and return, across Fusion modules, planning, logistics systems and partners. The SCM enterprise structure: inventory orgs, cost orgs, BUs and SFO flows, aligned with Finance. The integration architecture with MES, WMS, TMS, PLM, e-commerce, EDI partners, 3PLs and contract manufacturers. The master data architecture for items, BOMs, suppliers, customers and locations. Non-functional requirements like order volumes, pick release windows, planning run times and warehouse availability. Design authority across SCM workstreams and with Finance. And the roadmap: planning maturity, automation, AI and a control tower."

Tip: Mention alignment with Finance. SCM architects are judged on the seams.

2Design the SCM structure for a company with 5 plants, 12 DCs, central procurement and 3 selling entities.Enterprise StructuresIn-depth

Why they ask: a cross-functional design question that tests whether you understand SCM structures from finance's side.

Answer:

"I'd create one inventory org per plant and per DC, plus an item master org. Cost orgs by plant using standard cost, and DCs grouped into cost orgs using average or standard, with cost books for primary and management views. Procurement is one central procurement BU, with requisitioning BUs for each operating entity and rules for the sold-to legal entity. Each selling entity gets an order management BU, and fulfillment can come from any DC through sourcing rules. Supply chain financial orchestration handles the flows: central procurement to plants, and plants to selling entities with transfer pricing. Planning is one global supply plan with demand by selling entity or region. Shipping networks connect the DCs for rebalancing with transfer orders. And I'd validate the legal flows and tax with finance before configuring anything."

Tip: End with "validate with finance and tax first". It shows you know where these designs go wrong.

3Design the integration landscape for Fusion SCM with WMS, TMS, MES and e-commerce.IntegrationIn-depth

Why they ask: a core SCM architecture question.

Answer:

"E-commerce or CRM creates and updates orders in OM through REST or OIC events, with idempotency and status callbacks. OM sends fulfillment requests to the WMS through orchestration connectors, and the WMS returns pick, pack and ship confirmations and inventory adjustments. Shipping or WMS sends shipments to the TMS for planning, and carrier, tracking and freight cost come back. Fusion sends work orders and materials to MES, and completions, consumption and scrap come back. Inventory snapshots reconcile WMS and Fusion. It all runs on OIC or an enterprise iPaaS with event-driven patterns, monitoring and error queues. And the system of record for each data element is documented."

Tip: End with "system of record documented". It prevents most integration disputes.

4How do you ensure inventory stays in sync between Fusion and an external WMS?IntegrationIn-depth

Why they ask: WMS drift is a common, costly problem.

Answer:

"First decide who owns what: the WMS owns locator and LPN detail, Fusion owns org and subinventory level. Every receipt, shipment, adjustment and transfer in the WMS posts to Fusion in near real time. Messages have unique IDs so they're idempotent, and they're processed in order per item and lot. A daily reconciliation compares snapshots, and adjustments are only created with approval. Failed messages are visible to operations, not just IT. And counts are aligned at go-live."

Tip: Say "failed messages visible to operations". They're the ones who can fix them.

5How would you architect Order Management for very high order volumes (e.g. 200k lines/day)?Order ManagementIn-depth

Why they ask: a performance architecture question.

Answer:

"Orders come in through bulk or REST in parallel streams, with throttling and retry. Orchestration processes stay lean, with as few steps and waits as possible. Global Order Promising is tuned, with frequent collections and well-designed ATP rules, and non-urgent lines are scheduled in batches. Pick release runs in waves by warehouse with tested release rules. AutoInvoice is split by batch source and runs often. I'd do performance testing with production-like volumes and engage Oracle on capacity. And monitoring with alerts on backlog."

Tip: Mention performance testing at real volume. It's the step that prevents go-live failures.

6How would you architect supply chain planning for a global company?PlanningIn-depth

Why they ask: a senior planning architecture question.

Answer:

"Demand Management for statistical and ML forecasts by product, customer and region, with a consensus process. Supply Planning for constrained plans across plants and DCs, with a master production schedule for key plants. Sales and Operations Planning linking demand, supply and finance. Replenishment Planning for DC inventory policies. Data comes from Fusion collections and external systems by file, and planning owns the item planning attributes. The cadence is a weekly plan with daily net change. Scenarios support what-if and risk. And it connects to Global Order Promising for customer promises."

Tip: Mention who owns planning attributes. Data ownership decides whether planning works.

7What data foundations must be in place before planning can work?PlanningBasic

Why they ask: planning fails on data more than on software.

Answer:

"Item attributes: processing and pre- and post-processing lead times, order modifiers, safety stock policy, planning method and make or buy. Accurate BOMs and work definitions. Sourcing rules and assignment sets. Shipping, receiving and manufacturing calendars. Clean demand history, adjusted for returns, promotions and stockouts. Accurate open supply: POs, work orders and transfers. And resource capacities if the plan is constrained. Without these, the plan produces noise and planners won't trust it."

Tip: End with trust. It's the real cost of bad data.

8How would you architect product master data across PLM, Product Hub and downstream systems?Product HubIn-depth

Why they ask: a senior master data architecture question.

Answer:

"Engineering items and BOMs are authored in PLM, Oracle's or a third party. On release, they flow to Product Hub, the enterprise master, through change orders. Product Hub enriches commercial, planning and costing attributes through NIR workflows and data stewards. Then it publishes to e-commerce, WMS, MES and CPQ through events or OIC. Global numbering, item classes and data quality rules hold it together, and cross-references link legacy, supplier and customer part numbers."

Tip: State who owns what: PLM owns engineering, Product Hub owns the enterprise record.

9How would you architect DSCSA serialization for a pharmaceutical distributor with Oracle?ComplianceIn-depth

Why they ask: a specialist compliance architecture question.

Answer:

"A serialization repository, like TraceLink or rfxcel, holds serial numbers, aggregation and EPCIS events. Fusion tracks lots, and serials where needed, for inventory and orders. Inbound EPCIS events from manufacturers link to receipts and ASNs; outbound EPCIS goes with shipments; and verification requests handle saleable returns. Warehouses scan at receiving, picking and shipping, including aggregation and disaggregation. Mismatches go through an exception process with quarantine. And there's a full audit trail and data retention. The key is aligning Fusion lot, serial and shipment data with the serialization system down to the unit."

Tip: Mention aggregation. It's where serialization projects struggle.

10How do you design SCM for GxP-regulated environments?ComplianceIn-depth

Why they ask: pharma and medical device projects need validation.

Answer:

"Computer system validation, CSV or the newer CSA approach: a validation plan, risk assessment, IQ, OQ and PQ or risk-based testing, and traceability. 21 CFR Part 11: electronic signatures and audit trails on GMP-relevant transactions like QA release and status changes. Quality integration with inspections, deviations and CAPA. Lot genealogy and recall capability. Change control for configuration with impact assessments. And a validation strategy for quarterly updates, using risk-based regression."

Tip: Mention the quarterly update strategy. It's the hardest part of validated SaaS.

11How would you model contract manufacturers in Oracle Fusion?Outsourced ManufacturingIn-depth

Why they ask: outsourcing is common, and modeling it right matters for cost and visibility.

Answer:

"I'd use Fusion Outsourced Manufacturing, where we own the material and design and the contract manufacturer builds. The CM site is modeled as a contract manufacturing inventory org with work definitions. A purchase order for the CM's service is tied to a work order, and components are shipped to the CM or bought by them on our behalf. The CM reports progress and completions through B2B messages, and we receive the finished goods. Cost is material plus the CM service cost rolled into the assembly. Visibility comes through the supplier portal or B2B."

Tip: Mention the PO tied to the work order. It's the key modeling idea.

12How would you architect transportation and global trade with Oracle?LogisticsIn-depth

Why they ask: a senior logistics design question.

Answer:

"OTM handles transportation planning, tendering, carrier rates and freight settlement. GTM handles trade compliance: restricted party screening, classification like HTS and ECCN, licenses, customs filings and duty management. Fusion Order Management and Shipping integrate natively with both. Carriers connect by EDI or APIs, and customs brokers too. Landed cost management feeds the duties and freight into costing. And shipment tracking feeds visibility to customers."

Tip: Separate OTM and GTM clearly. They're often confused.

13How would you design the costing architecture for a multi-entity manufacturer?CostingIn-depth

Why they ask: a senior design question.

Answer:

"Cost orgs per plant, using standard cost with roll-ups for resources and overhead. A primary cost book for GAAP and a secondary book for management, IFRS, or a group view with intercompany markup removed. Valuation units at cost org level, or by lot for high-value items. Transfer pricing through supply chain financial orchestration for intercompany moves. Landed costs for imports. Variance reporting. And an annual standard cost cycle with simulation, approvals and revaluation."

Tip: Mention the group view without intercompany markup. It's what group finance needs.

14How would you design supply chain analytics on top of Oracle Fusion?Data ArchitectureIn-depth

Why they ask: analytics architecture for SCM.

Answer:

"Fusion Data Intelligence SCM analytics gives prebuilt KPIs like OTIF, inventory turns, PO cycle time and supplier performance. BICC extracts go to a data lake for enterprise analytics that join non-Oracle data like WMS, TMS and IoT. A supply chain control tower shows real-time exceptions, like late POs, stockouts and delayed shipments, with alerts and actions. Data definitions, like how OTIF is calculated, are governed. And I avoid heavy BIP extracts on production."

Tip: Mention governing the OTIF definition. Different definitions cause endless arguments.

15How would you use Oracle to improve supply chain resilience?ResilienceIn-depth

Why they ask: resilience is a board-level topic since recent disruptions.

Answer:

"Multi-sourcing through sourcing rules and supplier qualification. Supplier risk monitoring, financial, geopolitical and ESG, linked to supplier records. Inventory buffers for critical items using planning safety stock policies. Scenario planning for supplier failure or demand spikes. Control tower alerts with AI-driven exception handling. And visibility into tier-1 suppliers, and tier-2 where possible."

Tip: Mention scenario planning. It turns resilience from a slogan into a practice.

16Where would you apply AI agents in a Fusion SCM landscape?AIIn-depth

Why they ask: to see if you can find practical SCM use cases.

"In procurement: a supplier onboarding agent, a requisition assistant, contract risk review, and drafts for PO change negotiations. In order management: triaging exceptions like holds and late lines, and customer communications. In planning: explaining forecast anomalies and helping planners with exceptions. In inventory and quality: spotting unusual adjustments and nonconformances. In product data: enrichment and classification. With guardrails: role-based access, human approval for commitments like POs and prices, and auditing of every agent action."

17How would you architect migrating SCM from EBS to Fusion with plants going live in waves?MigrationIn-depth

Why they ask: wave migrations need coexistence design.

Answer:

"I'd start with a pilot plant or DC, then regions, often with finance going first as the backbone. During coexistence, EBS sites trade with Fusion sites through integrations for intercompany flows, transfers and planning. Product Hub in Fusion is the global item master, synced to EBS during the transition. Planning either collects from both instances into one plan or runs separate plans for a time. Coexistence is retired quickly, each wave reuses the template, and lessons go into the next wave."

Tip: Mention one plan across both instances. It's the hardest coexistence decision.

18How do you build a global SCM template for rollouts?TemplateBasic

Why they ask: templates make rollouts fast and consistent.

Answer:

"Core processes are standardized: P2P, O2C, returns, transfers and inventory control. The configuration baseline is documented and packaged. Rules say what countries or sites may change, like legal documents and tax, and what they may not. A rollout kit includes data templates, test scripts, training and a cutover plan. Template changes go through global governance. And I measure rollout duration and defects to keep improving the kit."

Tip: Mention measuring each rollout. The template should get faster every time.

19What SCM non-functional requirements do you define early?Non-FunctionalBasic

Why they ask: NFRs prevent go-live performance problems.

Answer:

"Volumes: orders and lines per day, receipts, shipments, items and BOM sizes. Batch windows: pick release, planning runs, collections and cost processing. Latency: order import to scheduling, and ship confirm to invoice. Warehouse availability: how much outage they can tolerate and offline fallback procedures. Integration throughput and error SLAs. Label and document printing performance. And data retention and archiving."

Tip: Mention printing performance. Slow labels stop a warehouse.

20Pick release is taking too long in a busy DC. How do you approach it?PerformanceIn-depth

Why they ask: a performance troubleshooting question.

Answer:

"I measure first: lines per release, the rules used and time per step. Then release rules: smaller waves by priority or carrier cutoff instead of releasing everything at once. Picking rules: simpler allocation logic and no complex sort orders on huge data sets. Reservation and auto pick confirm settings. Document printing moved to asynchronous processes. Competing processes, like planning or costing, rescheduled. And Oracle support with diagnostics if needed."

Tip: Start with smaller waves. It's usually the biggest win.

21How do you design SCM security for a large organization?SecurityIn-depth

Why they ask: SCM security has many layers.

Answer:

"Roles by job: buyer, category manager, warehouse operator, planner and order administrator. Data access by BU, inventory org and cost org, and procurement agent access by BU. Item data security for who can create and change items by class. SoD: supplier creation vs PO approval vs receiving vs invoicing, and inventory adjustments vs count approval. Partner access through supplier and customer portals with external identities. And provisioning automated from HCM."

Tip: Give one SCM SoD conflict. It shows you think like an auditor.

I present options with trade-offs, not only technical arguments. A design authority with business representation makes decisions. And one decisions log keeps everyone aligned."

24How do you handle a situation where the business wants to replicate a legacy process exactly?BehavioralBasic

Why they ask: a common push-back scenario.

Answer:

"I ask why the process exists, to find the underlying need. I show the standard Oracle flow with their data and quantify the benefits. I offer configuration that meets the need a different way. If they still insist, I document the cost, risk and upgrade impact and take it to the sponsor for a decision. And I revisit it after go-live with data."

Tip: Mention revisiting after go-live. It gives people a way to say yes now.

25How do you integrate EDI partners at scale?IntegrationIn-depth

Why they ask: EDI at scale needs a model, not one-off projects.

Answer:

"I use a B2B or EDI provider, a VAN or cloud EDI, for partner onboarding and translation between X12 or EDIFACT and a canonical XML or JSON. On the Oracle side, OIC B2B or Fusion Collaboration Messaging. A partner onboarding playbook covers testing, certification and go-live. Monitoring includes functional acknowledgments, 997 or CONTRL, and error alerts. 26How would you design order orchestration to support omnichannel fulfillment (ship from store, BOPIS)?Order ManagementIn-depth

Why they ask: a retail design question.

Answer:

"Fulfillment nodes, DCs, stores and suppliers for drop ship, are modeled as orgs or external fulfillment systems. Sourcing logic picks the node by distance, inventory, cost and capacity, using GOP rules or an external order-routing engine. There's an orchestration process per fulfillment type: ship from DC, ship from store, buy online pick up in store, and drop ship. Store inventory feeds give visibility across nodes. Changes and cancels are handled across systems. And returns can be made anywhere, routed to the right node."

Tip: Mention store inventory accuracy. Ship-from-store fails when store counts are wrong.

27How would you design inventory visibility across Oracle and non-Oracle locations?InventoryIn-depth

Why they ask: most companies have stock outside their ERP, at 3PLs or stores.

Answer:

"Fusion holds on-hand for the orgs it manages. External locations, like 3PLs, consignment sites and stores, send snapshots or transactions into Fusion as external orgs, or into a visibility layer. Global Order Promising collects supply from all sources so promises are realistic. Control tower dashboards show availability and aging. And I'd agree the latency, like hourly or daily, plus a reconciliation process with each external location."

Tip: Mention agreeing latency. Everyone assumes real time until it isn't.

28How would you architect source-to-pay for a large enterprise on Fusion?ProcurementIn-depth

Why they ask: a senior architecture question across the whole process.

Answer:

"I'd design it as a chain. Supplier life cycle first: registration, qualification, risk screening and performance. Then sourcing events and contracts with a clause library. Then catalogs and agreements, with guided buying in Self Service Procurement so employees buy on contract. Approvals by budget owner and category. Receiving and acceptance for both goods and services. AP automation with e-invoicing, document recognition and matching. Spend analytics and savings tracking. And integrations to supplier networks, risk providers and e-signature."

Tip: Present it as a chain from supplier to payment. It's easy to follow and shows you see the whole thing.

29How do you design procurement for services (contractors, SOWs) in Oracle?ProcurementIn-depth

Why they ask: services are harder to buy and receive than goods.

Answer:

"I use fixed-price service lines and rate-based lines, and complex work for construction and projects, which supports progress payments and retainage. Receipts for services are by amount or quantity, entered by the requester who confirms the work was done. For contingent workers, many companies use a vendor management system like Fieldglass or Beeline, integrated with Fusion for POs and invoices. Approvals go to the project or cost center owner. Matching can be 2-way, or 3-way with service acceptance as the receipt."

Tip: Mention who confirms the service. Service receipts are where control often breaks down.

30How do you architect manufacturing execution between Fusion and MES?ManufacturingIn-depth

Why they ask: most plants run an MES next to the ERP.

Answer:

"Fusion is the system of record for planning, work orders, costing, inventory and quality records. MES handles shop-floor detail: machine data, operator steps, and in pharma, electronic batch records. Work orders and BOMs or recipes go from Fusion to MES. Consumption, completions, labor and machine time, and quality results come back. I send summaries where ERP doesn't need the detail, near real time for inventory-critical transactions, and I build error handling and WIP reconciliation between the two."

Tip: Say "send summaries where detail isn't needed". Over-integrating is a common mistake.

31How does Oracle Maintenance fit into the SCM architecture?MaintenanceIn-depth

Why they ask: asset-heavy companies need maintenance tied to supply chain.

Answer:

"Oracle Maintenance manages equipment: assets, work orders, preventive maintenance schedules and meters. Spare parts come from inventory, and purchases go through procurement. Costs go to assets or cost centers, with integration to Fixed Assets. IoT data can trigger condition-based maintenance. The benefits are better uptime and optimized spare parts."

Tip: Mention IoT-triggered maintenance. It's where the field is going.

32How would you architect a closed-loop quality process with Oracle?QualityIn-depth

Why they ask: a quality architecture question.

Answer:

"Inspection plans run at receiving, production and shipping. Failures create nonconformance records, which lead to investigation and CAPA, corrective and preventive actions. Supplier quality uses supplier corrective action requests and scorecards that feed sourcing decisions. When a design or spec changes, a change order in Product Hub carries it. Customer complaints link to lots through genealogy. And analytics show defect trends. The loop is closed when what we learn changes specs, suppliers or processes."

Tip: Explain what "closed loop" means. It's the point of the question.

33How do you govern master data in an SCM landscape?Data ArchitectureBasic

Why they ask: master data quality decides SCM success.

Answer:

"Each domain has an owner: items, suppliers, customers, locations and BOMs. Stewards run workflows like NIRs, supplier registration and customer onboarding. Quality rules apply at entry, not after. KPIs track completeness, duplicates and time to create. Structural data, like UOMs and item classes, goes through change control. And data quality is reviewed regularly with business leaders."

Tip: Say "quality at entry, not after". Cleaning later always costs more.

34How do you test a global SCM template before the first rollout?TestingBasic

Why they ask: a flawed template repeats its flaws in every country.

Answer:

"Process scenarios cover every variant: product types, fulfillment types and entities. Integrations are tested with real partner test environments. Finance verifies the accounting for every flow. Volume and performance tests simulate peak days. Day-in-the-life simulations run in a warehouse and a plant. The cutover is rehearsed. And exit criteria are agreed with business leadership."

Tip: Mention peak-day volume tests. Templates often pass at average volume and fail at peak.

35How would you plan a wave-based go-live for 12 DCs?CutoverIn-depth

Why they ask: wave planning is an architect and program skill.

Answer:

"A pilot DC first, representative but lower risk, with four to six weeks of stabilization before wave two. Waves grouped by region or complexity, avoiding peak seasons. A repeatable cutover runbook with timing from the pilot. Inventory counts and on-hand loads for each wave. Communication with carriers, customers and suppliers. Hypercare teams that move with the waves. And lessons learned after each wave."

Tip: Mention the stabilization period. Rushing wave two is how problems multiply.

36How do you build an SCM digital roadmap?RoadmapBasic

42How would you design an inventory optimization approach?InventoryIn-depth

Why they ask: inventory optimization frees cash.

Answer:

"I'd segment items by value and variability, ABC and XYZ, and set service level targets by segment. Safety stock is calculated statistically from demand and lead-time variability. Order quantities balance holding cost against ordering cost. For a network of DCs, I'd think multi-echelon, deciding where buffers should sit. I'd monitor excess and obsolete stock with disposition rules. The tools can be Fusion Replenishment Planning or a specialist optimization tool."

Tip: Start with segmentation. One safety stock rule for every item is the usual mistake.

43How do you approach a supply chain control tower design?ArchitectureIn-depth

Why they ask: control towers are a common modernization goal.

Answer:

"Use cases first: late supplier POs, stockout risk, delayed shipments, quality holds and capacity issues. Then data: near real-time feeds from Fusion events, WMS, TMS, carriers and suppliers. Alerts have thresholds and owners, with recommended actions, using AI where it adds value. A workflow lets people act: expedite, reallocate or notify the customer. Dashboards serve both leadership and operations. And I start small and add use cases each quarter."

Tip: Say "use cases first". Control towers built data-first become dashboards nobody uses.

45How do you integrate CPQ with Oracle Order Management?Order ManagementIn-depth

Why they ask: CPQ to OM is a common integration.

Answer:

"CPQ owns configuration, pricing and quoting. An accepted quote becomes an order in OM through the native integration or OIC and REST, carrying the configuration, prices and discounts. I decide with the business whether OM reprices or freezes the CPQ prices; usually we freeze them. Configured items are created in OM and Configurator for assemble-to-order. Status updates go back to CPQ or CRM. And customer and item masters must match across the systems."

Tip: Recommend freezing CPQ prices. Repricing what sales already agreed causes disputes.

46How would you support a company switching from average to standard cost?CostingIn-depth

Why they ask: a big change that affects finance reporting.

Answer:

"First a business case and finance approval, because standard cost brings variances and different reporting. Then set standard costs for every item, starting from recent average or actual costs, with roll-ups for manufactured items. Switch at a period or year start, revaluing on-hand at the switch. Change the cost profiles and add the new variance accounts. Test receipts, work orders and shipments. And train finance on reading variances, because that's the new skill they'll need."

Tip: End with training on variances. The system change is easier than the people change.

47How would you architect reverse logistics at scale?ReturnsIn-depth

Why they ask: returns are costly, especially in e-commerce.

Answer:

"I'd set return authorization policies by product and customer, with an online return portal. Returns are routed to the right place: a DC, repair center or back to the supplier. Inspection and disposition rules decide whether to restock, refurbish, recycle, scrap or return to vendor. Credit timing rules say whether credit is given at authorization, receipt or inspection. Refurbishment runs through work orders with serial tracking. And analytics on return reasons and cost drive fixes upstream."

Tip: Mention credit timing. It's a business decision with real cash impact.

Supplier and customer onboarding processes are ready to absorb new records. SFO flows extend to new entities. Integration patterns are reusable for new 3PLs or MES. And a day-one playbook gives visibility through data integration before full migration."

49Tell me about a time an SCM design caused an unexpected finance impact.BehavioralBasic

Why they ask: cross-module awareness.

Answer (example):
