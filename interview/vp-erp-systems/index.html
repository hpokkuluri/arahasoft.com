1What does success look like for a VP of ERP Systems in the first year?RoleBasicTop question

Why they ask: to see your definition of success.

Answer:

"By the end of year one, I'd be a trusted executive partner to the CFO, COO, CHRO and CIO. There'd be a clear multi-year strategy and roadmap, approved and funded. Operations would be stable, with close, payments, payroll and shipping reliable. Business value would be visible, like fewer close days and higher automation rates. I'd have a strong leadership team and talent plan. Governance and controls would be clean, with no significant audit findings. And costs and the vendor strategy would be under control."

Tip: Lead with stable operations and value. Strategy is credible only on that base.

Likely follow-up

How will you know you've earned the CFO's trust by month 12?

When the CFO brings me into strategic decisions early — acquisitions, operating model changes, cost programs — not just to deliver what's already decided. Other signs: the CFO defends systems investment in executive meetings and asks my view on things beyond ERP.

2How do you spend your time as VP of ERP Systems?RoleBasic

Why they ask: priorities at the top.

Answer:

"Roughly 35% on strategy and business partnership: executive meetings, the roadmap and investment decisions. About 25% on the leadership team and talent: coaching, hiring leaders and culture. About 25% on program and operational oversight: major programs, risk and critical incidents. And about 15% external: vendors, industry and innovation. I adjust it to the organization's stage, transformation or steady state."

Tip: Give the percentages. It shows you've thought about it deliberately.

Likely follow-up

What would you cut first if you had less time?

Operational detail, which my leadership team should own.

3Present your vision for our enterprise systems in five minutes.StrategyIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: vision and communication.

Answer (structure):

"I'd start with their business context: strategy for growth, margins, M&A or global expansion. Then the problems today: fragmented systems, manual processes, a slow close and limited insight. Then the vision: a modern, AI-enabled, standardized platform with Oracle Fusion as the digital core, integrated data and analytics, automation at scale, secure and compliant. Five pillars: simplify, automate, insight, scale and protect. A roadmap in three horizons with value milestones. What it takes: investment, operating model, talent and governance. And how we'll measure success."

Tip: Use their own strategy words. It shows you listened.

Likely follow-up

How do you keep a five-minute vision concrete?

Three business outcomes — for example close in four days, working capital down 10%, and a scalable platform for acquisitions — then the platform moves to get there, and what changes in year one. Five minutes is enough if each outcome has a number and a date.

4How do you align enterprise systems investment with shareholder value?StrategyIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: financial framing.

Answer:

"I tie investments to financial drivers: revenue enablement, margin through cost to serve and SG&A, working capital, and risk reduction. Business cases include NPV and payback, validated by finance. The portfolio balances run, grow and transform. Benefits are tracked with executive accountability. And I communicate value in financial terms to the CFO and board."

Tip: Speak in NPV and working capital. It's the board's language.

Likely follow-up

Give an example of linking systems to shareholder value.

Faster close and better forecasting improve decision speed and investor credibility; inventory visibility frees working capital — for example $40 million released through better planning; lower cost per transaction improves margin. Linking systems to those metrics shows the investment in shareholder terms.

5What is your approach to AI in enterprise systems at the executive level?StrategyIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: AI leadership.

Answer:

"AI is a capability across finance, supply chain and HR, not isolated pilots. Platform choices: embedded AI in SaaS, like Oracle agents, plus an enterprise AI platform for cross-domain needs. A value portfolio with measurable targets for productivity, cycle time and quality. Responsible AI governance: risk tiers, human oversight, auditability, privacy and security. Investment in data foundations. A plan for workforce transition and reskilling. And board-level reporting on AI value and risk."

Tip: Mention board reporting. It shows you treat AI as an enterprise risk and opportunity.

Likely follow-up

What's the biggest AI mistake executives make?

Funding isolated pilots without data foundations or a plan to scale. Fifty pilots that never reach production cost money and teach little. I'd fund fewer use cases with clear owners, clean data and a path to production, and measure them like any other investment.

Close speed, controls and audit results, finance operations cost, quality of insight and forecasting, and readiness for M&A. We review it quarterly together, and I bring data on each, so the CFO sees systems as part of finance performance, not a separate IT topic.

7How do you present to the board about a major ERP transformation?ExecutiveIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: board communication.

Answer:

"Why: the strategic rationale and the risk of not acting. What: scope, approach, timeline and investment. Value: expected benefits and how they'll be measured. Risks: the top risks and mitigations, including financial reporting and operational continuity. Governance: the oversight structure. And for updates: milestones, budget, risks and benefits realized. Short, visual and in business language."

Tip: Mention the risk of not acting. Boards weigh both sides.

Likely follow-up

What does the board most need to hear?

Why the investment matters to the strategy, how risk is being managed, and what they'll see when — milestones, budget status and the top risks with mitigations. Boards don't need system detail; they need confidence that management understands the risks and has control.

9How would you design the enterprise systems organization for a $10B global company?Operating ModelIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: organization design at scale.

Answer:

"Capability-aligned product groups: Finance with R2R, P2P and O2C; Supply Chain with plan, source, make and deliver; People with HCM; and commercial integration. Platform groups for integration, data and analytics, AI, security and identity, and release and quality engineering. An architecture and governance office. Business relationship leaders for each function. Global delivery hubs and strategic partners. An Oracle CoE for standards. And funding by product capacity with portfolio governance."

Tip: Separate product groups from platform groups. It's the modern model.

Likely follow-up

How many layers would you have?

As few layers as possible — usually VP, a few directors over capability-aligned product groups like finance, supply chain, HR and data, then team leads. Strong leaders with clear ownership and end-to-end process owners matter more than the exact boxes.

14What are the critical success factors for an enterprise ERP transformation?TransformationBasicTop question

Why they ask: experience distilled.

Answer:

"Executive sponsorship and business ownership. Clear scope and principles, like standardize and adopt, not adapt. Strong program leadership and governance. Data readiness. Which success factor is most often missing?

Real business ownership. Many programs have an executive sponsor in name only, so decisions stall and the business doesn't adopt the new processes. Success needs business leaders who own design decisions, release their best people and are accountable for benefits.

15A board member asks why ERP programs so often fail. How do you answer?TransformationIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: board-level credibility.

Answer:

"I'd say they usually fail because they're treated as IT projects, ownership is unclear, they're over-customized, data is poor, change is underestimated, timelines are unrealistic, and governance is weak. Then explain our approach: business-led with CFO and COO sponsorship, a global template with a strict design authority, a data program from day one, phased rollouts, independent quality assurance, and benefit tracking. And I'd share my track record on similar programs."

Tip: Pair each failure cause with your countermeasure. It reassures the board.

Likely follow-up

What would you say to reassure the board?

Explain how our program addresses those failure causes directly — business ownership, limited customization, strong testing and data readiness, phased deployment and independent quality reviews — and show the current status against each. Then commit to telling the board early if any risk turns red.

28How would you handle a CEO demand to cut the ERP program budget by 30% mid-stream?ExecutiveIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: executive pressure.

Answer:

What would you protect from cuts?

Critical scope and controls — the processes that run the business and the security, SoD and testing that keep it safe. I'd show the CEO options: what a 30% cut removes, what risk it adds and what it delays, and propose a phased approach that keeps the core value.

32How do you decide between big-bang and phased ERP deployment?TransformationIn-depthTop question

Why they ask: deployment strategy.

Answer:

"Big-bang means fewer interim integrations and one faster cutover, but higher risk; it suits smaller, tightly integrated organizations. Phased, by module, region or business, means lower risk per wave and a learning curve, but coexistence costs and a longer timeline. I decide on complexity, interdependencies, risk appetite, resource capacity and the business calendar. Most large enterprises choose a pilot plus waves."

Tip: Say what most large enterprises do. It grounds the answer.

Likely follow-up

When is big-bang the right choice?

When the business is simple enough — one or two countries, standard processes — and interim integrations between old and new systems would be expensive and risky. Phased is safer for complex global businesses but costs more in temporary integrations and longer overall timelines.

40How do you think about capitalizing vs expensing ERP costs?FinanceIn-depth

Why they ask: financial acumen.

Answer:

"For SaaS, implementation costs fall under ASC 350-40 for cloud computing arrangements. Certain implementation costs can be capitalized and amortized over the term, while training, often data conversion, and post-implementation costs are expensed. I coordinate with accounting policy and the auditors, and track costs by category from day one. It affects how the budget is presented, opex versus capex, and the business case. And I'd always confirm the treatment with the accounting team."

Tip: Mention tracking by category from day one. Retroactive classification is painful.

Likely follow-up

Can SaaS implementation costs be capitalized?

Some can under ASC 350-40 for cloud arrangements, amortized over the contract term.
